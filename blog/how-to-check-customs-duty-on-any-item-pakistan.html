How to Check Customs Duty on Any Item in Pakistan

By Muhammad Shamshad AslamUpdated November 12, 20269 min read
Quick answer

To check customs duty on an item in Pakistan, find its eight-digit PCT code using FBR's tariff search, read the customs duty rate for that line in the Pakistan Customs Tariff 2026-27, then add any additional and regulatory duty, 18% sales tax and section 148 income tax. Check CPFTA rates and valuation rulings, and treat the result as an estimate.

How do you check the customs duty on an item in Pakistan?

You check it in two stages: first find the item's tariff code, then read and stack the rates attached to that code. The code is the hard part. Once you have it, the rest is arithmetic. This guide walks through each step with the official FBR pages we opened in November 2026. It assumes you know the basic idea of an HS code; if not, read how to find the HS code for your product first. For the full import process, see how to import from China to Pakistan.

Step 1: How should you describe the item?

Describe it the way the tariff does: by what it is, what it is made of and what it does, not by its brand or marketing name. "Wireless earbuds" is less useful than "headphones and earphones, combined with a microphone". Note the material for clothing, bags and furniture, the power source for appliances, and whether a part is a complete machine or a component. Write two or three versions of the description, because the tariff search matches words, and the right line may use a word you did not think of.

Step 2: How do you find the PCT code on FBR's site?

Use FBR's "Search Customs Tariff" page. When we opened it in November 2026, it offered two tools: "Find PCT Code or HS code by Description of Goods" and "Find Description of Goods by PCT Code or HS code". The page explains that the code has eight digits, and the first two represent the chapter.

  1. Search by description with your plainest word, such as "earphones" or "kettle".
  2. Read every line returned, not only the first one. Similar items often sit in neighbouring lines.
  3. If the seller gave you a Chinese HS code, use the reverse tool to read the description for its first six digits. Those six digits are international, so they usually point to the right heading.
  4. Pick the line whose wording fits your item most specifically. A specific description beats a general "other" line.

Step 3: Where do you read the customs duty rate?

Read it in the Pakistan Customs Tariff for the current year. FBR's customs tariff page listed "Pakistan Customs Tariff (F.Y 2026-27)" as a PDF when we checked, alongside older years, so make sure you open the right one. Find your eight-digit line and note the customs duty percentage. Since the 2025-26 budget the slabs have been 0, 5, 10, 15 and 20%, and the 2026-27 budget moved a number of industrial input lines into lower slabs, so an old rate you remember may no longer apply.

Step 4: Does a concession lower the rate?

Sometimes. Two places can override the standard rate:

  • The Fifth Schedule to the Customs Act. FBR publishes an updated 2026-27 version next to the tariff. It sets concessionary rates for specified goods, many of them machinery and industrial inputs, usually with conditions about who imports them and for what.
  • The China-Pakistan Free Trade Agreement. Listed lines carry a reduced customs duty if the goods come with a valid CPFTA certificate of origin. Sales tax and income tax still apply. See our CPFTA guide.

Most personal parcels do not meet the conditions for either, so plan on the standard rate unless you are sure.

Step 5: How do you add ACD and regulatory duty?

Add them as percentages of the same customs value as the customs duty. Profit reported on 1 July 2026 that SRO 1063(I)/2026 lowered additional customs duty and SRO 1064(I)/2026 capped regulatory duty at 20%, both from 1 July 2026. In practice:

  • ACD is 0% for goods in the 0 to 15% customs duty slabs, 2% on the 20% slab and 4% on 25% and higher.
  • Regulatory duty applies only if your PCT line is in the SRO 1064(I)/2026 list. Many everyday imports are not, but some consumer goods are.

Step 6: How do you add sales tax and income tax?

Add them in order, each on a bigger base. Sales tax is 18% of the customs value plus customs duty, ACD and regulatory duty. Then section 148 income tax is charged on the value plus all duties plus sales tax. PkRevenue reported FBR's tax year 2027 rates as 1% for Part I goods, 2% for Part II and 5.5% for Part III, the category that covers most finished goods. It doubles for people not on the Active Taxpayers List. If the goods have a reduced sales tax rate, the tariff alone will not show it, so ask a clearing agent for anything unusual.

Step 7: What value will customs actually tax?

Customs taxes the CIF value: item price plus insurance and freight to Pakistan, converted to rupees at the National Bank of Pakistan rates that FBR publishes. Where a valuation ruling covers your goods, customs can use the ruling's value instead of your invoice. Read how valuation rulings work before relying on a low invoice price.

Can you use the WeBOC duty calculator?

Yes, as a cross-check. FBR's customs pages link to an Item Duty Calculator on weboc.gov.pk. When we opened it in November 2026, it asked for the HS code, tariff description, unit of measure, quantity and unit value in US dollars, and converted the total to rupees at an exchange rate shown on the page. It is only as good as the code you enter.

That is about 52% of the customs value. If the same item sat in the 10% slab instead, ACD would drop to 0% and the total would fall to about 37%, which shows how much the choice of tariff line matters. Write down which line you used and why, so you can compare it with what customs assesses.

Why can the real duty still differ from your check?

Because customs makes the final decision on three inputs you can only estimate. These are the usual reasons the figure changes:

  • Classification. The assessing officer can place the item in a different PCT line, and the duty is then recalculated.
  • Value. A valuation ruling or a doubt about the invoice can raise the taxable value.
  • Exchange rate. Customs uses the rate on the day it assesses, not the day you paid.
  • The Rs 1,000 line. Post and courier parcels worth Rs 1,000 or less pay nothing under section 19C. Just above it, the whole value is taxed.

Frequently asked questions

Where can I check customs duty rates in Pakistan?

In the Pakistan Customs Tariff for the current year on FBR's customs tariff page. Use FBR's tariff search to find the PCT code first.

How do I find the PCT code for my item?

Use FBR's 'Find PCT Code or HS code by Description of Goods' tool, read all the lines it returns, and pick the most specific description that fits your item.

Is there an official duty calculator in Pakistan?

Yes. FBR links to an Item Duty Calculator on weboc.gov.pk that takes the HS code, quantity and unit value in US dollars. Treat it as a cross-check.

What taxes are added on top of customs duty?

Additional customs duty on some slabs, regulatory duty on listed goods, 18% sales tax and section 148 income tax of 1%, 2% or 5.5% for filers.

Why did customs charge more than I calculated?

Usually because customs classified the item differently, used a valuation ruling or a different exchange rate, or the parcel was just above the Rs 1,000 line.

Does the seller's HS code apply in Pakistan?

Only the first six digits are international. Pakistan uses eight-digit PCT codes, so use the seller's code as a starting point and confirm the full line.
