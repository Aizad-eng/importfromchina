How to Import Small Quantities From China to Pakistan

By Muhammad Shamshad AslamUpdated November 12, 20267 min read
Quick answer

To import a small quantity from China to Pakistan for your own use, buy a few pieces from a seller that ships here, or have them bought and shipped together in one parcel. You need only your CNIC, and duty applies above Rs 1,000. Many identical units can look commercial to customs, so larger quantities belong with a cargo company or the commercial route.

New to importing? Read our step-by-step guide on how to import from China to Pakistan.

How do you import a small quantity from China for your own use?

You buy the pieces you need and get them into Pakistan as one personal parcel, cleared on your CNIC. A small quantity for yourself might be two spare filters for a purifier, a pair of the same lamp for a bedroom, or a set of different tools for your own workshop. None of that needs an NTN or import registration.

The steps are simple:

  1. Find each item and copy its exact product link, with the variant selected.
  2. Check that each item is allowed and does not need a permit.
  3. Decide whether the seller ships to Pakistan or whether someone in China must receive the items.
  4. Combine everything into one shipment where you can.
  5. Pay the duty customs assesses on the whole parcel, or use a route where it is included.

For the general import process, see our guide on how to import from China to Pakistan. This guide covers what changes when there is more than one item.

What counts as a small quantity for Pakistan customs?

There is no fixed number. FBR's travel guide defines a commercial quantity as goods imported prima facie for trading or gain and not for personal use or gift. Customs officers judge each parcel by how many identical units there are, what the goods are and what the invoice says.

In practice the pattern matters more than the count:

Usually reads as personalMay read as commercial
Two of the same item for one homeDozens of the same item

Our guide to personal use versus commercial import goes deeper on where the line falls. If a parcel is judged commercial, it needs commercial clearance, and in baggage FBR says commercial quantities are released only on a fine of 30% of value on top of duty and taxes.

Can you buy just a few pieces from Chinese sellers?

Usually yes. Consumer sites such as Taobao, AliExpress, Temu and JD.com sell single units by design. Many 1688 listings start from one or two pieces even though the site is built for trade buyers, and the listing shows its starting quantity. On Alibaba.com, some suppliers mark items Ready to Ship with a minimum order of one, and many sell a single unit as a sample.

The catch is delivery, not the seller. Taobao, 1688, Xianyu and JD.com sellers ship only within China, so a few pieces from several of those sellers need someone in China to collect them in one place before they travel.

Is it cheaper to ship several items together?

Usually yes. Each shipment carries its own handling and minimum charges, so one box with four items costs less than four separate parcels. You pay on the chargeable weight of the combined box, which is the higher of actual weight and volumetric weight. Air cargo works out volumetric weight as length times width times height in centimetres divided by 6,000, and express couriers divide by 5,000.

Two things reduce the saving. Bulky items packed loosely add volume, so ask sellers to ship without oversized outer boxes where it is safe. And once the parcel is worth more than Rs 1,000, the whole value is dutiable, so combining small items does not avoid duty. The Rs 1,000 waiver under section 19C is too small to plan around.

How is duty worked out on a parcel with several items?

Each item is classified under its own tariff code, so one parcel can carry different duty rates. A phone case, a kitchen scale and a cable can all sit at different customs duty slabs. When we checked in November 2026, those slabs were 0, 5, 10, 15 and 20%, with some goods higher, and sales tax was 18% on the value plus duties. Regulatory duty applies only to listed goods and was capped at 20% from 1 July 2026.

Ask each seller for an invoice that lists the item, quantity and price. A clear list helps customs assess each line correctly instead of applying the highest rate to a vague description. Our customs duty guide shows how the total builds up.

What should you check before ordering several items?

  • Every variant. With several items, one wrong colour or size is easy to miss. Check each link has the right option selected.
  • Voltage and plugs. Pakistan runs at 230V and 50Hz with C and D plugs. China-market electrical items match the voltage, but US-market versions may be 110V.
  • Batteries. Loose lithium batteries and power banks are restricted on flights. One battery item can hold up a whole parcel.
  • Restricted goods. Phones need PTA registration and medicines need DRAP approval. Do not mix them into an ordinary parcel.
  • Branded goods. A counterfeit in the box can lead customs to hold the whole parcel.

What if you need more than a few pieces?

Then a personal route is the wrong tool. If you need many units, use a cargo company that ships by weight and handles commercial clearance, or import in your own name on the commercial route. That needs an NTN, a Pakistan Single Window subscription with NADRA biometric verification and a one-time Rs 500 fee, and usually a licensed customs agent. We only handle single items, or a few different single items together, so we are not the right choice for quantity orders.

How do you keep several items from different sellers organised?

Keep one simple list, because several items from several sellers is where mistakes happen. For each item, note the product link, the variant you chose, the price, the seller's name and the date it was ordered. Update it when each seller dispatches and gives a tracking number.

  • One link per item. Copy the link with the variant already selected, so nobody has to guess which option you meant.
  • Screenshots of the listing. Sellers edit listings. A screenshot of the price, variant and specifications on the day you ordered settles any later question.
  • Dispatch dates. A combined parcel waits for the slowest seller. If one item is made to order, it can hold up the rest.
  • Invoices. Keep each seller's invoice or order page, since customs may ask for the value of each item.

If one item is out of stock or delayed by weeks, decide early whether to drop it or wait. Holding a whole parcel for one slow seller is a common reason small orders arrive late.

What mistakes do people make with small quantity imports?

  • Ordering from several Taobao sellers and expecting each to ship to Pakistan.
  • Splitting an order into many tiny parcels, which multiplies shipping charges.
  • Assuming identical units are fine because the order feels small.
  • Using a vague invoice description, which can push the duty up.
  • Mixing a power bank or a restricted item into the box.

Frequently asked questions

How many items can I import from China for personal use?

There is no fixed number. Customs judges whether goods look like they are for trading, mostly by how many identical units there are and what the invoice says.

Do I need an NTN to import a few items?

No. A few items for your own use clear on your CNIC through a courier, Pakistan Post or a service. An NTN is for commercial imports in your own name.

Is it cheaper to order several items in one parcel?

Usually yes, because each shipment has its own handling and minimum charges. You pay on the combined chargeable weight of one box.

Can I split an order to stay under Rs 1,000?

The waiver is for parcels worth Rs 1,000 or less, which covers very little. Splitting an order adds shipping costs, so it rarely helps.

Do different items in one parcel pay different duty?

Yes. Each item has its own tariff code and duty rate, so ask sellers for an invoice that lists every item separately.

Where do I go for larger quantities?

Use a cargo company that ships by weight and handles commercial clearance, or import in your own name with an NTN and a Pakistan Single Window account.
