Gadgets From China That Work Differently in Pakistan

By importfromchina.shop teamUpdated October 11, 202610 min read
Quick answer

A gadget made for the Chinese market can behave differently in Pakistan. China-version phones ship without Google Play services. Smart home devices may only pair with an account set to the mainland China server. Plugs differ, US-market items may be 110V only, and radio devices must use frequencies allowed in Pakistan. SIM devices need PTA registration. Before you order, confirm the version, region, plug, voltage and app support in writing with the seller.

Why would the same gadget work differently in Pakistan?

Many Chinese brands build different versions of one product for different markets. The hardware often looks identical, but the software, the apps it talks to, the plug, and sometimes the radio bands are set for the market it was sold in. A product made for shoppers in mainland China is built around Chinese apps, Chinese cloud servers and Chinese regulations.

The differences fall into five groups: system software, apps and cloud servers, model names and hardware variants, power and plugs, and radio and registration rules. Some are cosmetic, like a Chinese manual. Others decide whether the device is usable at all, like a phone without Google services or a smart camera that only connects to a Chinese server.

When you buy on Taobao, 1688 or from a Chinese domestic seller, you usually get the China version. AliExpress and Alibaba sellers often offer both, with the global version marked as such and priced a little higher. Knowing which one you are buying is the single most useful check for electronics. For phones specifically, see our phone and PTA guide.

What is the difference between a China ROM and a global ROM?

On Android phones and tablets, the "ROM" is the installed system software. A China ROM is built for mainland China, where Google services are blocked. A global ROM is built for markets outside China.

Many popular apps are distributed through the Play Store, and some rely on Google Play services for sign-in, maps or notifications, so a phone without them can be awkward to use day to day. Installing Google services on a China ROM phone is possible on some models through unofficial methods, but it can break after updates and some apps may still refuse to run. Flashing a different ROM usually requires unlocking the bootloader, which can affect the warranty and cause features like Google Pay to stop working.

How can I tell which version a phone or tablet is?

Before buying, read the listing title and specs, and look at the screenshots of the phone's software if the seller shows them. Chinese characters on the home screen or an app store icon you do not recognise are clues. Sellers commonly write "Global Version", "Global ROM", "International Version" or "CN Version". If it is not stated, ask the seller in writing and keep the answer.

After delivery, check the software version in the phone's About section. Xiaomi's HyperOS is a good example: the version code is the release number followed by seven letters. The fourth and fifth letters show the region and the last two show the carrier, with XM meaning not locked to any carrier. Xiaomi builds separate versions for China, Europe (EEA), India and a global market, and the region letters tell you which one is installed. Community firmware lists commonly use CN for China and MI for global, though Xiaomi's own pages do not publish a full list. If the Play Store is missing on first boot, you almost certainly have a China version.

Note that "not China" does not always mean "global". Xiaomi's European (EEA) version, for example, differs from its global version to meet EEA rules on things like advertising. For use in Pakistan, either the global or the EEA build is usually fine. The one to avoid, if you depend on Google apps, is the China build.

Which smart home gadgets are region-locked?

Smart home devices often depend on the brand's cloud server, and brands run separate servers for mainland China and other regions. Users widely report that many devices sold for the Chinese market can only be added in the brand's app with the account region set to mainland China. That works, but users outside China report slow response, setup failures and that voice assistants like Alexa or Google Assistant cannot control those devices.

The safest rule is simple. For anything that needs an app to work, such as robot vacuums, smart cameras, smart plugs, air purifiers or fitness bands, buy the global version or confirm with the seller that it works with the app in your region. Our wearables guide covers smartwatches and earbuds, where app language and region settings are the usual problem.

It also helps to think about the long term. A device that depends on a brand's cloud keeps working only as long as the brand supports that server and that app for your region. If a China-market device works today only through a workaround, a future app update can break it, and there is no local support to call. Devices that work offline, or over standard connections like Bluetooth audio or USB, carry much less of this risk. A pair of in-ear monitors or a USB DAC behaves the same in Lahore as in Shenzhen. A smart camera tied to a Chinese cloud may not.

Will the plug and voltage work in Pakistan?

Pakistan uses 230V at 50Hz, with type D as the standard socket and type C plugs widely used. China uses 220V at 50Hz, mainly with type I plugs. Voltage is therefore compatible for China-market items, and most phone and laptop chargers are rated 100-240V anyway. You will usually need a plug adapter or a local cable for the type I plug.

The real risk is a US-market item rated only for 110V or 120V, which sometimes appears on cross-border listings. Check the label or listing for the input voltage before you order. Our guide to kitchen and home appliances and voltage covers high-wattage items.

Do model names differ between China and global versions?

Often, and this is where a lot of confusion starts. Brands frequently sell the same or very similar hardware under different names in different markets. Wikipedia's entry on Xiaomi HyperOS, for example, describes Poco devices as global versions of devices sold as Redmi in China. So a Redmi phone on Taobao and a Poco phone on a global store may be close relatives, with different software, and possibly different radio bands and chargers.

This works in both directions. A China listing may use a name you have never seen, for a product sold in Pakistan under another name. And a listing using a familiar global name may actually ship the China version. When comparing, search by the exact model code printed on the box or in the specifications, not just by the marketing name. If you are checking whether a product is already sold in Pakistan, search for both names.

Versions can also differ in hardware that matters day to day:

  • Charger and plug. China versions typically come with a charger for China's type I sockets, so plan for an adapter or a local charger.
  • Manuals and labels. China versions may have Chinese-only manuals and box labels.
  • Memory variants. Some storage and RAM combinations are sold only in China. PTA's FAQ says a change in memory variant counts as a different hardware variant for type approval purposes.

What about routers, walkie-talkies and other radio devices?

Anything that transmits radio signals has to use frequencies allowed in Pakistan. PTA's type approval FAQ says devices must operate within the frequency ranges permitted by PTA and the Frequency Allocation Board on a non-interference basis, and that use of restricted bands, such as certain HF and UHF bands, is prohibited without a licence or authorisation. Two-way radios (VHF, UHF, HF) are on PTA's list of equipment needing type approval, and cellular boosters and repeaters need extra clearance.

For routers and access points, the position is less clear-cut. PTA's FAQ lists switches, firewalls, servers and storage devices as exempt from type approval, but does not name Wi-Fi routers either way, and its rules have been updated over time. Type approval applies to commercial imports, but frequency rules apply to everyone using the device. PTA's FAQ also says type approved equipment must carry a verifiable mark, either a label or an electronic marking in the software, showing the PTA type approval number and the words "Approved by PTA". A locally sold router or radio with that mark is the low-risk option. An imported one without it is your responsibility to check. If you are importing a router, mesh system or radio, check the current PTA position for that type of device before ordering, and avoid models built for bands not used in Pakistan.

Do phones and SIM devices need PTA registration?

Yes. PTA uses the GSMA Type Allocation Code, the first part of the IMEI that identifies the model, in its DIRBS database. Any device with an IMEI, including phones, SIM tablets and SIM smartwatches, must be registered through PTA's DIRBS system, or it will be blocked on Pakistani networks after the grace period. PTA's DIRBS site says registration can be done within 60 days through its online service. You can check a device's status by sending its 15-digit IMEI (dial *#06# to see it) by SMS to 8484. The tax payable depends on the model and changes, so check it before you buy.

What should I check before ordering, step by step?

  1. Version. Global, China or another regional version. Ask in writing.
  2. Software. For phones and tablets, confirm Google Play is installed and English is available.
  3. App and region. For app-controlled devices, confirm which app is used and whether it works with a non-China account.
  4. Power. Input voltage range and plug type.
  5. Radio. For anything with Wi-Fi, cellular or radio, check it uses bands allowed in Pakistan.
  6. Registration. If it has an IMEI, plan for PTA registration and tax.
  7. Warranty. Assume no brand warranty in Pakistan and decide if that is acceptable.

Keep the seller's written answers. If the item arrives as the wrong version, the chat is your evidence in a dispute with the seller or the platform. Our guide to refunds and returns explains how those disputes work.

What are the common mistakes?

  • Choosing the cheapest listing without reading the version. The cheapest listing is often the China version.
  • Assuming English settings mean global software. A China ROM can be set to English and still lack Google services.
  • Buying a smart device for its app features. Some features exist only in the Chinese app or on the Chinese server.
  • Ignoring the input voltage. A 110V device on 230V can be destroyed in seconds.
  • Importing a radio device without checking the band. It may be illegal to use, even if it arrives.

Frequently asked questions

Does a China-version phone have Google Play?

Usually not. China ROM phones ship without Google Play services because Google is blocked in China. Buy the global version if you rely on Google apps.

Can I install Google Play on a China ROM phone?

On some models it is possible through unofficial methods, but it can break after updates and some apps may still not work. It is safer to buy the global version.

How do I know if my Xiaomi phone is a China version?

Check the software version in About phone. In HyperOS the fourth and fifth letters of the version code show the region. A missing Play Store is also a strong sign.

Will a Chinese smart home device work in Pakistan?

It may, but many China-market devices only pair with an app account set to the mainland China server, which can be slow. Buy the global version where possible.

Are walkie-talkies from China legal in Pakistan?

Two-way radios are on PTA's list of equipment needing type approval, and devices must use frequencies allowed in Pakistan. Check the band and the current PTA position before ordering.

Do Chinese appliances work on Pakistani electricity?

China uses 220V 50Hz and Pakistan 230V 50Hz, so voltage usually matches. You may need a plug adapter. Avoid US-market items rated only for 110V.
