How much does it cost to ship one item from China to Pakistan?

For one item, shipping usually costs Rs 5,000 to 20,000, depending on its size and type. A small, light item with no battery sits near the bottom of that range. A large box, a heavy item or anything with a battery or liquid moves towards the top, and very bulky items can go beyond it.

That figure is for getting the parcel from China to your door. It does not include the item itself, Pakistan customs duty on items worth more than Rs 1,000, or any service fee. This guide explains what decides where your item falls in that range, so you can estimate it before you ask anyone for a price.

If you want a comparison of shipping methods for bigger loads, such as sea freight per cubic metre, see our guide to shipping from China to Pakistan by air and sea. This page is only about one item.

Why is shipping one item so expensive?

People are often surprised that shipping can cost as much as the item. The reason is that a lot of the cost is fixed per parcel, whatever is inside it.

  • Collection and handling in China. The parcel has to be picked up from the seller or received at a warehouse, labelled and packed for export.
  • Export paperwork. Every parcel needs a declaration with its contents and value.
  • The flight. This is the part priced by weight.
  • Customs clearance in Pakistan. Someone has to file it, deal with any questions and pay any duty.
  • Local delivery. A domestic courier carries it from the airport city to your address.

A 200 gram item still needs all of these steps. Couriers also price in weight steps, commonly half a kilogram at a time, so a very light parcel pays for the first step in full. That is why small items cost much more per kilogram to ship than large ones.

What is volumetric weight, and how do you calculate it?

Aircraft run out of space before they run out of weight. So carriers charge a light but bulky box as if it were heavier. The number they use is called volumetric weight, or dimensional weight.

The formula for air freight is:

Length (cm) x Width (cm) x Height (cm) / 6,000 = volumetric weight in kg

Some express couriers use 5,000 instead of 6,000. DHL Express, for example, publishes a divisor of 5,000, which it also describes as 1 cubic metre counting as 200 kg. A smaller divisor gives a higher volumetric weight, so the same box costs more with that carrier.

You are charged on whichever is higher: the actual weight on the scale or the volumetric weight. That higher figure is called the chargeable weight.

A worked example. Take a boxed desk lamp. The shipping box is 50 cm long, 20 cm wide and 20 cm high, and it weighs 1.2 kg.

  • Volume: 50 x 20 x 20 = 20,000 cubic centimetres.
  • Volumetric weight at 6,000: 20,000 / 6,000 = 3.33 kg.
  • Volumetric weight at 5,000: 20,000 / 5,000 = 4 kg.
  • Actual weight: 1.2 kg.

The lamp is charged as 3.33 kg by air freight, or 4 kg with a courier using 5,000. That is roughly three times its real weight. This is why a lamp can cost more to ship than a heavier but compact item such as a small toolkit.

What else changes the cost of shipping one item?

  • Batteries. Devices with a built-in battery usually fly, but carriers handle them more carefully and many charge more. Loose lithium batteries and power banks are restricted by air. Our guide on lithium battery rules for shipping explains the categories.
  • Liquids, aerosols and perfumes. These are restricted by air, and some cannot be flown at all.
  • Fragile items. Glass, screens and ceramics may need extra packing, which adds size and weight.
  • Shipping inside China. Taobao and 1688 sellers ship only within China, so the item first travels to a warehouse. Many listings include this, but some charge for it.
  • The route. Express courier is fastest and usually dearest for one parcel. Consolidated air freight shares space with other parcels and is usually cheaper per kilogram. Post is cheapest but slow, and AliExpress scaled back its budget shipping options for Pakistan in mid 2025. Our comparison of air freight and courier for one item covers this.
  • Your address. Big cities are simplest. Some couriers add a charge for remote areas.

Does the shipping cost include customs duty?

No. Shipping and customs are separate costs, even when one company handles both.

Under section 19C of the Customs Act, as amended by the Finance Act 2025, no duties and taxes are charged on goods imported by post or courier where the value does not exceed Rs 1,000. The Finance Bill had proposed Rs 500, and the final Act set Rs 1,000. It was Rs 5,000 before. Above that line, duty and taxes apply, and the rate depends on what the item is.

Shipping can also affect customs. The customs value is commonly worked out on the item plus the cost of getting it to Pakistan, not only the price on the listing. So a higher shipping bill can mean slightly more duty as well. For how rates and values are worked out, read our guide to customs duty from China to Pakistan.

How can you estimate the shipping before you ask for a price?

  1. Find the package size. Many Alibaba, 1688 and AliExpress listings show the package dimensions and gross weight in the specifications, often labelled package size or packing size. Use the package, not the product.
  2. Work out the volume. Multiply length, width and height in centimetres.
  3. Divide by 6,000 for air freight, or by 5,000 if a courier is quoting.
  4. Compare with the gross weight. The higher figure is your chargeable weight.
  5. Note any battery, liquid or magnet. These can change the route or the rate.
  6. Place it in the range. A compact item under about a kilogram chargeable sits near Rs 5,000. A few kilograms chargeable, or a battery item, moves towards Rs 20,000. Anything charged at more than that, like the chair above, needs a specific quote.

How can you keep the shipping cost down?

  • Choose the compact version. A folding or slim model of the same product can halve the volumetric weight.
  • Skip bulky bundles. Listings that add a large stand, case or display box add volume. Pick the basic package if you do not need the extras.
  • Avoid separate batteries. If a device can run on batteries you can buy in Pakistan, do not ship spare ones.
  • Do not rush to courier. For most single items, consolidated air freight arrives within days of a courier and costs less.
  • Get one all-in price. A price that includes shipping and customs removes the risk of a second bill at the door.

What mistakes push shipping costs up?

  • Using the product size, not the box size. A 30 cm lamp can come in a 50 cm box.
  • Forgetting volumetric weight. A light item in a big box is charged as a heavy one.
  • Assuming free shipping covers Pakistan. Free shipping on Taobao or 1688 means free within China.
  • Not mentioning a battery. A battery item declared as plain electronics can be held or sent back.
  • Treating shipping as the whole cost. Customs on items over Rs 1,000 comes on top.
  • Shipping a cheap item. Paying Rs 5,000 to ship a Rs 1,500 item rarely makes sense.
