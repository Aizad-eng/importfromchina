Can I Import a Drone Into Pakistan? Rules and NOC (2026)

By Muhammad Shamshad AslamUpdated October 23, 20269 min read
Quick answer

Only with permission, and for most hobby buyers the practical answer is no. Pakistan's drone rules require registration with the Civil Aviation Authority, and drone importers have long needed a Ministry of Defence clearance, which individuals rarely get. Punjab also kept renewing a ban on outdoor drone flying through 2026. We do not bring drones into Pakistan without the required permission.

Can I import a drone into Pakistan in 2026?

Only with permission, and for an ordinary buyer that permission is very hard to get. A drone is not like a phone or a camera, where you pay duty and receive it. Drone imports sit under aviation and security rules, and the approvals involved are designed for registered operators, not for someone who wants a camera drone for weddings or travel.

So the honest answer for most people reading this is: do not order a drone from China to Pakistan unless you already hold the clearance. A seller in Shenzhen will happily ship it. The problem starts when it reaches Pakistan.

Which rules govern drones in Pakistan?

The main framework is the Civil Unmanned Aircraft Rules, 2024. Geo News reported in June 2024 that the rules had been issued on the Ministry of Aviation's website and made registration of drones with the Civil Aviation Authority mandatory. Dawn reported the same month that the draft still needed federal cabinet approval before taking effect, so the exact start date is not clear from public reporting.

Several older laws still apply alongside them. A 2025 commentary by an SDPI researcher in The News on Sunday says the Civil Aviation Ordinance of 1960, the Air Navigation Order of 2011 and the newer rules exist in parallel, and that import and flying approvals are split between the aviation regulator, provincial governments and the Ministry of Defence.

The regulator itself has also changed shape. Under the 2023 restructuring, airport operations moved to a separate Pakistan Airports Authority, while the Pakistan Civil Aviation Authority stays as the aviation regulator. When we checked in October 2026, we could not find the drone rules published on an official PCAA page, so the details below come from the rules as reported by Dawn and Geo News. Treat them as a guide and confirm the current text before you act.

How are drones grouped by weight?

The 2024 rules sort drones into four categories by maximum take-off weight, and the category decides what you need. This is how Dawn and Geo News described them in June 2024.

Category IIOver 250 grams up to 10 kgYesYesImporter must register
Category IIIOver 10 kg up to 25 kgYesYesMinistry of Defence clearance

Above 100 kg, Dawn reported, a drone is treated as a manned aircraft. Nearly every consumer camera drone sold on AliExpress, Taobao or Alibaba falls in Category I or II. Category II covers most popular folding camera drones, which means a pilot licence on top of registration.

Who has to approve a drone import?

Two bodies matter: the Civil Aviation Authority for registration, and the Ministry of Defence for import clearance. Geo News reported that under the 2024 rules anyone importing or exporting drones must first register, and that importing Category III and IV drones needs Ministry of Defence clearance.

In practice the Ministry of Defence no objection certificate, or NOC, has been the gate for years. Dawn reported in April 2021 that only around 100 to 200 modern drones had come in through legal channels, after importers obtained NOCs from the Ministry of Defence, and that the Ministry of Science and Technology estimated over 90% of the drones flying in Pakistan had been smuggled. The 2025 SDPI commentary says Ministry of Defence approval often has no defined turnaround.

Some guides online mention a Ministry of Interior NOC for drone imports. The sources we found point to the Ministry of Defence for import clearance, with the Interior Ministry named only as a member of a proposed civil drone authority. That authority, approved on paper in 2021, was still described as inactive in the 2025 commentary.

Do small drones under 250 grams need permission?

Yes, registration still applies. As reported, the 2024 rules require every unmanned aircraft to be registered with the CAA before it is flown, including Category I. What the reports do not list for Category I is the Remote Pilot Licence, which starts at Category II.

The 250 gram line matters because many sellers market "under 250g" drones as licence-free worldwide. That marketing is written for Europe and the US. In Pakistan you still register, you still face the import question, and you still face local flying bans. A small drone does not slip through the rules just because it weighs 249 grams.

Registration also has limits. Geo News reported that a person may not register more than three unmanned aircraft of the same category in one calendar year, that new owners get 15 days to apply after acquiring a drone, and that registration is renewed every three years.

Can I fly a drone in Punjab or elsewhere right now?

In Punjab, not outdoors without written approval. Punjab's Home Department has been renewing a Section 144 order that bans flying drones in open areas across the province, usually for 30 days at a time. ProPakistani reported a renewal by notification dated 22 July 2026, and Arab News reported an earlier one in June 2026.

The July 2026 order allowed small drones indoors at events in halls and marquees, exempted drones used for agriculture, irrigation and research, and allowed other activities approved in writing by the Safe Drone Usage Committee. Intelligence agencies and law enforcement were exempt. Check whether the ban is still in force in your area when you read this, because it has been extended month by month.

Outside Punjab, flying rules come from the aviation rules and any local orders. Under the reported 2024 rules, standard flights in daylight, within visual line of sight and no higher than 200 feet needed no separate permission, while other flights needed approval. Flights into restricted airspace need prior written permission from the CAA.

What about drone parts, batteries and FPV gear?

Parts are a grey area, and batteries are a shipping problem in their own right. The 2025 SDPI commentary says imported flight controllers, optical sensors and energy systems face tariffs, customs delays and opaque security screening. A box of drone parts can therefore attract the same questions as a finished drone, especially if together they make up a flyable aircraft.

Spare drone batteries are lithium packs shipped on their own, which air rules treat as the hardest category. Our guide to lithium battery shipping rules explains why loose packs rarely fly. Remote controllers, goggles and gimbals that contain built-in batteries are easier to ship, but a controller without the drone is of little use if you cannot legally fly.

What happens if a drone arrives without permission?

Expect it to be held, and possibly confiscated. Drones have long been the hobby item that trips customs in Pakistan, which is why our list of what you cannot import from China to Pakistan puts them near the top. Profit reported a case where Pakistan Customs seized drone cameras, along with hundreds of phones, from passengers arriving at Islamabad airport.

If you fly an unregistered drone, the reported 2024 rules set fines of up to Rs 100,000, plus up to Rs 10,000 for each day a violation continues. Those penalties are separate from anything customs does at the border.

Common mistakes with drones

  • Trusting the "under 250g, no registration" label. That is a foreign rule. Pakistan's reported rules register every category.
  • Calling it a toy or a camera on the declaration. Misdeclaring a drone adds a customs offence to the original problem.
  • Ordering parts to build one instead. Flight controllers and frames still attract security screening, and the result still needs registration.
  • Assuming a seller's "ships to Pakistan" means it is legal. The seller's job ends when the parcel leaves China.
  • Forgetting the flying ban. Even a legally imported drone cannot be flown outdoors in Punjab while the Section 144 order is in force, without written approval.

What is the legal route if you really need a drone?

The legal route exists, but it suits registered businesses and institutions with a clear use, such as surveying or agriculture. Based on the reported rules, it looks like this:

  1. Confirm the drone's category from its maximum take-off weight on the manufacturer's spec sheet.
  2. Register as an importer and apply for registration of the drone with the CAA, stating its intended use.
  3. For Category III and IV, and in practice often for others, obtain Ministry of Defence clearance before the drone ships.
  4. For Category II and above, the person flying it needs a Remote Pilot Licence, which Geo News reported requires training from an approved organisation and passing a CAA exam, with a minimum age of 18.
  5. Check provincial flying bans and get written approval for any flight they cover.

For one person who just wants a camera drone, buying from a dealer in Pakistan who has already handled the import and registration paperwork is usually the realistic option. Ask the dealer for the registration papers before you pay.

Frequently asked questions

Is it legal to import a drone into Pakistan?

Only with permission. The 2024 drone rules require registration with the Civil Aviation Authority, and importers have long needed Ministry of Defence clearance. Without it, expect the drone to be held at customs.

Do I need an NOC to import a DJI or other camera drone?

In practice, yes. Drone imports have long required a Ministry of Defence no objection certificate, and the 2024 rules require Ministry of Defence clearance for Category III and IV drones and registration for all.

Does a drone under 250 grams need registration in Pakistan?

Yes. As reported, the 2024 rules require every drone to be registered with the CAA, including Category I up to 250 grams. The Remote Pilot Licence starts at Category II.

Can I fly a drone in Lahore or elsewhere in Punjab?

Not outdoors without written approval while Punjab's Section 144 order is in force. It was renewed in 30-day steps through 2026, with exemptions for indoor events, agriculture, irrigation and research.

What is the fine for flying an unregistered drone in Pakistan?

The reported 2024 rules set fines of up to Rs 100,000, plus up to Rs 10,000 for each day the violation continues.

Can I import drone parts or spare batteries instead?

Parts can face the same security screening as drones, and loose drone batteries are hard to ship by air under lithium battery rules. Neither is a reliable workaround.
