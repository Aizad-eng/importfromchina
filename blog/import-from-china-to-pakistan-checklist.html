Import From China to Pakistan: A Step-by-Step Checklist

By Muhammad Shamshad AslamUpdated November 12, 20268 min read
Quick answer

To import from China to Pakistan, first decide your route. For one personal item, check the product is allowed, confirm the seller ships or use a service, work out the landed cost and pay. For a commercial shipment, register an NTN, subscribe on PSW, find the HS code and permits, pay through your bank, and file the goods declaration.

New to importing? Read our step-by-step guide on how to import from China to Pakistan.

What should you check before you import anything from China?

Before anything else, check three things: which route you are on, whether the product is allowed, and roughly what it will cost when it reaches you. Everything else follows from those answers. This page is a checklist to work through, not a full explanation. For the reasoning behind each step, read our main guide on how to import from China to Pakistan.

Work through the route check first:

  • Is this one item, or a few different single items, for you or your family? That is the personal route.
  • Is it several identical units, stock for a shop, or goods invoiced to a business? FBR defines a commercial quantity as goods imported prima facie for trading or gain and not for personal use or gift. That is the commercial route.
  • Will you import again next month? If yes, the commercial setup may be worth doing once. If not, let a registered party carry the paperwork.

How do the two routes compare at a glance?

The personal route asks for almost no paperwork, while the commercial route asks for registrations and documents before the goods ship. The table shows what each step looks like on each route.

What is on the checklist for one personal item?

For one item, the checklist is mostly about the product and the price, because the courier or service handles customs. Tick these off in order:

  1. Copy the exact product link and note the variant, colour and size you want.
  2. Check the product is allowed. Drones, medicines, replica weapons and counterfeit branded goods are the usual problems. Phones need PTA registration, and loose batteries and power banks are restricted on flights. See what you cannot import from China.
  3. Check it works here. Read the voltage label, the plug type and whether it is a China-only version with a Chinese-language app or region lock.
  4. Check whether the seller ships to Pakistan. Taobao and 1688 sellers ship within China only, so those items need someone to buy and ship them for you.
  5. Work out the landed cost. Add shipping and Pakistani duty and taxes to the item price, then compare with the price in Pakistan. Our cost guide shows worked examples.
  6. Remember the Rs 1,000 line. Under section 19C of the Customs Act, post and courier parcels worth Rs 1,000 or less pay no duties or taxes. Above that, the whole value is dutiable.
  7. Choose how to pay. Many Chinese sites take only international cards. JazzCash, Easypaisa and Raast work only with a local agent or service.
  8. Enter your name and CNIC exactly as on your card. Since 15 August 2026, under SRO 918(I)/2026, a CNIC can stand in for an NTN in the import manifest for small parcel importers, so enter it carefully.
  9. Write a full address and phone number with a landmark, and keep the phone on.
  10. Save the order receipt. The courier may ask for it as proof of value, and you will need it for warranty or a refund claim.
  11. Track the parcel and expect gaps in scans while it flies and clears customs.
  12. Check the parcel when it arrives, before the rider leaves if you can, and record any damage for the seller.

What is on the checklist for a commercial shipment?

For a commercial shipment, the checklist starts weeks before the goods ship, because registrations and permits take time. In order:

  1. Get an NTN. FBR says an individual's 13-digit CNIC is used as the NTN once you e-enrol on IRIS. Note the email you use there.
  2. Check sales tax registration with a tax adviser. Most commercial importers hold an STRN.
  3. Subscribe on PSW. When we checked in November 2026: NTN, FTN or STRN, CNIC, a SIM in your own name, the email from IRIS, a one-time Rs 500 fee and biometric verification at a NADRA e-Sahulat centre. The user ID lasts two years.
  4. Link your bank profile to PSW by IBAN, because the declaration needs a financial instrument.
  5. Find the PCT code for each product and read its duty and conditions. See how to find the HS code for your product.
  6. Check the Import Policy Order and LPCOs. If DRAP, PSQCA, PTA or another agency must clear the goods, start that application early.
  7. Get a proforma invoice with price, price term, packing and lead time.
  8. Arrange the payment through your bank against the right financial instrument.
  9. Book freight and give the forwarder your NTN for the bill of lading or airway bill.
  10. Ask for a CPFTA certificate of origin if your goods are on the reduced-duty list.
  11. Collect the documents: commercial invoice, packing list, transport document and permits. Our documents guide lists who issues each one.
  12. File the Single Declaration on PSW, yourself or through a licensed customs agent, and pay against the PSID.
  13. Respond quickly if customs routes the declaration for document review or physical examination.
  14. Collect the goods after release and keep the full file for later queries.

Which checks do people skip most often?

The checks people skip are the ones that cost money later: the tariff code, the regulator's permission and the real landed cost. Three to watch:

  • Skipping the permit check. Goods needing an LPCO are not released on duty payment alone.
  • Guessing the duty. Customs decides the code and the value, and can use a valuation ruling instead of your invoice.
  • Ignoring box size. Air shipping is charged on the higher of actual and volumetric weight, so a light but bulky item costs more than its weight suggests.

How long should each stage take?

Allow time for the seller, the flight, customs and local delivery, and add more if registrations are new. Rough planning ranges:

  • Seller dispatch: a few days for stock items, longer for made-to-order goods.
  • Air courier or air freight: commonly one to two weeks door to door including customs.
  • Sea freight: several weeks, with port handling on top.
  • PSW subscription and permits: plan these before you pay the supplier, not after the goods land.

Chinese New Year, from late January to mid February, and the weeks after the 11.11 sale are slow periods. Leave a margin of a week or two if you need the item by a fixed date.

What should you have ready when the goods arrive?

Have your identity document, your order or invoice and your phone ready, and know what you expect to pay. For a personal parcel, a genuine courier will show you a customs assessment if more duty is due. Never pay a "customs fee" through a link in an SMS. For a commercial shipment, keep the PSID receipt and the release order, because the clearing agent and the port will ask for them. Keep the whole file, from proforma invoice to release, together afterwards. If customs, your bank or FBR asks a question months later, those papers are your answer.

Frequently asked questions

What is the first step to import from China to Pakistan?

Decide your route. One item for personal use goes through a courier or service on your CNIC. Goods for trade need an NTN, a PSW subscription and a goods declaration in your name.

Do I need to register anywhere to import one item from China?

No. For one personal item the courier or service files the declaration. You usually give only your CNIC and address.

How do I know if an item is allowed into Pakistan?

Check the Import Policy Order lists and the regulator for your product. Drones, medicines, phones, counterfeit goods and loose batteries are the common problem areas.

What do I need for a commercial import from China?

An NTN, usually an STRN, a PSW subscription, a bank profile linked to PSW, the right PCT code, any permits, the shipping documents and a filed Single Declaration.

Is there a duty-free limit on parcels from China?

Only Rs 1,000. Under section 19C, post and courier parcels worth Rs 1,000 or less pay no duties or taxes. Above that the whole value is dutiable.

How long does importing from China to Pakistan take?

By air, commonly one to two weeks door to door after the seller dispatches. Sea takes several weeks. New registrations and permits add time before shipping.
