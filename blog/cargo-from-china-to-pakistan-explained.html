Cargo From China to Pakistan Explained: Air, Sea and Courier

By Muhammad Shamshad AslamUpdated November 12, 20269 min read
Quick answer

Cargo from China to Pakistan moves in three ways: express courier, which is fastest and priced in half-kilo steps; air cargo, consolidated freight on an airway bill cleared at an airport cargo terminal; and sea cargo, shared or full containers into Karachi. Door-to-door cargo companies bundle these with customs clearance. The right choice depends on size, urgency and whose name clears customs.

What does "cargo" mean when people ship from China to Pakistan?

In everyday Pakistani use, "cargo" means any paid shipment of goods from China that is not a normal postal parcel, and it covers three different systems: express courier, air cargo and sea cargo. People also use "cargo company" for a door-to-door service that collects goods at a warehouse in China, ships them by air or sea, clears customs and delivers in Pakistan for a price per kilo or per cubic metre.

The word hides big differences. A courier parcel and a sea container are cleared differently, priced differently and carry different risks. Knowing which one a company actually uses tells you how long to expect and what can go wrong. If you only want the costs and times for each mode, our guide on air freight vs courier for one item compares them for a single parcel.

How do courier, air cargo and sea cargo compare?

Courier is the fastest and dearest per kilo, sea is the slowest and cheapest per kilo for large volumes, and air cargo sits between them. The table summarises how each works for goods from China.

The duty and taxes are set by the same tariff whichever mode you use. What changes is the freight cost, the speed and who files the paperwork.

How is air cargo charged?

Air cargo is charged on chargeable weight, which is the higher of the real weight and the volumetric weight, with volume worked out as length x width x height in centimetres divided by 6,000. Couriers use 5,000, so the same box is charged a little lighter as air cargo than by courier.

Air cargo has a fixed cost for every shipment as well: the air waybill, terminal handling at both ends, and clearance in Pakistan. That is why a forwarder groups many customers' goods under one master air waybill. Each customer has a house air waybill under it, and the forwarder shares the fixed costs out. For one small box, those shared costs are still a large part of the bill.

How is sea cargo charged?

Sea cargo in a shared container (LCL) is charged per revenue ton, where one revenue ton is 1 cubic metre or 1,000 kg, whichever is higher. A full container (FCL) is charged per container, whatever is inside, up to its weight limit. Forwarders commonly round small LCL shipments up to a minimum, such as one revenue ton, so a tiny shipment pays as if it were one cubic metre.

Here is a worked example of the revenue ton rule. A shipment of 0.6 cubic metres weighing 900 kg is 0.6 by volume and 0.9 by weight, so it is charged as 0.9 revenue tons before any minimum. Sea cargo also has port and handling charges at both ends that do not depend much on size. Our guide on sea freight from China to Pakistan, LCL vs FCL goes through these in detail.

What is door-to-door cargo, and what does the price include?

Door-to-door cargo is a service where one company collects goods at its warehouse in China, ships them, clears them and delivers them in Pakistan for a quoted rate per kilo or per cubic metre. The appeal is simplicity: one company, one rate, no customs paperwork for you.

The details vary a great deal between companies, so ask what the rate covers:

  • Customs duty and taxes. Some rates include them, some do not, and some include them only for certain product types.
  • Shipping inside China. Getting the goods from the seller to the cargo company's warehouse is often extra.
  • Delivery inside Pakistan. A rate may end at a Karachi or Lahore office, with delivery to your city charged separately.
  • Volumetric weight. Ask which divisor they use and whether they round up.
  • Restricted items. Batteries, liquids, magnets and branded goods are often refused or charged more.

In whose name does your cargo clear customs?

This is the most important question to ask, because the name on the import documents is the person or company responsible for what is declared. A courier parcel for personal use clears in your name against your CNIC. A grouped cargo shipment usually clears in the forwarder's name or a client's, with your goods as part of it.

Customs takes misdeclaration seriously and acts against both importers and clearing agents. In July 2026, for example, Pakistan Customs in Karachi registered a case against an importer and its clearing agent after consignments declared as scrap were found to contain hundreds of new air conditioners, mostly from China and Thailand, and seized the goods. If a cargo rate looks far too low, ask how the goods are being declared, because an undervalued declaration can lead to goods being held or seized.

What documents travel with a cargo shipment?

Every cargo shipment needs a commercial invoice, a packing list and a transport document, and the importer files a goods declaration with Pakistan Customs. The transport document is the courier waybill, the air waybill or the bill of lading, depending on the mode.

  • Commercial invoice. What the goods are, their value, quantity and origin.
  • Packing list. Cartons, weights and sizes.
  • Certificate of origin. Needed if the importer wants reduced duty under the China Pakistan Free Trade Agreement for eligible goods.
  • Goods declaration. Filed on Pakistan Customs' WeBOC system or through Pakistan Single Window, which Customs says now offers the WeBOC functions to traders.

An importer clearing goods in their own name needs to be registered. Pakistan Single Window says subscribing requires an NTN, FTN or STRN, a CNIC with biometric verification at NADRA, a SIM in your own name and a one-time fee of Rs 500. What questions should you ask a cargo company before you pay?

Ask questions that reveal what the rate leaves out and who carries the risk. Get the answers in writing.

  1. Is customs duty included, and for which products?
  2. In whose name will the goods be cleared, and how will they be declared?
  3. Which volumetric divisor do you use, and do you round up?
  4. What happens if Customs holds or reassesses the goods? Who pays?
  5. Do you accept items with batteries, liquids or brand names?
  6. What compensation applies if a carton is lost or damaged?
  7. What is the expected delivery date, and where exactly does the rate end?

Which option suits one item, and which suits larger quantities?

For one item, courier or a single-item service is usually the sensible choice, because air and sea cargo carry fixed costs that only make sense when spread over many cartons. For larger quantities, a cargo company or the formal commercial import route is the right path, and you should use a licensed clearing agent.

Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest cargo from China to Pakistan?

Per kilo, sea cargo is cheapest for large volumes, but it has fixed port charges and a minimum, so for small shipments air cargo or courier can work out cheaper overall.

Is cargo the same as courier?

No. Courier is one type of cargo service where one company carries and clears a parcel end to end. Air cargo and sea cargo use forwarders, airlines or shipping lines and a separate clearing agent.

Do cargo companies include customs duty?

Some do and some do not, and some include it only for certain goods. Ask in writing whether duty is included for your exact product.

Can I ship one item by sea cargo?

Technically yes as part of a shared container, but minimum charges and port fees make it poor value and slow for one item. Courier or air is usually better.

Do I need an NTN to receive cargo from China?

A personal courier parcel clears against your CNIC. Clearing commercial cargo in your own name needs an NTN and registration with Pakistan Single Window.

Why are some cargo rates so much cheaper?

Low rates may exclude duty, local delivery or China-side shipping, or may rely on how the goods are declared. Ask what is included and how the goods will be declared.
