PTA Type Approval for Wireless Devices: Routers, Walkie-Talkies, IoT

By Muhammad Shamshad AslamUpdated November 12, 202610 min read
Quick answer

PTA type approval is the Pakistan Telecommunication Authority's sign-off that a model of radio or telecom equipment meets the standards it applies. Under PTA's December 2025 framework, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and other short range and IoT devices need type approval before they are imported, marketed or sold. Walkie-talkies on VHF, UHF or HF also face licence conditions.

What is PTA type approval?

Type approval is a certificate from the Pakistan Telecommunication Authority (PTA) confirming that a particular model of telecom or radio equipment meets the standards PTA applies and works within the conditions allowed in Pakistan. It is issued per model, not per brand, so a newer version of a device can need its own approval.

The process rests on PTA's Type Approval Technical Standards Regulations 2021. ProPakistani's report on those regulations noted three points that still shape how wireless gadgets are treated: wireless equipment works on a non-interference basis, VHF, HF and UHF devices carry licensing conditions, and duplicated, cloned, stolen or counterfeit devices are not valid for a certificate of compliance. This guide covers wireless devices other than phones. For phones, see our guide to importing a mobile phone and PTA registration.

What changed in 2025 and 2026?

Two sets of rules widened PTA's reach over wireless equipment, and a third change made enforcement tighter:

  • Short range devices and IoT, December 2025. PTA issued version 1.4 of its framework for short range devices (SRDs) and terrestrial IoT on 18 December 2025. According to a compliance firm's summary published in March 2026, all SRD and terrestrial IoT devices must obtain PTA type approval before being imported, marketed or sold in Pakistan, even when they run under licence-exempt conditions. Individual devices stay licence-exempt within the technical limits, while companies offering commercial IoT services need PTA authorisation.
  • Telecommunication Equipment Standards Regulations, August 2026. These were notified through SRO 1110(I)/2026, as Business Recorder reported on 6 August 2026, and took effect on publication in the gazette. They apply to licensees, manufacturers and importers of telecom equipment, adopt standards from bodies such as ITU, ETSI, IEC and CISPR along with EN, FCC and EU radio equipment directive standards, and require equipment to be assessed against them before it is deployed in Pakistan.
  • Checks without notice, September 2026. ProPakistani reported on 10 September 2026 that PTA officers can now visit the premises of equipment importers and others without prior notice to check compliance, and that records must be kept for at least three years.

Does a Wi-Fi router need PTA type approval?

For a commercial import, yes. A router broadcasts on Wi-Fi bands, Wi-Fi equipment is a short range device, and PTA's December 2025 framework says short range devices need type approval before they are imported, marketed or sold, even though using them does not need a licence. A router with a SIM slot adds a second layer, because a device with an IMEI is also handled through PTA's device registration system.

For one router bought for your own home, the rules we read do not set a separate personal allowance, as they do for phones. In practice a single branded home router is rarely an issue at clearance, while several identical units look like stock and are treated as a commercial import.

Do walkie-talkies need a licence in Pakistan?

Usually yes, beyond type approval. PTA's 2021 regulations include licensing conditions for VHF, HF and UHF devices, and require wireless equipment to work on a non-interference basis within allowed spectrum. Many inexpensive walkie-talkies sold online are built for frequency plans in other countries, so a set that is legal to use in one country can transmit on a band that is restricted here.

Before buying, read the exact frequency range in the listing and the transmit power, and compare them with what PTA allows. A radio that needs a licence you do not hold is a problem at customs and again when you switch it on.

Does type approval apply to one device for personal use?

The wording we could confirm is aimed at the market. The SRD framework speaks of devices being imported, marketed or sold, and the 2026 regulations name licensees, manufacturers and importers. The one personal allowance we found is for mobile devices: up to five per person a year under the 2021 regulations.

That does not make a personal device free of rules. Frequency limits apply to anyone who uses the equipment, and customs can still ask for proof of approval when it assesses a parcel. A single Bluetooth speaker or smartwatch rarely raises questions in practice. A walkie-talkie set on a restricted band, a SIM router or several of the same device is far more likely to be held. What is the difference between type approval and a certificate of compliance?

Type approval clears a model. A certificate of compliance (CoC) clears an individual device or consignment, and for phones and other IMEI devices it is issued through DIRBS, PTA's device registration system. The 2021 regulations say a duplicated, cloned, stolen or counterfeit device is not valid for a CoC, which is why a phone or SIM router with a bad IMEI cannot be fixed by paying tax. A commercial importer of IMEI devices needs both, in that order.

How does a commercial importer get type approval?

  1. Identify the model and its bands. Each model, and sometimes each hardware variant, needs its own approval.
  2. Collect laboratory reports. The 2026 regulations adopt international standards, so reports against the ITU, ETSI, IEC, CISPR, EN or FCC standards that fit the device are the starting point.
  3. Apply to PTA with the application form, undertaking, technical documents and fee. Foreign applicants supply extra documents under the 2021 regulations.
  4. Allow time. IB-Lenhardt, a certification consultancy, gives an average of about ten weeks for Pakistani type approval and notes that marking is required on approved products.
  5. Register IMEI devices in DIRBS before they ship.
  6. Keep records for at least three years, and expect PTA checks without notice.

What goes wrong with wireless gadgets bought abroad?

  • Wrong bands. Radios and some IoT devices sold for other markets use frequencies that are restricted in Pakistan.
  • Unregistered IMEI. A SIM router or tracker with an IMEI that is not registered can be blocked on Pakistani networks.
  • Quantity. Several identical devices look commercial, and commercial imports need approval.
  • Assuming a foreign certificate counts. FCC or CE marks show approval work for other markets, but PTA issues its own approval.
  • Forgetting the second regulator. Earphones, speakers and lithium batteries also appear on PSQCA's compulsory list for commercial imports.
