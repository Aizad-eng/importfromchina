Yiwu Market for Pakistani Buyers: Can You Buy One Piece?

By Muhammad Shamshad AslamUpdated October 23, 20269 min read
Quick answer

Usually not. Yiwu International Trade City in Zhejiang, China, is a wholesale market of about 80,000 booths, and most stalls sell by the carton or in lots of a few dozen pieces. A stall may sell one sample to a buyer standing in front of it, but not to someone in Pakistan. For one item, the same kind of product is usually easier to get from 1688, Taobao or Alibaba.

Can I buy a single item from Yiwu?

In most cases, no, not as a normal purchase. Yiwu International Trade City is a wholesale market. Its stalls exist to take orders from importers, shop owners and trading companies, and their prices assume you are buying cartons, not one piece.

There are exceptions. Sourcing guides that work in the market describe some stalls selling a single sample to a visitor, and some larger items, such as a light fitting, can have a minimum of one. But those exceptions happen face to face, in Chinese, with cash or a Chinese payment app. A stall owner in Yiwu has no reason to pack one cheap item for a stranger in Pakistan, and most will not.

So the honest answer for a Pakistani buyer who wants one item for personal or professional use is this: Yiwu is the wrong door. The same factories and traders very often sell online through 1688, Taobao or Alibaba.com, where a single piece is normal. This guide explains why, and what to do instead.

What is Yiwu International Trade City?

Yiwu International Trade City, also called China Commodity City or simply the Yiwu market, is a huge wholesale market for small commodities in Yiwu, a city in Zhejiang province in eastern China. Its first district opened on 22 October 2002. Wikipedia lists a floor area of about 5.5 million square metres across its districts and more than 75,000 stores.

More recent figures are bigger. When the market reopened on 28 February 2026 after the Spring Festival holiday, People's Daily reported about 80,000 booths, more than 2.1 million types of goods, and trade ties with over 230 countries and regions. On that first day it counted 291,000 visitors.

The market is operated by Zhejiang China Commodities City Group, which also runs Chinagoods, an online platform it launched in 2020 as the official website of the Yiwu market. The city keeps adding new market buildings. People's Daily described the Yiwu Global Digital Trade Center, opened in October 2025, as the city's newest market.

How do Yiwu stalls work, and what are the MOQs?

A Yiwu stall is a showroom, not a shop. Each booth displays samples of what its factory or trading company can supply. A buyer walks the rows, looks at samples, takes the stall's card, negotiates a price and a quantity, and places an order that is usually packed in cartons and handed to a freight agent.

MOQ means minimum order quantity, the smallest order a stall will accept. In Yiwu it is commonly set per carton or per style rather than per piece. Sourcing agents who work there describe minimums that run from a few dozen to a few hundred units per item for many goods, with small seasonal items going much higher. One toy market guide puts the usual stock minimum at several cartons per supplier, though designs can often be mixed within those cartons.

These figures come from agents with a commercial interest, and every stall sets its own rules, so treat them as a rough picture. The pattern is consistent though: the unit of trade in Yiwu is the carton, and the price on the sample assumes you are buying many.

Why won't a Yiwu stall send one piece to Pakistan?

Because it does not pay them, and because the market is not set up for it. Three things get in the way.

  • Price. A Yiwu price is a wholesale price on a thin margin. Selling one piece at that price earns the stall almost nothing for the time it takes to deal with you.
  • Payment. Deals are settled in yuan, typically face to face or through the buyer's own agent in China. JazzCash, Easypaisa and Raast are not part of that system.
  • Shipping. Stalls hand cartons to freight agents. They generally do not post single parcels abroad, and a courier charge for one small item would dwarf its price.

The same logic applies to factories in general. Our guide on whether you can buy one piece from a Chinese factory explains when a supplier will sell a single unit, such as a stock item or a sample, and when it will not.

What is yiwugo.com, and can you order from it in Pakistan?

Yiwugo.com is an online directory of Yiwu market shops, with an English version. Its own site title describes it as an online Yiwu wholesale market of 75,000 booths. Sourcing guides describe each online store as linked to a physical booth in the market, with chat and contact details for the stall.

It is useful for one thing: seeing what a Yiwu stall sells and how it prices. It is not a single-item shop. A buying guide published by a sourcing company says most products on it have a minimum of a few dozen pieces, and that its main users are retailers and small wholesalers. Ordering, payment and shipping overseas go through extra services and fees.

Chinagoods, the market operator's own platform, works the same way. Both are wholesale tools. If you find a product you like there, the practical next step for one piece is to search for the same item on 1688 or Taobao, where the same goods are often listed by single-piece sellers. Our guide on buying from 1688 in Pakistan covers that route.

Do Pakistanis need a visa to visit Yiwu?

Yes. Pakistani ordinary passport holders need a Chinese visa to enter mainland China. A visa checker that verified China's policy on 17 October 2026 listed Pakistan as visa required and not eligible for China's 240-hour visa-free transit. Apply through the official Chinese embassy visa process and check the current requirements for a business visit before you book.

A trip only makes sense if you are buying in quantity for a business through a proper commercial import. For one item for your own use, the cost of flights, hotels and a visa is far more than the item is worth. Also note the market closes for the Spring Festival holiday each year. In 2026 it reopened on 28 February.

Find the same product online from a seller who sells single pieces, then have it bought and shipped to you. These steps work for most Yiwu-style goods.

  1. Pin down the item. Note the model, size, colour and material. If you only have a picture from a Yiwu stall or yiwugo.com, keep it.
  2. Search 1688 and Taobao by image. Both apps have camera search. Our guide to searching Taobao and 1688 by image shows how.
  3. Pick a seller that sells one piece. Check the price table starts at 1, the seller has sales history and reviews, and the variant you want is in stock.
  4. Check AliExpress too. For small items, the same thing may already ship to Pakistan directly.
  5. Work out whether one piece is worth it. Shipping one item from China to Pakistan usually costs Rs 5,000 to 20,000 depending on size and type, so a cheap accessory rarely makes sense on its own.

Common mistakes when looking at Yiwu for one item

  • Treating a Yiwu wholesale price as the price you will pay. That price assumes cartons. One piece costs more, and shipping comes on top.
  • Messaging stalls on yiwugo.com and waiting. Many stall owners will not reply to a one-piece request from abroad, or will quote a full carton.
  • Paying a stranger's personal account. Pay only through a platform or service with a refund process.
  • Buying branded lookalikes. Copies of famous brands are counterfeits, and Pakistan Customs seizes them.
  • Forgetting restricted goods. Perfumes, aerosols and loose batteries are commonly restricted on flights, whatever market they come from.
