Pakistan Import Policy Order Explained for Buyers

By Muhammad Shamshad AslamUpdated November 12, 20269 min read
Quick answer

Pakistan's Import Policy Order is the Ministry of Commerce's rulebook on what may be imported. The current one, Import Policy Order 2022 (SRO 545(I)/2022), allows imports from anywhere unless an item is banned in Appendix A, restricted with conditions in Appendix B, or limited when used in Appendix C. It is amended often, so check the current text.

What is the Import Policy Order?

The Import Policy Order is the document that says which goods may come into Pakistan freely, which are banned, and which are allowed only on conditions. It does not set duty rates. The customs tariff decides how much you pay; the Import Policy Order decides whether the goods may come in at all. For the full import process, start with our guide on how to import from China to Pakistan.

Who issues it and under what law?

The Ministry of Commerce issues it under section 3(1) of the Imports and Exports (Control) Act 1950. The current version is the Import Policy Order 2022, notified as SRO 545(I)/2022 on 22 April 2022. ProPakistani reported at the time that it was published on the Trade Information Portal of Pakistan. It is amended by further SROs, usually after approval from the Economic Coordination Committee, so the 2022 text is a base that has changed many times.

What is the basic rule of the order?

Everything is importable unless the order says otherwise. ProPakistani quoted the general rule when the order was notified: the import of all goods may be allowed from worldwide sources, unless the order bans, prohibits or restricts them. That is why there is no general import licence in Pakistan. It also means that a product not named anywhere in the order is not banned by it, though other laws and regulators may still apply. Reading the order is therefore a search for your product's code in a few lists, not a hunt for a permission that covers everything. The order also contains a transition rule: an amendment does not apply to goods whose bill of lading or irrevocable letter of credit was issued before the amending order. Our guide on whether you need an import licence explains what registrations sit around this.

What are Appendix A, B and C?

They are the three lists most buyers need. Each one is organised by tariff code and product description.

AppendixTitleWhat it means for a buyer
Appendix ABanned items (negative list)Cannot be imported unless the government specifically relaxes the ban
Appendix BRestricted itemsImportable only if the stated condition is met, such as a regulator's approval or a standard
Appendix CUsed or second-hand goodsItems not allowed, or allowed only with limits, when used

Other appendices support specific paragraphs. If your item is not on any of these lists, it is freely importable under the order, though other laws can still apply.

What is inside Appendix B?

Appendix B is split into parts by type of condition. The order's contents list these parts:

  • Part I, health and safety requirements, for goods that must meet a condition before entry.
  • Part II, procedural requirements, for goods that need a specific permission or document.
  • Part III, live animals and animal products.
  • Part IV, plants and plant products.
  • Part V, narcotic drugs and psychotropic substances.
  • Part VI, medical devices.

In practice, a Part B condition turns into an LPCO: a licence, permit, certificate or other document from a regulator. PSW says filing through its Single Declaration is mandatory where such a requirement applies, and PSQCA release orders, for example, are applied for and tracked on PSW. Our documents guide lists the common regulators.

What else does the order cover?

The order is longer than its lists. Its paragraphs cover several situations a buyer may meet:

ParagraphTopic
5 to 7Prohibitions, restrictions, and restrictions under other laws
8 and 9Classification of goods, and disputes about whether an item is importable
10Import of used plant, machinery and equipment
11Clearance of goods sent by overseas Pakistanis without foreign exchange
13Temporary import
16Vehicles under the personal baggage, transfer of residence and gift schemes
18Change of consignee
21Relaxation of prohibitions and restrictions

When it was notified, the order also listed items that cannot be imported from India.

How often does it change, and what changed in 2026?

It changes several times a year, and 2026 was no exception. Two examples we found:

  • Used cars. Dawn reported on 16 January 2026 that the order was amended to stop used-car imports under the personal baggage scheme, leaving only the transfer of residence and gift schemes, with a one-year bar on transferring such vehicles.
  • Forced labour. Profit reported on 15 July 2026 that the ECC approved an amendment restricting imports of goods made with forced or compulsory labour, using the definition in the ILO's Forced Labour Convention.

Because of this, a list you saw a year ago may be out of date. Check the current consolidated text, or ask a licensed customs agent, before you rely on it.

How does the order fit with other laws and regulators?

The order is not the only rule that can stop or condition an import. Its paragraph 7 deals with prohibitions and restrictions imposed under other laws, and several regulators apply their own rules alongside it:

  • Environmental law. The IEA's summary of the order notes that it supplements the Pakistan Environmental Protection Act 1997, under which importing hazardous waste is prohibited and handling or importing a hazardous substance needs a licence.
  • Telecom law. PTA requires type approval for terminal equipment imported for sale, and under its 2021 regulations, as reported by ProPakistani, a person may import five mobile devices for personal use in a calendar year.
  • Drug law. DRAP controls medicines and medical devices, including imports for personal use.
  • Standards. Goods under PSQCA's compulsory standards need a PSQCA release order, applied for through PSW.

So an item can be absent from Appendix A and B and still need permission from one of these bodies. When the order and another law both apply, you have to satisfy both. A licensed customs agent will usually know which regulators touch a given tariff code.

How do you check whether your item is restricted?

Check by tariff code, not by product name. In order:

  1. Find the eight-digit PCT code for your item. Our guide on how to check customs duty shows FBR's search tools.
  2. Search Appendix A for the code or its four-digit heading. If it is there, stop.
  3. Search Appendix B and read the condition in the column next to the code. Note which regulator it names.
  4. If the item is used or refurbished, check Appendix C.
  5. Check other regulators even if the order is silent. Phones need PTA registration, and medicines need DRAP permission.
  6. Look for amending SROs issued after the version you read.

Does the order give a gift or personal allowance for parcels?

No. The order's personal baggage and gift schemes are about vehicles, not about parcels you buy from a Chinese seller. A parcel is an import of goods and is assessed like one. The only small-parcel relief is in customs law: section 19C of the Customs Act says no duties or taxes are demanded on post or courier goods worth Rs 1,000 or less. Quantity also matters. FBR treats goods imported prima facie for trading or gain, rather than personal use or gift, as a commercial quantity, and commercial rules then apply. Our guide to personal use vs commercial import explains that line.

What does the order mean if you want one item from China?

For most gadgets, tools, parts and household items, nothing beyond the normal customs process: they are freely importable. The order matters when your item is banned, needs a regulator's approval, or is used. Second-hand items deserve a closer look, because Appendix C limits some goods only when they are used, and a refurbished or pre-owned listing on a Chinese site counts as used. Phones, medicines and anything with a radio transmitter are the other categories where a quick check saves a held parcel. Frequently asked questions

What is the Import Policy Order in Pakistan?

The Ministry of Commerce's order under the Imports and Exports (Control) Act 1950 that lists banned, restricted and used-goods items. The current one is SRO 545(I)/2022.

What is Appendix A of the Import Policy Order?

The banned items list, also called the negative list. Goods on it cannot be imported.

What is Appendix B of the Import Policy Order?

The restricted items list. Goods on it can be imported only if the stated condition is met, such as approval from DRAP, PSQCA or another regulator.

Does the Import Policy Order set customs duty?

No. It decides whether goods may be imported. Duty rates come from the Pakistan Customs Tariff and related SROs.

How often is the Import Policy Order amended?

Several times a year, through SROs from the Ministry of Commerce. In 2026 amendments covered used cars and goods made with forced labour.

Is a gift parcel from China exempt under the Import Policy Order?

No. The order's gift scheme is for vehicles. A parcel is assessed as an import, and only post or courier goods worth Rs 1,000 or less pay nothing.
