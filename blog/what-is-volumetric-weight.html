What Is Volumetric Weight and How Is It Calculated?

By Muhammad Shamshad AslamUpdated October 23, 20265 min read
Quick answer

Volumetric weight is the weight a carrier charges for the space a parcel takes up. You multiply length, width and height in centimetres and divide by a set number: 5,000 for DHL, FedEx and UPS express, and commonly 6,000 for air freight. You pay on whichever is higher, the volumetric or the actual weight.

What is volumetric weight?

Volumetric weight, also called dimensional weight, is a weight worked out from the size of a box rather than from a scale. Aircraft fill up by space before they fill up by weight, so carriers charge a light but bulky box as if it were heavier. A pillow and a brick of the same weight do not cost the same to fly.

The carrier then compares the volumetric weight with the actual weight and charges the higher one. That higher figure is the chargeable weight.

How do you calculate volumetric weight?

Measure the outer box in centimetres, multiply the three sides, and divide by the carrier's divisor.

Length (cm) x Width (cm) x Height (cm) / divisor = volumetric weight in kg

The divisor is the part that changes. When we checked in October 2026, the big express couriers and air freight used these:

A smaller divisor gives a bigger number, so the same box is charged as heavier by an express courier than by air freight. FedEx also rounds each side up to the next whole centimetre before multiplying. Some forwarders use 5,000 for air freight too, so always ask which divisor a quote uses.

What does it look like with real numbers?

Three examples, with typical box sizes rather than measurements of any one product.

Example 1: a bulky, light box. A boxed table fan in a carton of 40 x 30 x 20 cm, weighing 2 kg.

  • Volume: 40 x 30 x 20 = 24,000 cubic cm.
  • At 5,000: 24,000 / 5,000 = 4.8 kg.
  • At 6,000: 24,000 / 6,000 = 4 kg.
  • Charged on volume: 4.8 kg by courier, 4 kg by air freight, against 2 kg on the scale.

Example 2: a small, dense box. A set of metal tools in a box of 30 x 20 x 10 cm, weighing 3 kg.

  • Volume: 30 x 20 x 10 = 6,000 cubic cm.
  • At 5,000: 1.2 kg. At 6,000: 1 kg.
  • Charged on actual weight: 3 kg, because it is higher than both.

Example 3: the DHL example. DHL's own help page uses a 20 kg shipment measuring 75 x 40 x 40 cm.

  • Volume: 75 x 40 x 40 = 120,000 cubic cm.
  • At 5,000: 24 kg.
  • Charged on 24 kg, not 20 kg.

The pattern is simple. Dense items such as phones, earbuds and tools are charged close to their real weight. Hollow items such as lamps, fans, chairs and anything in a big retail box are charged on size.

What is chargeable weight, and how is it rounded?

Chargeable weight is the higher of the actual weight and the volumetric weight. It is the number your shipping price is built on. Carriers then round it up to their next weight step, commonly the next half kilogram for express, so a 1.1 kg chargeable parcel is often billed as 1.5 kg. Check the rounding rule of the service quoting you, because it varies by carrier and service.

For a parcel of several boxes, the volumetric weights of the boxes are usually added together.

How can you keep volumetric weight down?

  • Use the package size, not the product size. Listings on Alibaba, 1688 and AliExpress often show package dimensions and gross weight in the specifications.
  • Pick the slim or folding version of the same item if one exists.
  • Skip bulky bundles such as display boxes or large stands you do not need.
  • Prefer air freight to express for a light, bulky item, because the 6,000 divisor charges it as lighter.

Frequently asked questions

What is the formula for volumetric weight?

Length x width x height in centimetres, divided by the carrier's divisor. DHL, FedEx and UPS use 5,000. Air freight commonly uses 6,000.

Why do DHL and air freight give different volumetric weights?

They use different divisors. DHL divides by 5,000 and most air freight divides by 6,000, so the same box comes out about 20 percent heavier with DHL.

Do I pay for actual weight or volumetric weight?

Whichever is higher. That higher figure is called the chargeable weight, and the shipping price is based on it.

How do I find a product's box size before I buy?

Look for package size or packing size and gross weight in the listing's specifications. Use the box measurements, not the product's own size.

Is volumetric weight used for sea freight?

Sea freight is priced mainly by cubic metre rather than by volumetric kilograms. For one item, sea freight rarely makes sense because of fixed port and document fees.
