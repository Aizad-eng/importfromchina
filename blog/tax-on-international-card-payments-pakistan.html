Tax on International Card Payments in Pakistan: Filer vs Non-Filer

By Muhammad Shamshad AslamUpdated October 23, 20269 min read
Quick answer

From 1 July 2026, advance tax on card payments to merchants outside Pakistan is 0.5% if you are on FBR's Active Taxpayers List and 1% if you are not, under section 236Y. It was 5% and 10% before. Your bank or app also adds its own foreign transaction fee, tax on that fee and an exchange rate margin, which on several cards we checked costs more than the tax.

What tax do you pay on international card payments in Pakistan?

You pay advance income tax under section 236Y of the Income Tax Ordinance, 2001. The law says every banking company shall collect advance tax, at the time it transfers money outside Pakistan, from a person who completed a credit, debit or prepaid card transaction with a person outside Pakistan. The rate is set in Division XXVII of Part IV of the First Schedule, and the law says the tax is adjustable.

In plain terms: when you buy something on AliExpress, Temu, Amazon or any foreign site with a Pakistani card, your bank or app deducts a small percentage as tax and passes it to the FBR. It is separate from the bank's own fees and separate from any customs duty on the parcel when it arrives.

What are the filer and non-filer rates in 2026-27?

For tax year 2027, which started on 1 July 2026, FBR's withholding tax rate card shows 0.50% for people on the Active Taxpayers List and 1.00% for people who are not. FBR's budget 2026-27 salient features describe the change as a reduction of advance tax on foreign payments through debit, credit and prepaid cards from 5% to 0.5%.

FBR's own rate card carries a disclaimer that the Ordinance prevails if the card and the statute ever differ. Rates change with each budget, so check the current card before relying on any figure, including ours.

Which card payments does section 236Y cover?

Any card transaction with a person outside Pakistan, where your bank sends money abroad to settle it. That covers online shopping on foreign sites, app and software subscriptions, and card payments you make while travelling. Payments to Pakistani merchants are not covered.

A rupee price on a foreign site does not always change this. SadaPay's help centre says rupee charges from international merchants such as streaming services are settled in US dollars and treated the same as a regular international transaction. So if a foreign shop shows you a price in rupees, expect your card provider to treat the payment as international.

How do you know if you count as a filer?

For this tax, filer means being on the FBR's Active Taxpayers List, usually called the ATL. The rate card uses the terms ATL and non-ATL, not filer and non-filer. You get on the list by filing your income tax return.

  • By SMS. FBR's ATL page says an individual can send ATL, a space, and the 13-digit CNIC to 9966.
  • Online. FBR's IRIS portal has an Active Taxpayer List search by CNIC, passport number or NTN.
  • Your bank's view. Your bank or app checks the list against your CNIC. If you have just filed, it can take time for the bank's records to catch up, so check a transaction after you file.

For an individual, the FBR uses the 13-digit CNIC as the NTN once you e-enrol on IRIS. Being registered is not the same as being active. You need to have filed a return to appear on the list.

Can you get the advance tax back?

You can adjust it, if you file. Section 236Y says the tax collected under it shall be adjustable. That means a person who files an income tax return can count the tax deducted on card payments against the tax they owe for the year, and claim it in the return. Keep your card statements or the app's transaction details as a record.

If you do not file a return, there is nothing to adjust it against, so in practice it is a cost. At 1% on a single order it is small. At the old rate of 10% it was not, which is why many people noticed it more before July 2026.

Why does your bank or app still show 5% or 10%?

Some published schedules had not caught up when we checked in October 2026. SadaPay's schedule of charges for July to December 2026, revised June 2026, still said withholding tax is charged on all international transactions at 5% for filers and 10% for non-filers. FBR's rate card for the same period shows 0.5% and 1%.

A printed schedule and the amount actually deducted are not always the same thing, so look at the deduction on a real transaction. If you see 5% or 10% taken on a payment made after 1 July 2026, ask your bank or app in writing which rate it applied and why. Quote the FBR rate card for tax year 2027 in your message.

Is there FED or sales tax on international card payments?

On the bank's fee, yes. On the purchase itself, we found no FED. Card providers charge a fee for foreign transactions, and that fee is a financial service, so tax is added on top of it.

  • SadaPay lists a 6% international transaction fee excluding FED. The worked example on its own help page charges 6.96% in total once the tax on the fee is included. Its schedule says charges are not inclusive of FED unless stated.
  • HBL publishes its schedule of bank charges for July to December 2026 as exclusive of FED and sales tax, and marks foreign transaction charges as an item on which FED or sales tax applies.
  • NayaPay says all rates in its July to December 2026 schedule are exclusive of applicable taxes, except items marked with an asterisk. Its US$0.25 international card transaction fee is not marked.

The tax rate on bank fees can differ by province, so we have not stated one. The practical point is to read the fee as the fee plus tax.

How much does the bank's exchange rate add?

It depends on the bank, and it is usually the least visible cost. HBL's page on foreign transactions explains the usual chain. Visa, Mastercard or UnionPay first converts the shop's currency to US dollars at the network's rate. Then the US dollar amount is converted to rupees at the rate at which HBL buys dollars to settle the transaction. HBL notes that this rate can differ from the rate on the day you paid, because of market movements and any risk premium on dollar availability. HBL then adds foreign transaction charges from its schedule, which lists 4% of the transaction amount as the international POS transaction fee on several of its debit cards for July to December 2026. HBL says transactions settled in foreign currency count for this fee.

SadaPay says its conversion rates are passed on to it by partner banks, and then adds its 6% fee. NayaPay describes FX settlement costs, the cost of buying foreign currency from authorised dealers to settle the payment, plus its US$0.25 per transaction. None of them publishes the exchange margin as a single percentage, so the easiest way to see it is to compare the rupee amount on your statement with the open-market rate on the same day.

Worked example: what a Rs 20,000 order really costs on a card

Suppose your card provider converts a foreign order to Rs 20,000 before any charges, and you pay with a SadaPay card. This is an illustration using the rates published when we checked in October 2026. It assumes the advance tax is calculated on the converted order amount. Your provider's exact base and rounding may differ.

Two things stand out. First, after the July 2026 cut, the difference between a filer and a non-filer on this order is only Rs 100. Second, the card fee is now the bigger cost by far. On a bank debit card with a 4% foreign transaction fee, the fee before tax on the same order would be Rs 800, still well above the advance tax.

This is only the payment. Customs duty and taxes on the parcel when it lands in Pakistan are a different calculation. Our guide to customs duty from China to Pakistan explains that part.

Common mistakes with tax on foreign card payments

  • Confusing the advance tax with the card fee. The 236Y tax is 0.5% or 1% now. A 6% or 4% deduction is the provider's fee, not the tax.
  • Not checking whether the old rate was applied. Some schedules still printed 5% and 10% in October 2026.
  • Throwing away statements. If you file a return, you need the record of the tax deducted to adjust it.
  • Assuming rupee billing avoids the charges. Card providers can still treat a foreign merchant's rupee charge as international.
  • Paying for someone else on your card. The tax and the SBP cap of US$30,000 per person per year on card-based foreign transactions are yours, not theirs.
  • Forgetting customs. Paying tax on the card payment does not pay the import duty on the parcel.

Frequently asked questions

What is the tax on international card payments in Pakistan in 2026?

From 1 July 2026, FBR's rate card shows advance tax under section 236Y of 0.5% for people on the Active Taxpayers List and 1% for others. It was 5% and 10% before.

Does section 236Y apply to AliExpress and Temu purchases?

It applies to card transactions with a merchant outside Pakistan, which covers foreign shopping sites. Card providers such as SadaPay treat rupee charges from foreign merchants as international too.

Can I get the advance tax on card payments back?

Section 236Y says the tax is adjustable, so if you file an income tax return you can count it against the tax you owe for the year. Keep your statements as a record.

Why is my bank still deducting 5% on foreign card payments?

Some schedules had not been updated when we checked in October 2026. Check the actual deduction, and if 5% or 10% was taken after 1 July 2026, ask your bank in writing which rate it applied.

Is FED charged on international card transactions?

Tax is charged on the bank's foreign transaction fee, not on the purchase itself. SadaPay's 6% fee comes to 6.96% in its own example, and HBL marks foreign transaction charges as subject to FED or sales tax.

Is there a limit on spending abroad with a Pakistani card?

Yes. The State Bank of Pakistan caps card-based international transactions at US$30,000 per person per year, counting all your cards together.
