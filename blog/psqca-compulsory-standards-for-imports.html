PSQCA Compulsory Standards for Imported Goods Explained

By Muhammad Shamshad AslamUpdated November 12, 202610 min read
Quick answer

PSQCA compulsory standards are Pakistani quality standards that certain imported goods must meet before customs releases them. The products are listed in Appendix N of the Import Policy Order, and commercial importers need an electronic release order from PSQCA through Pakistan Single Window. The list covers food, cosmetics, cement, steel, tyres and many household electrical goods.

What is PSQCA and what does it do at import?

PSQCA, the Pakistan Standards and Quality Control Authority, is the federal body that sets and enforces Pakistani product standards. At the border it works under section 8 of the PSQCA Act 1996, checking that listed imported goods meet the same standards as the same goods made in Pakistan. Its own import and export page says it carries out pre-shipment and post-shipment conformity checks at seaports, dry ports and airports, and works to ISO 17020.

The rule behind this sits in the Import Policy Order. It says imports are subject to the same national quality standards or regulations as similar goods produced in Pakistan, and that the list of those standards is the one PSQCA notifies, published as Appendix N. A product on Appendix N needs PSQCA's clearance before customs releases it. For the wider set of clearances some goods need, see our companion guide on which imports need an NOC in Pakistan.

Which imported goods fall under PSQCA compulsory standards?

Appendix N lists goods by PCT code alongside the Pakistani standard (PS number) or international standard (IEC) they must meet. The fullest list we could read was the annex to a PSW circular dated 22 December 2023, which runs to 135 lines numbered against Appendix N entries up to 159. The groups are:

  • Food: packaged milk, honey, coffee, spices, cooking oils, infant food, biscuits, juices, bottled water, table salt.
  • Personal care: skin powder, skin creams, shampoo, hair dye, toothpaste, shaving cream, toilet soap, detergent.
  • Building materials: cement, steel bars and sheets, paint, plastic pipes, sanitary taps.
  • Vehicles and tyres: car, motorcycle and bicycle tyres, motorcycle rims, lead acid batteries, motorcycles including electric ones, three wheelers.
  • Household electrical goods and gadgets: irons, microwaves, kettles, toasters, speakers, headphones, switches, plugs, adaptors, extension leads, LED lamps, lithium batteries, UPS units and solar inverters.
  • Other items: gas stoves and heaters, foam mattresses, spectacle frames, razors and blades, lighters, electricity meters, surgical gloves, some X-ray equipment.

How does PSQCA clearance work at customs now?

It runs through Pakistan Single Window (PSW). The December 2023 PSW circular told traders importing Appendix N goods to file a Single Declaration (Imports) through PSW so that PSQCA issues an electronic release order, after which customs clears the consignment. The switch from the older WeBOC system took effect on 3 January 2024 at all Karachi seaports and Jinnah International Airport, and at the dry ports and airports in Lahore and Peshawar.

The circular describes these as commercial imports, coded HC type. Customs will not release a listed commercial consignment until the PSQCA release order is in the system, which is why a missing release order is one of the common reasons containers sit at the port. That idle time costs money, as our guide to demurrage and detention charges explains.

What does an importer have to provide?

PSQCA's import page links to the forms a commercial importer needs. When we checked in November 2026 these were:

  • a post-shipment checklist for mandatory products,
  • an application for a PSQCA certificate,
  • a consignee information form,
  • an undertaking.

The same page links separate fee schedules for food, for non-food products and for electrical and electronic products, plus a schedule of laboratory fees. The amounts are in the linked files rather than on the page itself, so we have not repeated them here. Check the current schedule before you budget, because a laboratory report adds both cost and days.

Does PSQCA apply to one item bought for personal use?

The official notices we read were written for commercial imports and did not say how a single personal item is treated. The PSW circular speaks of commercial imports, and PSQCA's page does not mention an exemption for personal use either way. We did not find a published exemption, so we cannot tell you one exists.

What we can say is that the risk grows with quantity. Ten identical irons or a carton of LED bulbs look like trade stock, and trade stock of an Appendix N product needs a release order. One kettle for your own kitchen is a different case, but the decision sits with the officer assessing the declaration. For the line customs draws between personal and commercial imports, see our guide to how to import from China to Pakistan.

Why do earbuds, chargers and batteries matter here?

Because several of the most popular items from China sit on the list. Headphones and earphones, plug adaptors, extension leads, LED lamps and portable lithium batteries all appear in the PSW annex with an international standard attached. Many buyers assume PSQCA is only about cement, steel and food. For gadgets it matters as soon as you order more than one or two.

Wireless earbuds can also fall under a second regulator, because Bluetooth devices come under PTA's framework for short range devices. Battery shipping rules apply on top of both. Our guide to importing earbuds, smartwatches and wearables from China covers those points.

How do you check whether your product is on the list, step by step?

  1. Get the PCT code. Appendix N is organised by code. Our guide on finding the HS code for your product explains how.
  2. Compare it with Appendix N. Look for your code in the current list, and read the description, because one code can cover more than one product.
  3. Note the standard. The entry names a PS or IEC standard. Ask the seller whether the product has a laboratory report against that standard.
  4. Check the declaration route. A commercial consignment needs a Single Declaration through PSW and a PSQCA release order before customs releases it.
  5. Ask PSQCA if unsure. Its import section publishes the forms and a contact page.

What mistakes do importers make with PSQCA goods?

  • Assuming a foreign mark is enough. A mark printed on the box is not a PSQCA release order. Customs looks for the release order in the system.
  • Using the wrong code. A code that misses Appendix N may clear once and then be reassessed later, with penalties for misdeclaration.
  • Ordering stock as several small parcels. Splitting trade quantities into parcels to avoid the rules is a misdeclaration risk, not a shortcut.
  • Applying after arrival. Paperwork started once the goods land means days of port or airport storage.

Frequently asked questions

What is Appendix N?

It is the list in Pakistan's Import Policy Order of products that must meet Pakistani quality standards notified by PSQCA. Each entry gives a PCT code and the PS or IEC standard that applies.

Are earphones on the PSQCA compulsory list?

Yes. The PSW annex dated December 2023 lists headphones and earphones under PCT 8518.3000 against IEC 60268-7.

Are lithium batteries covered by PSQCA?

Secondary lithium cells and batteries for portable applications, PCT 8507.2000 and 8507.6000, are listed against IEC 61960-3 in the same annex.

How do commercial importers get PSQCA clearance?

They file a Single Declaration through Pakistan Single Window, and PSQCA issues an electronic release order before customs clears the goods. This has applied at Karachi, Lahore and Peshawar since 3 January 2024.

Is there a PSQCA exemption for personal use?

We did not find one published. The notices we read are written for commercial imports, and quantity is what usually makes customs treat goods as commercial.

Does a CE mark replace PSQCA clearance?

No. A mark on the product is not a PSQCA release order, and customs looks for the release order for listed commercial imports.
