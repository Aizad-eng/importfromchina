How to Make Sure a Chinese Seller Packs Your Item Safely

By Muhammad Shamshad AslamUpdated November 12, 20268 min read
Quick answer

To make sure a Chinese seller packs your item safely, ask before they ship: keep the item in its original box, add an outer carton with at least 5 cm of cushioning on every side, protect corners and screens, declare any battery, and send you the packed size, weight and a picture of the sealed box. Agree it in the platform chat so you have proof.

Who decides how your item is packed in China?

The seller decides how your item is packed, so the only way to influence it is to ask, clearly and in writing, before they ship. Most Chinese sellers pack for domestic delivery inside China, where a parcel travels a day or two by road. An international parcel to Pakistan is handled many more times, through sorting centres, an aircraft hold, customs and local delivery, so domestic-style packing is not always enough.

Sellers respond best to short, specific requests. "Pack well please" means nothing. "Please add an outer carton with bubble wrap around the original box" is something they can do. Put requests in the platform's own chat, not on another app, so the record stays with the order. Checking that the seller is genuine in the first place matters too, and our guide on how to verify a Chinese seller covers that.

What should you ask the seller for before they pack?

Ask for five things before the seller packs, and keep each request to one line.

  1. Keep the original retail box. The maker's box often has fitted inserts that protect the item better than anything added later.
  2. An outer shipping carton. Put the retail box inside a sturdy corrugated carton, not just a plastic bag.
  3. Cushioning on every side. Bubble wrap, foam or paper so the item cannot move.
  4. The packed size and weight. So you can check the shipping charge, which is based on the box size.
  5. A picture of the sealed box with the label. This shows how it left and confirms the shipping details.

If the item has moving parts, such as a printer head, a hinge or a motor, also ask the seller whether it has a transport lock or a fitted insert and to make sure it is in place. Many machines are designed to be shipped with these, and leaving them out is a common cause of damage.

A simple message works: "Please keep the original box, put it in a strong outer carton with bubble wrap on all sides, then send me the box size, weight and a picture of the sealed parcel. Thank you." A translation app can turn this into Chinese if the seller prefers.

How much cushioning does an item need?

An item needs enough cushioning that it sits in the centre of the box and cannot touch the walls, and a common guideline is at least 5 cm of packing material between the item and every wall and corner. That is the advice of Packsend, an Australian packing and courier chain, which also says there should be no empty space in the box, because under-filled boxes can collapse.

Two points to pass on to the seller:

  • Fill every gap. Crumpled paper or bubble wrap on the base, the top and the sides. A loose item in a big box is how screens crack.
  • Match the cushioning to the weight. Heavy or sharp items flatten thin bubble wrap. They need denser foam or more layers.

Should the seller double box your item?

Double boxing, a retail or inner box inside a larger outer carton, is worth asking for with fragile items, but it adds size, and couriers charge by size. Packsend's guidance for double boxing is an outer box at least 14 cm longer, wider and deeper than the inner one, with cushioning between them.

Here is what that does to the shipping weight with a courier that divides by 5,000. An inner box of 30 x 20 x 15 cm has a volumetric weight of 1.8 kg. An outer box 14 cm larger in each direction, 44 x 34 x 29 cm, has a volumetric weight of about 8.7 kg. That is close to five times the charge for the same item.

So use double boxing where it earns its cost, for glass, screens, ceramics and precision instruments. For sturdy items such as tools, clothes or a laptop in its fitted retail box, an outer carton that fits closely with good cushioning is usually enough. The balance between protection and size is the whole skill of packing for international shipping.

How do batteries and liquids change the packing?

Items with lithium batteries must be declared and packed to air transport rules, and liquids, aerosols and perfumes are restricted by air. This is less about damage and more about whether the parcel is allowed to fly. The airline body IATA has been tightening the packing requirements for lithium batteries packed with or inside equipment, including a new requirement introduced from January 2025.

Ask the seller to keep the battery inside the device if it is designed that way, to protect switches so the device cannot turn on in transit, and to declare the battery on the shipment. Do not let a seller add a power bank or loose battery as a gift. Our guide on lithium battery rules for shipping from China to Pakistan explains the categories.

What should the outside of the box show?

The outside of the box should carry one clear shipping label on the largest face, with your name exactly as on your CNIC, your full address and a working phone number. Packsend also advises putting delivery details inside the box as well, in case the outer label is torn, and marking fragile parcels "FRAGILE" and "THIS SIDE UP".

Ask the seller to seal the box with tape across all seams on the top and bottom, the H pattern used by most couriers, and to remove or cover old labels and barcodes if they reuse a carton. An old barcode can send a parcel to the wrong place.

What proof should you keep in case the item arrives damaged?

Proof matters because a damage claim usually turns on two questions: was the item packed as agreed, and was it damaged in transit or before it left. Keep the platform chat where the seller agreed the packing, the seller's picture of the sealed parcel, the tracking number and the order page. If damage happens, a refund or replacement depends on showing what was agreed and what arrived.

When the parcel arrives, take your own pictures of the outer box before opening, then of the inner packing and the item. Keep all the packing until the claim is settled. Remember that customs duty, once paid, does not come back: DHL Pakistan, for example, says duties paid to Pakistan Customs are non-refundable even if the goods are returned or rejected. That is one more reason to get the packing right before the item leaves China.

What should you do if the item arrives damaged?

Frequently asked questions

Can I ask a Chinese seller to pack my item better?

Yes. Ask in the platform chat before they ship, with a specific request such as an outer carton and bubble wrap on all sides. Most sellers will agree to simple requests.

How much bubble wrap does a parcel need?

A common guideline is at least 5 cm of cushioning between the item and every wall and corner of the box, with no empty space left inside.

Does double boxing increase the shipping cost?

Yes, often a lot, because couriers charge by box size. A 30 x 20 x 15 cm box inside an outer box 14 cm bigger each way goes from about 1.8 kg to 8.7 kg chargeable.

Should I ask the seller for a picture of the packed parcel?

Yes. A picture of the sealed box with its label shows how it left and helps with any damage claim.

Can the seller put a power bank in my parcel?

Avoid it. Loose lithium batteries and power banks are restricted by air and can stop the whole parcel from flying.

Is customs duty refunded if my item arrives broken?

Usually not. Duty paid to Pakistan Customs is generally non-refundable, so a refund for damage comes from the seller, platform or service, not from Customs.
