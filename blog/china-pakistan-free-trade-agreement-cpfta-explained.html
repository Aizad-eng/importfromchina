China-Pakistan Free Trade Agreement (CPFTA) Explained for Buyers

By importfromchina.shop teamUpdated October 3, 20266 min read
Quick answer

The China-Pakistan Free Trade Agreement (CPFTA) reduces or removes customs duty on listed goods made in China when the shipment carries a valid Certificate of Origin issued in China. It does not reduce sales tax, additional customs duty or advance income tax, which still apply in full. For a single item, the certificate is rarely available from a marketplace seller, so most one-off parcels pay the normal duty rate. The saving is real for registered importers moving commercial volumes.

What is the CPFTA?

The China-Pakistan Free Trade Agreement is a bilateral trade agreement between the two governments. The first phase took effect in 2007 and a second phase, often called CPFTA Phase II, came into force in late 2019 and widened the list of goods with reduced or zero customs duty. The agreement covers goods in both directions. For Pakistani importers, it means that many products of Chinese origin can enter Pakistan at a lower customs duty rate than the same products from, say, Vietnam or Germany, provided the origin is proven.

Which taxes does it reduce?

Only one: customs duty. This is the point most buyers miss. Pakistan charges several taxes on an import, and the FTA touches just the first line.

So if an item carries 20 percent customs duty and CPFTA brings it to zero, the saving is that 20 percent of customs value and the knock-on effect on the taxes calculated on top of it. The rest of the stack remains. Combined import taxes commonly run 25 to 60 percent of customs value without the FTA; with it, the figure is lower but far from zero. The layering is explained in how customs duty from China to Pakistan is calculated.

What is a Certificate of Origin?

A Certificate of Origin (CO) is a document that certifies the goods were produced in China under the agreement's rules of origin. For the CPFTA, the preferential certificate is issued in China by authorised bodies, commonly the China Council for the Promotion of International Trade (CCPIT) or China Customs, on application by the exporter. It lists the exporter, the importer, the goods, their HS code and the origin criterion met. It must match the invoice and the goods declaration. A general certificate of origin or a seller's statement on the invoice is not the same thing and does not give the preferential rate.

Does every Chinese product qualify?

No. Three conditions have to hold at once. First, the goods must be on the agreement's list of covered PCT codes at a reduced rate. Pakistan kept a list of protected lines, so some common consumer goods are not covered or only partially reduced. Second, the goods must meet the rules of origin, which usually require that they were wholly produced in China or that enough of their value was added there. A product assembled in China from mostly foreign parts may not qualify. Third, the preferential Certificate of Origin must accompany the shipment and be accepted by Pakistan Customs. The lists and rates are revised, so confirm the current treatment for your PCT code. Finding that code is covered in how to find the HS code for your product.

Can an individual buying one item use CPFTA?

In theory, yes, there is no rule against it. In practice, almost never. The certificate is applied for by the exporter for a specific shipment. A marketplace seller on Taobao, 1688 or AliExpress will not apply for one for a single piece, and most would not know how. Trading companies on Alibaba sometimes can for a commercial order, with a fee and a few days' delay, but it is unusual for one unit. Consolidated air freight shipments carrying many unrelated parcels are not covered by one certificate either. The result is that one-off parcels are assessed at the normal customs duty rate.

How much difference does it make on a single item?

Less than people hope. Even where the certificate is obtainable, the reduction applies to customs duty only, and the cost and delay of getting the document can exceed the saving on one unit. The FTA was built for registered commercial importers clearing containers under their own NTN, where a few percentage points across a large consignment is meaningful. For a buyer who wants one gadget or one garment, the tax stack is what it is, and the useful questions are what the item's PCT code is and whether the item is restricted, not whether an FTA applies. Frequently asked questions

Does CPFTA make imports from China tax free?

No. It reduces or removes customs duty on listed goods with a Certificate of Origin. Sales tax, additional customs duty, regulatory duty and advance income tax still apply.

Who issues the Certificate of Origin for CPFTA?

Authorised bodies in China, commonly CCPIT or China Customs, on application by the exporter for a specific shipment.

Can I get a Certificate of Origin for an AliExpress or Taobao order?

Almost never. Marketplace sellers do not apply for preferential certificates for single pieces. One-off parcels are assessed at the normal duty rate.

Are all Chinese goods covered by the CPFTA?

No. Coverage is by PCT code, Pakistan kept a protected list, and the goods must meet rules of origin. Check the current treatment for your code.

Does CPFTA apply to phones and electronics?

Some electronic lines are covered and some are not, and phones still need PTA registration and tax regardless. Confirm the specific PCT code.
