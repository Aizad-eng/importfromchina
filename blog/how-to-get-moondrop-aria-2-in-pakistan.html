What is the Moondrop Aria 2 and who is it for?

The Aria 2 is a wired in-ear monitor (IEM) from Moondrop, a Chinese audio brand. It uses one dynamic driver per side, which reviewers describe as having a TiN ceramic-coated dome composite diaphragm developed in-house. It is a step up from the free earphones that came with your phone, but it is not a wireless earbud. You plug it into a headphone jack or a USB-C dongle.

It suits three kinds of people in Pakistan:

  • Someone who listens on a phone or laptop for hours a day and wants a comfortable, well-built wired set.
  • Someone who already owns a cheaper IEM and wants something sturdier, with a metal shell instead of plastic.
  • Someone planning to add a small USB-C DAC later, because the Aria 2 comes ready for a balanced 4.4mm connection.

Because it is wired, it has no battery to wear out and no Bluetooth lag in games or videos on your phone. That alone is why many people still choose a wired IEM over earbuds.

It does not suit you if you need a microphone for calls, want wireless, or have very small ears. The official package list includes no microphone cable, and reviewers note the shell is a few millimetres wider than the original Aria.

In plain terms, 33 ohms and 122dB/Vrms mean it gets loud easily. A reviewer at Everyday Listening called it very easy to drive. You do not need a separate amplifier for normal listening from a phone dongle.

The same review adds useful build details. The shells are alloy-cast and CNC-milled and left unpainted, which should avoid the chipping some owners saw on painted earlier Moondrop shells. Isolation is about average for a dynamic driver IEM, so it blocks less outside noise than you might expect on a loud bus or rickshaw ride. Turn the volume up less, not more, in noisy places.

Which version of the Aria 2 should you buy?

Moondrop's page lists one version, with no colour options and no microphone variant. That makes picking simple, but listings on Chinese marketplaces can still confuse you:

  • Aria versus Aria 2. The original Aria is an older model with a different diaphragm and a painted shell. Sellers still list it. Check the title says "Aria 2" or "ARIA2" and that the listing images show the unpainted metal shell with a laser-etched faceplate pattern.
  • The cable and plugs. The Aria 2 cable uses a modular connector with a threaded lock, and the box contains both a 3.5mm and a 4.4mm plug. If a listing shows only one plug in the package contents, ask the seller before ordering.
  • Bundles. Some sellers bundle the Aria 2 with a dongle or extra tips. A bundle only makes sense if you want that exact dongle. Otherwise order the earphones alone.

Will the Aria 2 work with my phone in Pakistan?

A quick word on the two plugs. The 3.5mm plug is the normal headphone plug. The 4.4mm plug is a balanced connection, where each earpiece gets its own separate pair of wires from the source. Sources usually give more voltage and lower noise on their balanced output. The Dawn Pro, for example, is rated at 2Vrms on 3.5mm and 4Vrms on 4.4mm. With an easy-to-drive IEM like the Aria 2 you will not need that extra voltage for loudness, but the lower noise floor can help.

Yes, with the right adapter. Many recent phones have no 3.5mm jack, so you need one of these:

  • A basic USB-C to 3.5mm dongle. These are sold widely on Daraz, including UGREEN ones. Because the Aria 2 is easy to drive, this works fine for everyday use.
  • A USB-C DAC with a 4.4mm port. Swap the Aria 2's plug to 4.4mm and connect to something like the Moondrop Dawn Pro, which is rated at 4Vrms on its 4.4mm output. This gives more headroom and a lower noise floor.
  • A laptop or PC headphone jack. The 3.5mm plug goes straight in.

iPhones with a USB-C port work with USB-C dongles. iPhones with a Lightning port need a Lightning to 3.5mm adapter, or a USB-C DAC plus a Lightning to USB-C cable such as FiiO's LT-LT3, which is listed on Daraz.

One thing to remember: the 4.4mm plug is for sources with a 4.4mm balanced port. For a 3.5mm source, simply swap back to the 3.5mm plug instead of using an adapter.

How does customs work for a pair of IEMs?

Earphones are electronics, and Pakistan has no meaningful duty-free threshold for commercial parcels. Customs duty depends on the HS code, and sales tax of 18% applies on top, plus other levies. Combined, these commonly come to 25% to 60% of the customs value, but rates change with budgets and SROs. Wired IEMs have no battery, so there are no air shipping restrictions to worry about. What should you check when the Aria 2 arrives?

  1. The box contents. Earphones, cable, 3.5mm plug, 4.4mm plug, ear tips, storage case and manual should all be there.
  2. The model name. The box and the shell should say Aria 2, not the original Aria.
  3. Both channels. Play a stereo track and confirm sound from left and right at equal volume.
  4. The 2-pin connection. Pins should seat firmly. Note the polarity marking so you plug each side back in the same way.
  5. The modular plug. Screw the plug on fully. A loose thread causes crackles that sound like a fault.
  6. Fit. Try each tip size. Most complaints about sound come from a poor seal, not from the earphones.

Common mistakes when buying the Aria 2

  • Ordering the original Aria by accident because the listing title says only "Moondrop Aria".
  • Expecting a microphone. The Aria 2 has none in the box, so you need a third-party mic cable for calls.
  • Buying an expensive amplifier you do not need. With 122dB/Vrms sensitivity, a decent dongle is enough.
  • Leaving the 4.4mm plug on and using an adapter for a 3.5mm source, instead of swapping to the included 3.5mm plug.
  • Judging the sound with the wrong tip size before getting a good seal.
