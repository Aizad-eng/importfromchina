Importing Car and Bike Spare Parts From China to Pakistan

By importfromchina.shop teamUpdated October 3, 20267 min read
Quick answer

Yes, you can import a single car or bike spare part from China to Pakistan, and for Chinese-brand vehicles or discontinued parts it is often the only option. Success depends on the OEM part number, confirming fitment for a right-hand-drive vehicle, and avoiding counterfeit branded parts, which customs seizes. Small parts ship cheaply by air; heavy or bulky parts such as bumpers, panels and rims can cost more in freight than they are worth.

Can I import one spare part from China to Pakistan?

Yes. Owners of Chinese cars such as MG, Changan, Haval and Chery, riders of Chinese motorcycles, and anyone with an older Japanese car whose parts are no longer stocked locally end up on Chinese platforms. 1688 and Taobao carry OEM-supplier and aftermarket parts at factory prices; Alibaba has the same in English. Importing one part is routine. Getting the right part first time is the whole challenge, because there is no return route.

Start with the part number

The single most important piece of information is the OEM part number. It is printed or stamped on most original parts, listed in the vehicle's parts catalogue, and often available from a dealer's parts desk if you ask. Searching Chinese platforms by part number returns far more accurate results than searching by description, and it lets the seller confirm fitment. Also gather the vehicle's VIN, model year, engine code and variant, because many parts changed mid-generation. A photo of the old part, with any numbers visible, resolves most doubts.

OEM, OEM-supplier or aftermarket?

Listings use these words loosely:

  • Genuine OEM is the part in the vehicle brand's own box. It is the most expensive and, for foreign brands, the most likely to be faked on Chinese platforms.
  • OEM-supplier is the same factory that makes the part for the brand, selling it without the brand logo. Often the best value, if the seller is genuine.
  • Aftermarket is a third-party copy. Quality ranges from excellent to dangerous. For brakes, steering and suspension, prefer known aftermarket brands over unbranded parts.

Avoid listings showing a Toyota, Honda, Suzuki or other brand logo at a fraction of the dealer price. Those are counterfeits, and customs seizes them.

The left-hand-drive trap

China drives on the right with left-hand-drive vehicles. Pakistan drives on the left with right-hand-drive vehicles. Many parts are mirrored between the two: headlights (beam pattern), wiper arms, side mirrors, dashboard trims, some steering components and interior panels. A Chinese seller will send the LHD version unless you say otherwise, and the RHD version may not exist for that model on the Chinese market. For any part that has a left and a right or that relates to the driving position, confirm RHD fitment explicitly before buying.

What does customs look for?

Auto parts have their own HS codes and are often in the higher duty slabs. On top of customs duty you pay additional customs duty, sales tax at the standard rate and advance income tax, on the customs value including freight. Valuation rulings apply to many parts, so customs may assess a minimum value. Combined taxes commonly fall in the 25 to 60 percent range of customs value, some parts above it, and change with budgets, so confirm the current rate. Beyond the tax, customs looks for counterfeit branding, used parts, which are restricted in many categories, and hazardous items. Read the customs duty guide for the calculation.

Some items in this category are also refused by air carriers or need special routes. Airbag modules and seatbelt pretensioners contain explosive charges. Lead-acid and lithium vehicle batteries are restricted. Brake fluid, oils, coolant, paint and aerosols are flammable liquids. Shock absorbers filled with gas can be refused. Say so when asking for a price if your part is in one of these groups; it changes the route or makes a single piece impractical.

Heavy and bulky parts

Air freight is priced by weight or by volumetric weight, whichever is higher, and volumetric weight is length by width by height in centimetres divided by 6000. A bumper, bonnet, door, exhaust system or set of alloy wheels is light for its size, so it is charged on volume and the freight can exceed the part's price. Sea freight is priced per cubic metre with a practical minimum of around one cubic metre and fixed port and documentation fees, so it does not work for one part. Single-part imports therefore suit small and medium parts: sensors, ECUs, pumps, bearings, lights, mirrors, clutch kits, brake components, trim, filters and motorcycle engine parts. For a bumper or panel, a local body shop usually wins.

What commonly goes wrong?

  • Wrong part number or variant. The part is for the facelift model or the other engine. There is no exchange route.
  • LHD part arrives. The mirror or headlight is for the wrong side of the car.
  • Counterfeit seized. A branded part at a suspicious price never arrives.
  • Transit damage. Lights and plastic trims crack without padding.
  • Freight shock. A large light part turns out to cost more to fly than to buy.

Frequently asked questions

Can I import a single car part from China to Pakistan?

Yes. One part is a normal import. You pay duty and taxes on the landed value, and the main risks are a wrong part number, a left-hand-drive version and counterfeit branded parts.

How do I make sure a part from China fits my car?

Use the OEM part number, give the seller your VIN, model year and engine code, and send a photo of the old part. For anything with a left and right, confirm right-hand-drive fitment explicitly.

Will customs seize fake branded car parts?

Yes. Parts carrying a vehicle brand's logo at a fraction of the dealer price are counterfeit and are seized without compensation. Buy OEM-supplier or known aftermarket brands instead.

Is it worth importing a bumper or body panel from China?

Rarely for a single piece. Air freight is charged on volume for large light parts and often exceeds the part's price. Sea freight is uneconomical below about one cubic metre.

Can car batteries, airbags or brake fluid be shipped from China by air?

Generally not on standard air routes. Batteries, airbag modules and flammable fluids are restricted and need special handling, which is impractical for a single part.
