What is the Truthear Hexa and who is it for?

The Hexa is a wired in-ear monitor from Truthear, a Chinese brand. It is a hybrid design: one dynamic driver handles the low end and three balanced armature drivers cover the rest. That is the main reason people look for it. It is the common next step for someone who has tried a single-driver IEM and wants more separation between instruments.

It suits you if you:

  • Listen to wired audio on a phone, laptop or PC and do not need a microphone.
  • Own a single-driver set such as a Truthear Gate or Moondrop Chu-series and want a step up.
  • Have average or larger ears. Reviewers consistently mention fit as a problem for smaller ears.

It does not suit you if you need calls, wireless, or a very small earpiece.

Hybrid or single dynamic driver: which suits you?

A dynamic driver is a small speaker cone, much like a tiny loudspeaker, and is usually good at bass. A balanced armature is a tiny, fast driver originally made for hearing aids. Hybrids such as the Hexa give different frequency ranges to different drivers, and a crossover inside the shell splits the signal between them.

There is no single right answer, and more drivers do not automatically mean better sound. Tuning matters as much as driver count. If your current earphones sound muddy when many instruments play together, a hybrid is worth trying. If you are happy with a single-driver set and mainly want better build or comfort, a premium single-driver IEM may be the better spend.

Is the Hexa hard to drive?

No. At 20.5 ohms and 120dB/Vrms it reaches normal volume easily. SoundGuys concluded that a basic Apple or Google dongle is enough and a USB-C DAC or amp is not required. A better DAC can lower background hiss, which balanced armature drivers sometimes reveal on noisy sources, but it is optional. If you do want one, our guide to the FiiO KA17 covers a higher-powered option.

A practical tip for sensitive IEMs like this one: if you hear a faint hiss in quiet passages, it usually comes from the source, not the earphones. Try another dongle or a laptop's headphone jack before assuming a fault. Also keep your phone's media volume moderate. With 120dB/Vrms sensitivity, the Hexa gets loud well before the volume bar reaches the top, and long sessions at high volume are the real risk to your hearing.

The stock cable ends in a 3.5mm plug, according to SoundGuys, so it plugs straight into a laptop, a PC front-panel jack or any USB-C to 3.5mm adapter. It is not designed for wireless use, and there is no Bluetooth module for it in the box.

Which version should you pick, and what should you avoid?

Truthear's page lists one version: no colour choices, no plug choices and no microphone version. SoundGuys also notes it has no microphone. So the choices come down to the seller and the cable:

  • Pick the plain Hexa. Listings titled "Hexa" with "1DD + 3BA" in the description are the correct model.
  • Do not confuse it with other Truthear models. Truthear also sells the Gate, Zero:Red, Pure and Nova. They have different driver layouts and tuning.
  • Want a mic or 4.4mm? Order the standard Hexa and add a separate 0.78mm 2-pin cable with the termination you need. SoundGuys notes the cable can be swapped for any cable with matching connectors.
  • Know the socket. Truthear describes a sunken 0.78mm 2-pin socket. When buying a replacement cable, ask the seller whether it suits recessed 2-pin sockets.

Will the Truthear Hexa fit my ears?

This is the main risk with the Hexa. SoundGuys lists "smaller ears won't fit" as a drawback, and the cable is designed to hook over the ear. Truthear includes two kinds of silicone tips, thick-bore and thin-bore, in three sizes each, plus one pair of foam tips. The tips change both comfort and how deep the nozzle sits. Foam tips expand to fill the ear canal and often give the best seal for people between sizes. If you have had trouble with larger earphones before, start with the smallest thin-bore tips and wear them over the ear as intended. If fit is a real worry, a smaller single-driver IEM sold locally might be a safer first purchase.

What should you check when the Hexa arrives?

  1. Box contents. Earphones, four-strand cable, three pairs of thick-bore tips, three pairs of thin-bore tips, one pair of foam tips, manual and warranty card.
  2. Both sides play evenly. Use a mono track or a centred vocal. The voice should sit in the middle of your head.

Common mistakes when buying the Hexa

  • Assuming it has a mic. It does not, so plan a mic cable if you take calls.
  • Buying a replacement cable without asking whether it suits a recessed 2-pin socket.
  • Ignoring fit warnings if you have small ears.
  • Spending on a high-power amp. The Hexa needs very little power. Spend on a clean, low-noise source instead.
  • Ordering from a listing that does not say "1DD + 3BA" and ending up with a different Truthear model.
