Is It Legal to Import From China to Pakistan?

By Muhammad Shamshad AslamUpdated November 12, 20268 min read
Quick answer

Yes, it is legal to import from China to Pakistan. Goods not banned or restricted under the Import Policy Order 2022 can come in once duties and taxes are paid. An individual can receive a personal parcel on a CNIC, while importing goods to sell needs an NTN and a Pakistan Single Window account. Counterfeits and some regulated goods are not allowed.

New to importing? Read our step-by-step guide on how to import from China to Pakistan.

Is it legal to import from China to Pakistan?

Yes. Importing from China is as legal as importing from any other country. The basic rule in Pakistan's Import Policy Order is that goods may be imported from any source unless the Order bans or restricts them. Nothing in Pakistani law treats a parcel from China differently from a parcel from Dubai or the UK.

What makes an import legal is not the country. It is three things: the goods themselves are allowed, any permit the goods need is in place, and the import is declared to customs with the right value and the duty and taxes paid. Get those three right and the import is legal whether it is one phone case or a container of machinery.

This guide is only about the legal side. If you want the full process from finding a product to delivery, start with our guide on how to import from China to Pakistan.

Which laws control imports into Pakistan?

Two laws decide most of it: the Import Policy Order, which says what may come in, and the Customs Act 1969, which says how it is declared and taxed. Other agencies add their own rules for particular goods.

Can an individual import from China without a company or NTN?

Yes. An individual can legally receive goods from China for personal use on a CNIC, with the courier, Pakistan Post or a service filing the customs declaration. No company, import licence or NTN is needed for that.

Registration becomes necessary when you import commercially in your own name, meaning goods to sell or regular imports for a business. A commercial importer files declarations on Pakistan Single Window (PSW). PSW's subscription page lists an NTN, FTN or STRN, a CNIC, a SIM registered in your own name, the email in your FBR IRIS record, biometric verification at a NADRA e-Sahulat centre and a one-time fee of Rs 500, when we checked in November 2026. Our guide on whether you need an NTN to import one item covers this in detail.

The line between personal and commercial matters legally. FBR's travel guide defines a commercial quantity as goods imported prima facie for trading or gain and not for personal use or gift. It sets no fixed number, so customs judges by quantity, type and invoice.

What makes an import from China illegal?

An import becomes illegal when the goods are prohibited, when a required permit is missing, or when the declaration is false. These are the common ways it happens:

  • Counterfeit branded goods. Section 15 of the Customs Act prohibits goods with a counterfeit trade mark or false trade description. These cannot be released by paying duty. Customs can detain and seize them.
  • Banned goods. Items on the Import Policy Order's banned list cannot come in at all.
  • Restricted goods without the permit. A medicine without DRAP approval, or a drone without permission, is held at customs.
  • Under-declaring the value. Asking a seller to write a lower value on the invoice is misdeclaration, which is an offence. Customs also uses valuation rulings and its own data, so it often reassesses anyway.
  • Commercial goods passed off as personal. FBR's travel guide says goods brought in commercial quantities are released only on payment of a fine equal to 30% of their value, in addition to duty and taxes.
  • Bypassing customs. Goods brought in without declaration, or through someone who promises no customs at all, are smuggled goods, whatever they cost.

Is it legal to buy from Taobao, 1688, Temu or AliExpress?

Yes. The platform you buy from does not affect legality. A product bought on Taobao and shipped through an agent is treated the same as one bought on AliExpress and posted to your door. Customs looks at what the goods are, what they are worth and who is receiving them.

The practical difference is delivery. Taobao and 1688 sellers ship only within China, so someone there must receive the item and send it on. That is legal, and it is how buying agents and single-item services work. Temu and AliExpress ship to Pakistan directly. ProPakistani reported in July 2025 that AliExpress had stopped offering its budget routes, such as AliExpress Standard Shipping and Cainiao, for Pakistan, so check what checkout shows for your address.

Is it legal to pay a Chinese seller from Pakistan?

Yes, through proper channels. A Pakistani debit or credit card with international use enabled can legally pay on a Chinese site, and banks collect an advance income tax on foreign card payments under section 236Y of the Income Tax Ordinance. Your bank shows the current rate.

For a commercial import in your own name, payment goes through your bank and is linked to your declaration on PSW. Paying a Pakistani agent or service in rupees, who then pays the seller, is also legal. What is risky, though not always illegal, is paying an unknown person through an informal channel with no record. If the deal goes wrong you have no proof and no protection.

Do you have to pay duty for an import to be legal?

Yes, for anything above a very small value. Section 19C of the Customs Act, as amended by the Finance Act 2025, says no duties and taxes are demanded on goods imported through post or courier worth no more than Rs 1,000. Above that, the whole value is dutiable.

When we checked in November 2026, customs duty ran in slabs of 0, 5, 10, 15 and 20%, with higher rates on some goods, followed by sales tax at 18% on the duty-paid value. Regulatory duty applies only to listed goods and was capped at 20% from 1 July 2026 under SRO 1064(I)/2026. Our customs duty guide shows how the total is built.

Paying the duty a customs officer assesses is the legal part. Whether the amount feels fair is a separate question, and the answer is a formal objection, not avoiding the declaration.

What happens if customs finds a problem with your import?

It depends on the problem. Prohibited goods, such as counterfeits, are detained and can be seized, and paying duty does not release them. Goods missing a permit are held until the permit is produced or the goods are returned. A value customs disagrees with is reassessed, and you pay the higher duty to release the parcel.

For a personal parcel, the courier usually contacts you to supply documents or pay what customs assessed. If you ignore that, the parcel may be returned or abandoned. Acting quickly with the invoice and proof of payment solves most holds.
