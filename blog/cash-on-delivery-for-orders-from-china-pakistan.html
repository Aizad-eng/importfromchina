Can You Pay Cash on Delivery for Orders From China?

By Muhammad Shamshad AslamUpdated October 23, 20268 min read
Quick answer

No, not in any way you can rely on. When we checked in October 2026, we found no cash on delivery option for Pakistan on Temu or Shein, and a July 2026 buying guide says it is rarely available on cross-border AliExpress orders. The seller in China has to be paid before it dispatches, so cross-border orders are almost always paid upfront.

Can you pay cash on delivery for an order from China?

Usually not. Cash on delivery is normal for shopping inside Pakistan, but for an order shipped from China it is rare enough that you should plan as if it does not exist. When we checked the big platforms in October 2026, neither Temu nor Shein listed cash on delivery as a way to pay from Pakistan, and AliExpress offers it rarely, if at all, on cross-border orders.

The rest of this guide explains why, what each platform does offer instead, and how to tell a genuine way of paying from an offer that only uses the words cash on delivery to win your trust.

Why is cash on delivery so rare on cross-border orders?

Because the money and the goods move in the wrong order. With cash on delivery, the seller sends the goods first and gets paid only when the courier collects cash at your door. That works when the seller, the courier and the buyer are all in one country. It breaks down across borders for several reasons:

  • The seller carries all the risk. A Chinese seller would pay for the item, international shipping and customs handling before knowing whether you will pay. If you refuse the parcel, sending it back to China often costs more than the item.
  • Cash has to cross a border. Rupees collected in Pakistan must be converted and sent to China. That needs a local partner who holds the cash, reconciles it and remits it, which adds cost.
  • Customs sits in the middle. Parcels clear customs in Pakistan before delivery. Somebody has to pay any duty before the courier can release the parcel, so a refused parcel can leave duty paid on goods nobody wants.
  • Platforms prefer to hold the money. Most large marketplaces collect payment at checkout and hold it until delivery is confirmed. That protects buyers through a refund process, and it protects sellers without cash on delivery.

On the cross-border sites we checked in October 2026, cash on delivery was not offered for Pakistani addresses.

Does Temu offer cash on delivery in Pakistan?

We found no sign of it. When we opened Temu's Pakistan pages in October 2026, the accepted payment logos were cards, including Visa, Mastercard, American Express and UnionPay, plus Apple Pay and Google Pay. Cash on delivery was not among them. Temu's Pakistan terms of use describe providing a valid payment method, such as a credit card, and authorising Temu to charge it. They do not mention cash on delivery.

The same terms say that, where applicable, you act as the importer, and that duties collected at the time of purchase are estimates. If customs assesses more, you are responsible for the extra. So even a fully prepaid Temu order can involve a payment later, which is tax, not cash on delivery for the item. Our guide on whether Temu delivers to Pakistan covers the wallet options, delivery times and returns.

Does Shein offer cash on delivery in Pakistan?

Not on the payment page we checked. Shein's global How to Pay page, opened in October 2026, has sections for cards, online bank transfer, several regional wallets, and separate sections for JazzCash and Easypaisa. There was no cash on delivery section. For JazzCash, the page says you select it on the order page, then open your JazzCash wallet, go to payment requests and enter your MPIN to approve the payment.

So Shein is prepaid too, but it is one of the easier sites to pay from Pakistan because a local wallet works. Our guide on how to buy from Shein in Pakistan walks through sizing, shipping and returns.

Does AliExpress offer cash on delivery to Pakistan?

Rarely, and not in a way you should plan around. A July 2026 AliExpress buying guide for South Asia from the payments company WorldFirst says cash on delivery is rarely available on cross-border AliExpress orders. The same guide calls a Visa or Mastercard debit or credit card the most reliable way for Pakistani buyers to pay, and says JazzCash and Easypaisa are not directly supported at AliExpress checkout.

If a listing appears to offer cash on delivery to Pakistan, read the checkout carefully before you rely on it. What counts is the payment step for your own delivery address, not a banner on the product page.

What about buying locally with cash on delivery?

If paying at the door matters more to you than price or range, buy from stock that is already in Pakistan. Daraz lists cash on delivery among its payment methods. Its returns and refunds FAQ says refunds for cash on delivery orders go to your Daraz Wallet, with a stated refund time of 30 minutes, so the money comes back as wallet credit rather than cash.

The trade-off is choice. A specific model, a niche part or an item sold only on Chinese sites is often not available locally at all. Our comparison of Temu and Daraz sets out where each one wins.

Is the customs charge at your door the same as cash on delivery?

No. When a parcel you ordered yourself arrives by courier, the courier may ask you to pay the duty and taxes customs assessed before it hands the parcel over. DHL Pakistan's import guide, for example, says it sends an email or SMS if customs requires tax payments or more documents. That money goes to customs, not to the seller. You have already paid for the item, and you pay the tax on top.

This catches people out with prepaid orders. A parcel worth more than Rs 1,000 sent by post or courier can attract duty and taxes, and the checkout price on a foreign site may not include all of it.

How do you spot a fake cash on delivery offer for a China item?

Be careful with anyone who promises cash on delivery for an item coming from China and then asks for money first. Common warning signs:

  • A booking fee, advance or deposit to a personal account before the so-called cash on delivery order ships.
  • A page that uses Temu, Shein or AliExpress names or logos but takes payment outside the official app or website.
  • No written refund policy and no way to check who you are dealing with.
  • A price that leaves out shipping or customs, with the rest to be collected later.
  • Pressure to pay quickly because the offer is about to end.

Paying upfront is normal for cross-border orders. What makes it safe is knowing exactly what you are paying for, who you are paying, and what happens if the item does not arrive.

Common mistakes when looking for cash on delivery from China

  • Trusting a cash on delivery badge on social media for an item that ships from China.
  • Paying a deposit to a personal account to secure a cash on delivery order.
  • Thinking a prepaid order means nothing more to pay. Duty can still be due, depending on the site's terms and the parcel's value.
  • Refusing a parcel at the door to avoid duty. The item is usually lost, and getting a refund for it is hard.
  • Choosing a worse product just to get cash on delivery, when a prepaid route with a clear refund policy would get you the exact item.
