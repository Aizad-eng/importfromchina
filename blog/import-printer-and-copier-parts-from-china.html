Importing Printer and Copier Parts From China to Pakistan

By Muhammad Shamshad AslamUpdated November 12, 20269 min read
Quick answer

Yes, single printer and copier parts can be imported from China to Pakistan, and fusers, formatter boards, drums, rollers and printheads are common orders. The biggest trap is voltage. HP sells separate 110V and 220V fuser kits, and Pakistan runs at 230V, so only the 220V version is right here. Match the exact part number and printer model before you order.

New to importing? Read our step-by-step guide on how to import from China to Pakistan.

Which printer and copier parts are worth importing from China?

The parts worth importing are the ones that fail often, cost a lot locally, or are simply not stocked: fuser units and fuser film sleeves, formatter boards and main boards, drum units, pickup and separation rollers, transfer belts, printheads for inkjet printers, and laser scanner units. China has a large printer parts industry, including original parts taken from used machines and new compatible parts.

Small parts with a clear part number travel well. A whole office copier is heavy and bulky and usually better bought locally. If you are new to buying from China as an individual, start with our guide on how to import from China to Pakistan.

Why does fuser voltage matter in Pakistan?

A laser printer's fuser heats the toner onto the paper, and its heating element is built for one voltage range. Pakistan's mains supply is 230 V at 50 Hz, so you need the 220 V version. A 110 V fuser on a 230 V supply is wrong and should not be installed.

HP's own product pages show how clearly this is split. When we checked in November 2026, HP listed the LaserJet 220V Fuser Kit 4YL17A for the Color LaserJet Enterprise M856dn, the MFP M776dn and the Flow MFP M776z and M776zs. A separate LaserJet 110V Fuser Kit, 4YL16A, is sold for 110 V markets. Many Chinese sellers list both versions on the same page, sometimes with a voltage selector that is easy to miss.

When you order a fuser, film sleeve or heating element:

  • Pick the 220 V option, and make sure it is the selected variant before you copy the link.
  • Check the part number against the label on your old fuser.
  • Ask the seller to confirm the voltage in writing.

How do you find the right part number?

Take the number from the old part, the printer's service documentation or the maker's support pages, then search for that number. Printer parts often fit several models and carry two numbers: a kit number and the number of the unit inside it.

  1. Write the printer model and series exactly. Include letters such as dn, dw or MFP, because these change parts.
  2. Read the label on the failed part. Fusers, boards and drums usually carry a printed number.
  3. Check the maker's page for compatibility. HP's supply pages list compatible printers, as with the 4YL17A above.
  4. Search that number on Taobao, 1688, AliExpress or Alibaba, and compare the listings' compatible models with yours.
  5. Ask the seller to confirm fit for your exact model.

Will compatible toner and ink cartridges work in your printer?

Often, but not always, and firmware can change that. In a 2016 newsroom post, HP explained that a firmware update had added a dynamic security feature that stopped some third-party cartridges without an original HP security chip from working, including some that had worked before. HP said cartridges with original HP security chips continued to work, and it released an optional firmware update that removed the feature for affected printers.

The practical points for a buyer in Pakistan:

  • Compatible cartridges are sold with their own chips. Ask the seller which printer firmware versions they work with.
  • Think before you update printer firmware if you rely on compatible cartridges.
  • Genuine cartridges from a different market can also be a problem on some models, because some makers sell regional versions. Check the cartridge's region or market with the seller.

Single cartridges are usually cheap enough that shipping takes a big share of the cost, so they make most sense combined with a larger part.

What about formatter boards, main boards and firmware?

Boards are the riskiest parts, because the same board can carry different firmware or settings. A formatter or main board from a used machine may hold the original machine's configuration. A new board may need setting up for your model.

  • Copy every number on your old board, not just the printer model.
  • Ask the seller whether the board is new, refurbished or taken from a used machine.
  • Ask whether it ships with firmware for your model and market.
  • Plan for a technician who knows the model, if the board needs configuring.

Which printer items ship easily and which do not?

Sealed parts ship easily, while liquids and heavy units do not. Rollers, boards, printheads in sealed packs, drums and fusers are fine by air. Bottled ink and refill liquids can be refused for air shipping, so ask before ordering. A whole copier or a large paper tray unit is heavy, and air couriers charge the larger of actual and volumetric weight, with DHL dividing length, width and height in centimetres by 5,000.

Shipping a normal parcel from China usually costs around Rs 5,000 to 20,000 depending on size and type, so a part worth around Rs 10,000 or more is where single imports start to make sense.

What duty and tax applies to printer parts?

Parcels worth Rs 1,000 or less pay no duties or taxes under section 19C of the Customs Act. Above that, customs assesses the whole value by tariff code. In FY 2026-27 customs duty is charged in slabs of 0, 5, 10, 15 and 20 percent, with some goods higher. Additional customs duty is 0 on the 0 to 15 percent slabs and 2 percent on the 20 percent slab, regulatory duty applies only to listed goods, and sales tax is 18 percent on the value plus duties, with withholding income tax on top.

What should you check when a printer part arrives?

Check the voltage and the part number first. A fuser should show its voltage on the label, and it should read 220 V or a 220 to 240 V range. Compare the part number and the connector positions with your old part before fitting it.

  • Switch the printer off and let an old fuser cool fully before removing it.
  • Handle boards by the edges and keep them in their anti-static bag until fitting.
  • Remove every strip of protective tape and packing from drums and rollers.
  • Print a configuration page after fitting, so you have a record of the firmware version and page count.
  • Keep the packaging until you are sure the part works properly.

If the part is the wrong version or arrives damaged, report it straight away with the details and the label numbers.

What are the common mistakes with printer parts?

  • Ordering a 110 V fuser because it was the default variant on the listing.
  • Matching by printer series instead of the exact model and part number.
  • Buying a used board without asking about firmware or settings.
  • Updating firmware right after fitting compatible cartridges.
  • Shipping one cheap cartridge where shipping costs more than the cartridge.
  • Ordering bottled ink for air shipping without asking first.

Frequently asked questions

Can I import one printer part from China to Pakistan?

Yes. Fusers, boards, rollers, drums and printheads are practical single imports, especially when the part is worth around Rs 10,000 or more.

Which fuser voltage do I need in Pakistan?

The 220 V version. Pakistan's supply is 230 V at 50 Hz, and makers such as HP sell separate 110 V and 220 V fuser kits.

Will compatible cartridges from China work in my HP printer?

Often, but firmware can affect it. HP has used dynamic security that blocks cartridges without an original HP chip, so ask the seller which firmware versions their cartridge supports.

Is a used original board a good idea?

It can be for older models, but it may carry the previous machine's settings or firmware. Ask the seller about its source and firmware first.

Can bottled ink be shipped by air?

Liquids can be refused for air shipping, so ask before ordering bottled ink or refill kits. Sealed cartridges and printheads usually ship without trouble.

Can you import toner cartridges in quantity?

No. We only handle single items or a few different single items. For quantities, use a cargo company or the commercial import route.
