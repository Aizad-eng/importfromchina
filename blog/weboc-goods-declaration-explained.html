WeBOC and the Goods Declaration (GD) Explained

By Muhammad Shamshad AslamUpdated November 12, 20269 min read
Quick answer

WeBOC (Web Based One Customs) is Pakistan Customs' online clearance system, and the goods declaration (GD) is the form in it that declares an import's value, tariff code and duties. Commercial importers or their customs agents file the GD, now often as a Single Declaration on PSW, pay against a PSID, and the system routes it to a green, yellow or red channel.

What is WeBOC?

WeBOC, short for Web Based One Customs, is the computerised system Pakistan Customs uses to clear imports and exports. FBR describes it as developed jointly by Pakistan Customs and PRAL, FBR's IT company. Its own feature list includes online manifest filing, online payments, goods declaration filing at any hour of the day, and a risk management system with green, yellow and red channels. If you are working out the whole route first, read our guide on how to import from China to Pakistan.

What is a goods declaration?

A goods declaration, or GD, is the importer's formal statement to customs about a shipment: what the goods are, their tariff code, their value, the duties and taxes due, and the documents behind them. Customs assesses the GD, the duties are paid against it, and the goods are released on it. Every import has one, whether you file it yourself or a courier files it for you.

The declaration is the importer's own statement, and customs checks it rather than writing it. That is why the accuracy of the description, code and value sits with whoever files it, and why a mistake on the GD leads to reassessment rather than a quiet correction.

A GD also creates a record. The GD number and date are what customs, banks and other agencies use to find your shipment later, and some rules count time from them. For example, Dawn reported that the January 2026 vehicle amendment counts the waiting period from the GD date of the last import.

How is WeBOC related to Pakistan Single Window?

WeBOC is still the core customs system, and PSW is the trader-facing portal connected to it. PSW's WeBOC to PSW page says WeBOC remains the customs management system and is one of the systems integrated into PSW, that all functions traders had in WeBOC are available in PSW, and that PSW is the system for licences, permits and certificates from other agencies. Two points follow from that page:

  • Where an import needs an LPCO from a regulator, filing through PSW's Single Declaration is mandatory.
  • Where it does not, the trader may file through either system. PSW also supports filing before the cargo arrives, while most WeBOC GDs are filed after arrival.

So the "GD" in WeBOC and the "Single Declaration" in PSW are two routes to the same thing. Our PSW guide covers subscription and bank linking.

Who can file a goods declaration?

A registered importer or a licensed customs agent acting for one. To file in your own name you need a PSW subscription, which PSW says requires an NTN, FTN or STRN, a SIM in your name, the email from your IRIS record, a one-time Rs 500 fee and NADRA biometric verification, plus customs registration from inside the portal. PSW's Single Declaration page adds a second prerequisite: your banking profile must be integrated with PSW.

For a courier parcel, the courier or its clearing agent files the declaration under its own registration. You do not need a login. Many first-time commercial importers also use a licensed customs agent for their first shipments, because the agent already knows which codes and documents customs at that port expects.

What goes into a goods declaration?

A goods declaration brings together consignment details, commodity lines, payment details and documents. PSW's page for the import Single Declaration describes the steps in this order:

SectionWhat you enter
Declaration typeConsignment category and declaration type, chosen when you create it
Consignment informationFields that vary by collectorate and mode, such as air or sea
Financial instrumentCurrency, delivery term, bank, IBAN, mode of payment, instrument number, invoice number and date
Commodity informationEach item line, with its description, tariff code, quantity and value
DocumentsUploads by document type, such as the invoice, packing list, transport document and permits
PaymentA summary of duties and taxes and a system-generated PSID

The information has to match the paper trail. Our guide to the documents required to import from China lists each supporting document and who issues it.

What changed for import manifests in August 2026?

From 15 August 2026, the importer's tax number has to appear on the carrier's manifest as well as on the GD. ProPakistani reported in June 2026 that under SRO 918(I)/2026 FBR updated the Import General Manifest format to make the importer's NTN or FTN mandatory, with a CNIC or passport number accepted for small parcel importers and one-time importers without an NTN. Give your forwarder the number before the goods ship.

What happens after you file the GD?

WeBOC's risk management system decides how closely customs looks at it. FBR's WeBOC page defines the three channels:

  • Green: cleared without intervention.
  • Yellow: cleared on the basis of the uploaded documents.
  • Red: treated as higher risk, with physical examination of the goods.

The same FBR page gives a split of 30% green, 49% yellow and 21% red for import consignments, without saying which period those figures cover. If customs disagrees with your code or value, it reassesses and the duty changes. Our guide to the air cargo clearance process follows a shipment from arrival to release.

How are duties paid on a GD?

Duties and taxes are paid against a Payment Slip ID, or PSID. PSW's Single Declaration page says a confirmation with a system-generated PSID appears when you save and submit the declaration. You pay that PSID through your bank's channels, and the payment is matched to the declaration electronically. Agency fees for permits are paid the same way. The amounts are worked out from the customs value and the rates for your tariff code; our guide on how to check customs duty shows the order.

What should you prepare before a GD is filed?

Prepare the documents and numbers before the goods arrive, because the declaration can only be as accurate as the papers behind it. PSW allows filing before arrival, which saves time only if everything is ready. Have these to hand for your customs agent, or for yourself if you file:

  1. Your PSW user ID, checked to be active, and your NTN and STRN.
  2. The commercial invoice with the real price, currency and price term, and the invoice number and date.
  3. The packing list with cartons, weights and contents.
  4. The bill of lading or airway bill, showing your NTN as consignee.
  5. The financial instrument details from your bank, matching the invoice currency and payment mode.
  6. The eight-digit PCT code for each item line, with a note of why you chose it.
  7. Any LPCO the goods need, such as a PSQCA release order or a DRAP permission, applied for in good time.
  8. A CPFTA certificate of origin, if you are claiming the reduced duty.

If you use a customs agent, they file under their own registration, so there is no reason to share your own PSW login.

What usually goes wrong with a goods declaration?

Most problems are mismatches or missing permissions, not system faults. The common ones:

  • A tariff code customs does not accept, which leads to reassessment and a different duty.
  • A value below a valuation ruling, which customs replaces with the ruling value.
  • No linked bank profile, so no financial instrument can be attached.
  • A missing LPCO for a restricted item, so the goods cannot be released on payment alone.
  • A consignee name or number on the manifest that does not match the GD.
  • A lapsed PSW user ID. PSW says IDs last two years.

Do you need WeBOC for one parcel from China?

No. For one item for personal use, the courier or service clears it and identifies you by CNIC. Post and courier parcels worth Rs 1,000 or less pay no duties or taxes under section 19C of the Customs Act. Frequently asked questions

What is WeBOC in Pakistan?

Web Based One Customs, the online clearance system developed by Pakistan Customs and PRAL. It handles manifests, goods declarations, assessment and payments.

What is a GD in customs?

A goods declaration: the importer's formal statement of what is being imported, its tariff code, value and duties. Customs assesses and releases goods against it.

Is PSW replacing WeBOC?

Not exactly. PSW says WeBOC remains the customs management system integrated into PSW, while PSW is the main trader portal and is mandatory where a regulator's permit applies.

What do green, yellow and red channels mean?

Green is cleared without intervention, yellow is cleared on uploaded documents, and red means physical examination of the goods.

How do I pay duty on a goods declaration?

Submitting the declaration generates a PSID. You pay that PSID through your bank, and the payment is matched to the declaration.

Do I need a WeBOC login to receive a parcel?

No. The courier or service files the declaration for a personal parcel under its own registration and identifies you by CNIC.
