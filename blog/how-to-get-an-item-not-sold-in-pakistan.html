For items worth around Rs 10,000 or more, a single-item import service is usually the simplest route.

Is the item really not sold in Pakistan?

Before you import anything, spend ten minutes proving that nobody here sells it. A lot of products that seem unavailable are on sale in Pakistan under a different name, a different model number or by a seller who does not show up in Google.

  • Search the exact model number, not the product name. A search for a wireless lavalier mic returns hundreds of results. A search for the exact model code returns the one you want, or nothing. If the brand sells several versions, search each one.
  • Search Daraz with filters off. Sort by newest as well as by relevance, because small sellers with one listing sink to the bottom.
  • Ask in the right local market. Electronics, tools, car parts and hobby gear each have their own markets in Karachi, Lahore and Rawalpindi. A shopkeeper who does not stock it will often know who does, or who can order it.
  • Check Instagram and Facebook sellers. Many importers of niche gadgets sell only through social media pages and Marketplace. Search the model number in their posts.
  • Look for an official distributor. Visit the brand's own website and look for a store locator or a Pakistan distributor. If one exists, you get a local warranty, which is worth paying a little more for.

If all of that comes back empty, or the only local listing is the wrong colour, size or version, you have a genuine case for importing.

Why are some products never sold in Pakistan?

Knowing the reason helps you judge whether importing is sensible or whether you are about to hit a wall.

  • The market is too small for a distributor. Specialist tools, lab and medical accessories, hobby electronics and replacement parts for less common machines rarely sell in enough volume for anyone to stock them. These are usually fine to import.
  • The item needs approval here. Phones and other devices with a SIM need PTA registration. Medicines and many supplements need DRAP. Drones need prior permission. Sellers avoid these, and so should you unless you understand the rules.
  • It is a China-market version. Some brands sell a model only in China, often with a Chinese-language interface or app. It may still work here, but check the language settings and app support first.
  • It is new. Many gadgets reach Chinese platforms months before any Pakistani seller imports them.
  • Shipping it is awkward. Loose lithium batteries, power banks, aerosols and flammable liquids are restricted by air, so local importers leave them out.

Can the item legally come into Pakistan?

This is the question to answer before you spend any money. If the item falls into one of these groups, stop and read the rules first.

How do you find the exact item on a Chinese site?

Finding the item is often harder than buying it. Three methods work well.

  • Search by image. Taobao, 1688 and AliExpress all let you upload a photo and find matching listings. A clear photo of the product on a plain background works best.
  • Search the model number. Chinese listings often include the factory model code even when the title is vague.
  • Search the Chinese name. Put the product name through a translator and search the Chinese words. Results on Taobao and 1688 are much better in Chinese than in English.

When you find it, compare a few listings. The same item is often sold by several sellers at different prices, and the cheapest is not always the same quality. Look at sales numbers, ratings and the photos buyers have posted.

What will it cost to get the item to Pakistan?

The listing price is only the start. For one item, plan for three costs on top of it.

  • Shipping. Shipping one item from China usually costs Rs 5,000 to 20,000, depending on its size and type. Large, light boxes are charged on their volume, and batteries or liquids cost more to fly. Our guide to the cost to ship one item from China to Pakistan explains the maths.
  • Customs. Post and courier parcels worth Rs 1,000 or less are duty-free under section 19C of the Customs Act, as set by the Finance Act 2025. Above that, duty and taxes apply. Rates depend on the product, so see our guide to customs duty from China to Pakistan.
  • A service fee, if someone buys and ships it for you.

Add these together and compare with any local price you found. That total is the landed price, and it is the only number worth comparing.

How do you get one item that isn't sold in Pakistan, step by step?

  1. Confirm it is not sold here. Search the model number on Daraz, in markets and on social media.
  2. Check the rules. Make sure it does not need PTA, DRAP or other permission, and that it can be flown.
  3. Find the exact listing. Use image search or the model number, and pick a well-rated seller.
  4. Check the variant. Confirm the colour, size, plug type and language version before you buy.
  5. Work out the landed price. Item, shipping, customs and any fee, in rupees.
  6. Choose your route. Order yourself where the platform delivers to Pakistan and you can pay, or use a service for Taobao, 1688 and higher-value items.
  7. Keep the paperwork. Save the order page and invoice. Customs or the courier may ask for proof of value.

What mistakes do people make when importing an item they cannot find here?

  • Not searching the model number. People import something that was on sale in a local market all along, at a similar price, with a warranty.
  • Buying the wrong version. China-market, global and US versions can differ in language, plug and app support.
  • Ignoring the rules. A phone that will not register or a supplement held for DRAP approval is money lost.
  • Using a VPN to reach another country's Temu site. The address and payment usually do not match, and checkout fails.
  • Comparing the listing price, not the landed price. Shipping and customs can be more than the item.
  • Importing something cheap. A Rs 2,000 item rarely makes sense once shipping is added.

Frequently asked questions

How can I buy a product that is not available in Pakistan?

Find the same item on a Chinese site using its model number or an image search, check it is allowed into Pakistan, then either order it yourself from a site that delivers here, such as Temu or AliExpress, or use an import service that buys and ships it for you.

How do I know if an item is really not sold anywhere in Pakistan?

Search the exact model number on Daraz, ask in the local market for that type of product, check Instagram and Facebook sellers, and look for an official Pakistan distributor on the brand's website. If all of these come up empty, it is probably not sold here.

Can I order from Taobao or 1688 if the item is only sold there?

Not directly. Taobao and 1688 are built for buyers in China, use Chinese payment methods and do not deliver to Pakistan themselves. You need a buying service or agent that buys it in China and ships it to you.

Is it legal to import a product into Pakistan that local shops don't sell?

Usually yes, for personal use. The exceptions are items that need approval or are banned, such as phones without PTA registration, medicines without DRAP approval, drones without permission, counterfeits and weapon lookalikes.

How much extra will it cost to bring one item from China to Pakistan?

Shipping one item usually costs Rs 5,000 to 20,000 depending on its size and type, plus customs duty if it is worth more than Rs 1,000, plus any service fee. That is why importing one item usually makes sense only from around Rs 10,000 upwards.

Can someone in Pakistan buy an item from China for me and deliver it to my home?

Yes. A single-item import service buys the item in China, ships it by air, clears customs and delivers it to your address. We do this for one item at a time, for one price in rupees that you pay once before it ships.
