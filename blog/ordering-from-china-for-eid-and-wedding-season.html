Ordering From China for Eid and Wedding Season: Deadlines

By Muhammad Shamshad AslamUpdated October 23, 20268 min read
Quick answer

Order at least five to six weeks before the date you need the item, and earlier around Chinese holidays. For Eid al-Fitr 2027, expected on 9 or 10 March, pay by mid January, because Chinese New Year on 6 February 2027 shuts most sellers. For Eid al-Adha 2027, expected on 16 or 17 May, pay by early April. Both Eid dates depend on moon sighting.

How early should you order from China before an event?

Order five to six weeks before the date you need the item in your hands. That covers three things: the seller's dispatch time in China, the shipping itself, and a buffer for the delays that cluster around busy seasons.

Here is how we count it for one item sent by air:

  • Seller dispatch: often a few days for an in-stock listing, longer for made-to-order or customised items. Read the dispatch time on the listing.
  • Shipping and customs: with us, usually 7 to 15 days after the seller dispatches, to anywhere in Pakistan.
  • Buffer: at least a week, for tracking gaps, size exchanges or a late seller. Add more around 11.11 and Chinese New Year.

Four weeks is the realistic minimum for an in-stock item in a quiet month. Six weeks is the comfortable plan. For a day-by-day view of where the time goes, see our import timeline from China to Pakistan.

What are the key dates for 2026 and 2027?

When we checked in October 2026, these were the expected dates. Islamic dates depend on moon sighting by the Central Ruet-e-Hilal Committee in Pakistan, so treat them as estimates and recheck closer to the time.

  • Wedding season: the main season runs through the cooler months from November to February, as reporting on Pakistan's weddings has described it.
  • Chinese New Year 2027: Saturday, 6 February 2027, the start of the Year of the Goat. The Lantern Festival that closes the season is on 20 February.
  • Ramadan 2027: expected to begin around Monday, 8 February 2027, subject to moon sighting.
  • Eid al-Fitr 2027: expected on Tuesday, 9 March or Wednesday, 10 March 2027, depending on the Shawwal moon. These are projections from the International Astronomical Center reported by ProPakistani, and Pakistan's own sighting can differ by a day.
  • Eid al-Adha 2027: expected on Sunday, 16 May or Monday, 17 May 2027, depending on the Dhul Hijjah moon.

Why does Eid al-Fitr 2027 need such an early order?

Because Chinese New Year falls about a month before it. Chinese New Year 2027 is on 6 February, and Ramadan is expected to begin around 8 February, so the Chinese holiday season overlaps almost exactly with the run-up to Eid al-Fitr.

China's statutory Spring Festival holiday was extended to four days from 2025, which with weekend adjustments made an eight-day official break. The real slowdown is longer. Many factories and sellers stop dispatching a week or more before the holiday and restart a week or more after it, so in practice little moves for roughly two to four weeks. China's official 2027 holiday schedule had not been published when we checked in October 2026.

So the safe plan is to pay by mid January 2027, so the seller dispatches before the closures. If you miss that, the item will probably dispatch in the second half of February, and with 7 to 15 days of shipping after that, it may arrive close to Eid or after it. That is a gamble we would not take for an Eid outfit or gift.

What about Eid al-Adha 2027?

Eid al-Adha is easier, because no major Chinese closure falls just before it. With Eid expected on 16 or 17 May 2027, paying by early April gives a comfortable margin, and mid April is the last realistic point for an in-stock item.

One thing to watch is China's May Day holiday. The Labour Day holiday was increased to two statutory days from 2025, usually giving a five-day break around 1 May. If you order in late April, the seller's dispatch may pause for those days. Ordering earlier avoids that.

How does the 11.11 sale affect wedding orders?

It slows everything for a while. 11.11 on 11 November is China's biggest shopping day, and dispatch and freight are usually slower for one to three weeks afterwards. That lands right in the middle of the busiest wedding months.

For a December wedding, the cleanest plan is to pay before 11 November so the seller dispatches before the rush. If you want a sale price on something for a January or February event, buying on 11.11 can still work because you have time to absorb the delay. Our guide to Chinese New Year and 11.11 shipping delays covers the pattern in detail.

What should you order early for wedding season?

Anything that cannot be replaced locally at short notice. Common examples are decorative lighting, specific jewellery or accessories, embroidered fabrics, gifts for the couple, camera and lighting gear for the event, and outfits in a particular style.

Clothes need extra time. Chinese sizes often run small, and an exchange means a second international shipment. Order clothes earlier than other items and check the size chart in centimetres against your own measurements. Our guide to importing clothes and shoes from China explains how to read the charts.

Gadgets make popular wedding and Eid gifts, and most travel without trouble. Devices with a built-in battery, such as earbuds or a smartwatch, usually fly fine, but power banks and loose batteries are commonly restricted by air, so check before you choose one as a gift.

How do you plan an order for an event, step by step?

  1. Fix the date you need it. Use the day before the event, not the event day.
  2. Count back six weeks. That is your comfortable pay-by date. If it falls between mid January and late February 2027, move it before mid January.
  3. Read the listing's dispatch time. Made-to-order items can add a week or more.
  4. Check for holiday notices. Chinese sellers often post a notice on the listing before Chinese New Year saying when they will stop and restart dispatch.
  5. Get a delivered price and date. Ask for an expected delivery date in writing before you pay.
  6. Have a local backup. For anything essential to the day, know where you could buy a substitute in Pakistan if the parcel is late.

Common mistakes when ordering for Eid and weddings

  • Counting from the order date to the event with no buffer. A seven-day tracking gap can wipe out a tight plan.
  • Forgetting Chinese New Year. In 2027 it sits right before Ramadan, so January is the real deadline for Eid al-Fitr.
  • Ordering clothes last. Sizes are the most common reason for a second order. Clothes should be first.
  • Treating Eid dates as fixed. They move by a day with moon sighting. Plan for the earlier date.
  • Ordering items that cannot fly. Perfumes, aerosols and loose lithium batteries are commonly restricted by air, which rules out some popular gift items.
