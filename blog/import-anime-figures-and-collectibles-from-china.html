Importing Anime Figures and Collectibles From China to Pakistan

Quick answer

Yes. A single anime figure, model kit, trading card box or designer toy can be imported from China to Pakistan, and Taobao is the main source because Chinese collectors buy the same licensed releases at domestic prices. The risks are bootlegs, which are poor quality and can be treated as counterfeits by customs, box damage in transit, and long pre-order waits. Duty applies on the landed value. Buy licensed items from official or highly rated stores.

Can I import one figure from China to Pakistan?

Yes. Collectibles are a natural single-item import: they are light, rarely restricted, and the market in Pakistan is tiny, so most releases are never stocked locally. Chinese collectors buy the same licensed figures from Good Smile, Bandai, Kotobukiya, Alter, Max Factory and Chinese studios such as Myethos and APEX, through official Tmall stores and licensed retailers on Taobao, at domestic prices. That makes Taobao the richest source for a Pakistani collector. What you need to manage is authenticity, packaging and timing.

Taobao as the source

Taobao and its brand-store arm Tmall are where Chinese collectors shop. You will find official brand stores, licensed retailers, pre-order specialists and a large second-hand market. The site is in Chinese and takes only Chinese payment, and sellers ship within China only, so a Pakistani buyer needs an agent with a Chinese address to buy and forward the item. Read how to buy from Taobao in Pakistan for how that works. Search in Chinese for better results: the character's name in Chinese plus the maker's name finds listings that an English search misses. Image search in the Taobao app also works well for figures.

Bootleg vs licensed

Bootlegs, unlicensed copies of real figures, are everywhere on Taobao, 1688 and AliExpress. They are cast from the original, so photos look convincing, but paint, seams, joints and plastic quality are much worse and the box is a poor copy. They also infringe the maker's trademarks, so customs can treat them as counterfeit goods and seize them. Signs of a bootleg:

  • A price far below the retail price of the licensed release.
  • Listings that avoid the maker's name or say "domestic version", "mould version" or similar.
  • Stock photos only, no photos of the actual box with the authenticity sticker.
  • A seller who sells hundreds of different figures with no brand store status.

Licensed items come from official Tmall brand stores, licensed retailers with a recognisable history, or second-hand listings with clear photos of the real box and sticker. Chinese studio figures sold under their own brand names are genuine products, not bootlegs, and are often excellent value.

Fragile packaging and box condition

Collectors care about the box, and shipping is where boxes get hurt. Window boxes dent, corners crush and blister packs crack if a parcel is handled roughly. Air freight parcels are handled many times. Ask the seller to double-box the item with padding around every side and not to ship in the manufacturer's box alone. Some sellers offer a reinforced shipping box for a small fee. Be realistic as well: a minor dent on an outer box is not a shipping claim, and there is no return route from Pakistan. If mint-box condition is essential to you, understand that it cannot be guaranteed by anyone across two countries.

Pre-orders

Many figures are sold on pre-order months before release, often with a deposit and a balance due on release. Through an agent, this becomes a longer process: the item is reserved, released in China months later, then shipped to you. Two things to clarify before you commit. First, how the pre-order is handled and when payment is due, because a rupee price quoted today for an item released in six months is exposed to exchange rate movement. Second, release dates slip; Chinese New Year, 11.11 and 618 all cause delays. If you need the item by a date, buy an in-stock listing instead.

Duty on figures and collectibles

Figures, model kits and toys are dutiable in Pakistan under their HS codes, with additional customs duty, sales tax at the standard rate and advance income tax on the customs value including freight. Valuation rulings may apply. Combined taxes on imports commonly fall between 25 and 60 percent of customs value and change with budgets, so confirm the current rate. Collectibles are sometimes assessed as toys and sometimes at the declared value, and a high-value figure is a high-value import in customs' eyes regardless of what it is. What commonly goes wrong?

  • Bootleg arrives, or is seized. The price was too good. Buy from official stores.
  • Box crushed. Single-boxed shipping. Ask for double boxing before dispatch.
  • Pre-order delayed. Release slipped by months. Buy in-stock if timing matters.
  • Wrong version. Standard versus deluxe, with or without bonus parts. Specify.
  • Price moved. Exchange rate shifted during a long pre-order gap.

Frequently asked questions

Can I buy anime figures from Taobao in Pakistan?

Yes, through an agent. Taobao ships within China only and takes Chinese payment, so an agent buys the figure, receives it in China and forwards it to you.

How do I tell a bootleg figure from a licensed one?

Price far below retail, listings that avoid the maker's name, stock photos only and no authenticity sticker are the usual signs. Buy from official Tmall brand stores or licensed retailers.

Will customs seize a bootleg figure?

It can. Bootlegs infringe trademarks and may be treated as counterfeit goods, which are prohibited. Even if one gets through, the quality is poor.

Is customs duty charged on an anime figure?

Yes. Figures and model kits are dutiable, with sales tax and advance income tax on the customs value. Confirm the current rate because it changes with budgets.

Can I pre-order a figure from China through a service?

Usually yes, but clarify how payment and timing work before committing. Releases can slip by months and a rupee price quoted far in advance is exposed to exchange rate movement.
