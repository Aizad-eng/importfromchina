Importing Medical or Lab Equipment From China: DRAP Rules for One Unit

By Muhammad Shamshad AslamUpdated October 23, 20269 min read
Quick answer

Yes, but DRAP rules apply before customs releases it. Under the Medical Devices Rules, 2017, a commercial importer needs a DRAP establishment licence plus enlistment (Class A) or registration (Classes B, C and D) for the device. A single unit can also come in through narrower routes: a personal-use NOC for a patient, a hospital's prior approval, or an evaluation permit.

Do you need DRAP approval to import one medical device?

Yes. In Pakistan, medical devices are regulated by the Drug Regulatory Authority of Pakistan (DRAP) under the Medical Devices Rules, 2017, notified as S.R.O. 32(I)/2018. Rule 26 says no medical device is released from customs unless the importer has obtained a clearance certificate from an officer authorised by DRAP. That applies to one unit as much as to a container.

What changes with one unit is the route. The normal commercial route is built for distributors. For a single device there are narrower routes for patients, hospitals and evaluation, and which one fits depends on who you are and whether the device is already available in Pakistan.

Is your equipment a medical device or ordinary lab equipment?

It depends on what the manufacturer says the item is for. Rule 12 defines medical devices as instruments, apparatus, machines, reagents for in-vitro use, software and similar items intended by the manufacturer for medical purposes, such as diagnosis, monitoring or treatment of disease or injury, supporting life, or giving information by examining specimens from the human body.

That second part catches a lot of lab equipment. The rules define in-vitro diagnostic (IVD) devices to include reagents, calibrators, control materials, specimen containers, software and related instruments used to examine human or animal specimens for diagnosis or monitoring. A chemistry analyser sold for clinical labs is a medical device. A general-purpose lab oven or a microscope sold for teaching, with no medical intended use on the listing or in the manual, may fall outside the definition.

Read the manufacturer's intended use carefully. Many Chinese listings describe the same machine as "for hospital, clinic and laboratory use". If the listing, manual or label claims a medical or diagnostic purpose, assume DRAP rules apply.

What are DRAP's Class A, B, C and D?

DRAP sorts medical devices into four classes by hazard, with Class A the lowest and Class D the highest. Schedule A of the rules gives these examples:

ClassHazard levelExamples in the rulesDRAP product approval
ALowTongue depressors, disposable masks, hospital beds, non-invasive electrodes, examination glovesEnlistment
BLow to moderateHypodermic needles, suction equipment, hearing aids, electronic thermometers, blood pressure monitors, ECG machinesRegistration
CModerate to highLung ventilators, bone fixation plates, baby incubators, surgical lasersRegistration
DHighHeart valves, implantable defibrillators, breast implantsRegistration

The class comes from the manufacturer's intended use and the technology involved, using the rules in Schedule A. For active diagnostic equipment, the rules place most devices that monitor vital signs or capture physiological signals in Class B, with exceptions moving some higher. If two rules apply, the higher class wins. The manufacturer should document its classification, and DRAP's Medical Devices Board settles disputes.

What does enlistment or registration mean for one unit?

It means the product must already be approved by DRAP before it can be imported commercially. Rule 13 provides enlistment for Class A devices and registration for Classes B, C and D. Rule 14 says the application is made by a licensed local manufacturer or by an importer acting as the foreign maker's authorised representative in Pakistan.

Rule 21 then sets the condition for normal imports: the importer must hold a valid medical device establishment licence and the device's enlistment or registration, and have premises for proper storage. The licence conditions include suitable premises, good distribution practice, and at least one full-time technical person with a relevant degree such as pharmacy or biomedical engineering.

For a clinic that wants one unit, this route is rarely realistic. If the exact model is already enlisted or registered by a Pakistani distributor, buying through that distributor is usually simpler. A temporary exemption under S.R.O. 224(I)/2023 let licensed establishments import without enlistment or registration until 31 December 2024 for Classes A and B, and only with documents such as a notarised ISO 13485 certificate and a manufacturer's authorisation letter. We could not find a later extension when we checked in October 2026, so check DRAP's current notifications.

Can a patient import a device for personal use?

Yes, in small quantities, with DRAP permission if it does not come in your own luggage. Rule 24 allows small quantities of medical devices for personal use, including devices that are not enlisted or registered. If the device is not part of a passenger's own baggage, DRAP or an authorised officer must grant permission before the import, and the quantity must be reasonable for personal needs.

DRAP's import and export guidelines say the application can be made by the patient or a family member, at the DRAP field office in your province or online through DRAP's personal-use NOC portal. The documents listed are:

  • A copy of the CNIC of the patient, and of the applicant if a family member applies.
  • A list of the devices with name, country of origin and quantity.
  • A prescription from a registered practitioner showing the prescriber's PMC registration number, where required.

This route is for a patient's own use. It does not cover a clinic buying equipment to use on patients it charges.

Can a clinic or hospital import one unit that is not sold in Pakistan?

Yes, if the device is not enlisted, registered or available in Pakistan, and the hospital gets DRAP's approval first. Rule 24(d) allows such devices for patients in public or private hospitals, on conditions: the device is not sold or distributed in the market, it is on free sale in its country of origin, it is used only in that hospital or institution, consumption records are kept by qualified staff, and a DRAP clearance certificate is obtained when the shipment arrives, before customs clearance.

DRAP's guidelines add the paperwork for the pre-approval: an application to the field office, the device's name, quantity and origin, a free sale certificate, the hospital or institution's registration, and an affidavit on stamp paper that includes supplying the device to patients on a no profit, no loss basis. When the parcel lands, the hospital applies for the customs NOC with the pre-approval, invoice and packing list.

A small private clinic should check whether it qualifies as a registered hospital or institution under these terms before ordering.

What about a unit for evaluation or research?

A separate permit covers small quantities for clinical investigation or evaluation. Under Rules 22 and 23, you apply on Form 9 to DRAP's Director of Medical Devices and Medicated Cosmetics with the fee, and the permit is issued on Form 10. The device must be used only for the stated purpose, DRAP may visit the premises where it is kept, and you must keep a full record of its use. This suits universities, research labs and companies evaluating a device, not routine clinical use.

What happens at customs?

Customs holds the device until DRAP clears it. Rule 26 says a medical device cannot be released without a DRAP clearance certificate. If the Collector of Customs or a DRAP officer suspects the device does not comply, the matter goes to DRAP's medical devices division. Customs can hand it over against a written undertaking not to dispose of it, and the importer must return it within ten days if asked.

Duty and sales tax apply as for other goods. For how that part is worked out, see customs duty from China to Pakistan.

What about spare parts and accessories?

Accessories can be classified separately from the main device. Schedule A says an accessory to a medical device may be classified on its own under the same rules. So a replacement probe, cable or headset is not automatically treated the same as the machine it plugs into, but it is not automatically outside DRAP either.

This is a typical one-unit need. A step-by-step check before you order

  1. Read the intended use on the listing and manual. Medical or diagnostic claim means DRAP rules apply.
  2. Work out the likely class from Schedule A, and ask the seller what class the device has in other markets.
  3. Check whether the exact model is already enlisted or registered in Pakistan. If it is, a local distributor may be the simplest route.
  4. Pick your route: personal-use NOC for a patient, hospital pre-approval for an unavailable device, or a Form 9 permit for evaluation.
  5. Ask the seller for a free sale certificate, ISO 13485 certificate and declaration of conformity. DRAP routes commonly ask for them.
  6. Get DRAP's approval before the item ships, not after it lands.

Common mistakes

  • Treating a CE or FDA mark as DRAP approval. Foreign approvals help with paperwork but do not replace DRAP's.
  • Assuming lab equipment is never a medical device. Instruments for examining human specimens count as IVD devices.
  • Using the personal-use route for a clinic. It is for a patient's own use only.
  • Applying after the parcel arrives. Hospital and personal routes need permission before import.
  • Ordering several units. Small quantities are the basis of every non-commercial route.

Frequently asked questions

Does a medical device from China need DRAP approval in Pakistan?

Yes. Under the Medical Devices Rules, 2017, customs releases a medical device only after a DRAP clearance certificate. The route depends on whether it is a commercial import, personal use, hospital use or evaluation.

What is the difference between DRAP enlistment and registration?

Enlistment is DRAP's approval for low-hazard Class A devices. Registration is required for Classes B, C and D. Both are applied for by a licensed manufacturer or the foreign maker's authorised importer.

Can I import a medical device for my own use without a licence?

Yes, in small quantities, with DRAP permission before import if it is not in your own luggage. Apply at a DRAP field office or online with your CNIC, a device list and a prescription where required.

Can a clinic import one machine that is not available in Pakistan?

A registered hospital or institution can, with DRAP's prior approval under rule 24(d), if the device is not enlisted, registered or available in Pakistan and will not be sold.

Is laboratory equipment regulated by DRAP?

If it is intended for examining human or animal specimens for diagnosis or monitoring, it is an in-vitro diagnostic medical device under the rules. General lab equipment with no medical intended use may fall outside.

Do spare parts and accessories need DRAP approval too?

They can. The rules allow an accessory to be classified separately from the main device, so check the accessory's own intended use and class before ordering.
