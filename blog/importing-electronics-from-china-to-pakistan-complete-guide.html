Importing Electronics From China to Pakistan: The Complete Guide

By importfromchina.shop teamUpdated October 11, 202611 min read
Quick answer

You can import a single electronic item from China to Pakistan, but check five things before you pay. Phones and other SIM devices need PTA registration. Built-in batteries usually ship fine by air, while power banks and spare batteries often do not. Pakistan runs on 230V 50Hz with C and D sockets. China-market versions may lack Google services or work only on Chinese servers. Warranty is usually limited to the country of purchase, and duty and taxes are charged on the customs value.

Which electronics make sense to import from China one at a time?

The items that make the most sense are the ones Pakistani shops do not stock, or stock only in an older or more expensive version. Think niche audio gear such as in-ear monitors and small USB DACs, retro handheld game consoles, electronics tools like smart soldering irons and LCR tweezers, car diagnostic scanners, dash cams, camera lenses and gimbals, and replacement parts for machines and appliances. These are products where Chinese brands lead and local stock is thin.

The items that make less sense are the ones already sold widely in Pakistan with a local warranty, such as mainstream phones and big-brand laptops. You can still import them, but the saving is smaller once duty is added, and you lose local warranty support. Our laptop guide and phone and PTA guide go through those two categories in detail.

Before you decide, ask three questions. Is it sold locally, and at what price? Does it need any approval or registration in Pakistan? Does it have a battery, a plug, an app or a radio that could cause trouble? The rest of this guide answers those questions category by category.

Which electronics have extra rules in Pakistan?

Most electronics clear customs like any other goods: they are classified under a PCT code and taxed. A few categories have an extra layer of rules on top of that. The table below summarises the ones that matter most for a single buyer.

PTA's type approval FAQ says type approval is required for anyone importing terminal equipment for commercial purposes. It lists mobile handsets, tablets and dongles, two-way radios, SIM or IMEI based tracking devices and IoT devices among the equipment that needs it, and lists switches, firewalls, servers, storage devices, non-SIM laptops and desktops, and GPS-only devices as exempt. A person bringing in one phone deals with DIRBS registration rather than type approval. PTA's DIRBS site says registration can be done within 60 days through its online service, and that there are no registration counters at the airport. You can check any IMEI by sending it by SMS to 8484. We do not state PTA tax amounts because they change and depend on the model, so check the current figure before you buy a phone.

Will a gadget from China work on Pakistan's power supply?

Usually yes, because both countries use 220 to 230 volts at 50Hz. Pakistan's mains is 230V 50Hz with type D sockets as the standard and type C plugs widely used. China runs on 220V 50Hz and mainly uses type I plugs, the angled flat pins, with A and C also found.

In practice that means three checks. First, look at the rating on the charger or power supply. Most phone, laptop and camera chargers say "100-240V", which works anywhere. Second, check the plug. A type I plug needs an adapter or a replacement cable for Pakistani sockets. Third, watch out for US-market items rated only for 110V or 120V. Plugging one into a Pakistani socket can destroy it, and it needs a proper step-down converter, not a plug adapter. Our kitchen and home appliance guide covers high wattage items like kettles and air fryers, where a cheap converter is not enough.

What happens to the battery inside a gadget during shipping?

Air cargo rules treat lithium batteries in three ways, and the one your item falls into decides whether it flies easily. The IATA guidance document for 2026 separates batteries contained in equipment (installed in the device), batteries packed with equipment (in the same box but not installed), and batteries shipped by themselves.

A phone, laptop, pair of earbuds or dash cam with its battery installed is the easy case. A device with a separate battery in the box is also common, though from 1 January 2026 those packed-with batteries must ship at a state of charge of 30% or less in most cases. The hard case is a loose battery. IATA classifies power banks as batteries, not as equipment, and says lithium-ion batteries shipped by themselves cannot go on a passenger aircraft without special approval. Many couriers simply refuse them. We explain the full picture in lithium battery rules for shipping from China to Pakistan.

Is a China-version gadget different from the global version?

Sometimes it is very different. Phones sold for mainland China ship without Google Play services, because Google is blocked there. Xiaomi's China builds, for example, have used their own app store in place of the Play Store and carried fewer languages. Smart home devices made for China may only pair with an account set to the mainland China server, and users outside China report lag and setup problems with them.

Many Chinese brands sell two or more versions of the same product: a China version and a global version, sometimes also EU or India versions. The global version usually costs a little more and is the safer pick for Pakistan. Before ordering, read the listing title and specs for words like "Global Version" or "International Version", and ask the seller in writing which version it is. The full checklist is in gadgets that work differently in Pakistan.

What warranty do I get on electronics bought from China?

Assume the manufacturer warranty will not be honoured in Pakistan unless the brand says otherwise in writing. Xiaomi's Pakistan warranty notice, for example, says its limited warranty is valid only in Pakistan and that the product is not eligible for international warranty service, with some exceptions for specific models. Apple's warranty says it may restrict service for iPhone and iPad to the country where the device was first sold.

What you realistically get is the seller's own promise, the platform's buyer protection, and paid repair at local shops or brand service centres. Keep the order page, invoice and chat history. We cover this in depth in warranty and after-sales when importing from China.

How are imported electronics taxed in Pakistan?

Duty and taxes are calculated on the customs value, which is based on CIF: the cost of the item plus freight plus insurance. On top of that value, several charges can stack. Customs duty depends on the PCT code and commonly falls in slabs such as 0%, 3%, 11%, 16% or 20%, with some goods higher. Additional customs duty is typically 2% to 7%. Regulatory duty applies to some listed goods. Sales tax is 18%, and advance income tax applies to commercial imports. Combined, the total is commonly 25% to 60% of the customs value, but rates change with budgets and SROs, so confirm the current rate for your code.

Two details catch electronics buyers out. First, customs may not accept the price on your invoice. For many goods, the Directorate General of Customs Valuation publishes valuation rulings that set the value customs will use, which we explain in how customs valuation rulings work in Pakistan. Second, the same gadget can fall under different PCT codes depending on what it does, so a product described vaguely can be classified at a higher rate. Our customs duty guide walks through the full calculation.

How do I choose a seller for an electronic item?

For electronics, the seller matters more than the platform. You want a seller who states the exact model number, the version (China or global), the plug type and the battery details, and who answers questions in writing. A seller who will not say which firmware or region version they are sending is a seller to skip.

On Alibaba, a Verified Supplier badge means an inspection company such as SGS, TÜV Rheinland or Intertek has checked the company's profile and capabilities, but it does not tell you anything about one specific unit. For any seller, you can check the Chinese business licence against the official company registry. The method is in how to verify a Chinese seller. For a recent order we sourced one TDH39/DD45-type audiometric headset from a verified Alibaba supplier and delivered it in Pakistan, and the same principle applied: confirm the exact model and connector in writing before paying.

How do I import one electronic item from China, step by step?

  1. Find the exact listing. Pick a specific product page on Alibaba, AliExpress, Taobao or 1688, and note the model number and version.
  2. Check local availability. Search Pakistani shops and Daraz for the same model so you know what you are comparing against.
  3. Check the rules. Use the table above. If it has a SIM, plan for PTA. If it is a loose battery or power bank, expect shipping trouble.
  4. Check power and plug. Confirm the voltage range and plug type, and avoid 110V-only versions.
  5. Check the version. Ask the seller to confirm global or China version, app language and region.
  6. Get a full landed price. Add shipping, duty, taxes and any service fee. The listing price is only the start.
  7. Pay and keep records. Save the listing, the chat and the invoice for warranty and customs.
  8. Check it on arrival. Power it on, confirm the model and version, and check the battery is not swollen before you throw away the box.

What are the most common mistakes with electronics imports?

  • Buying a China version by accident. The listing photo looks the same, but the software does not include Google services or English.
  • Ordering a power bank or spare battery by air. It may be refused at the origin and sit with the seller or forwarder.
  • Ignoring the 110V label. US-market appliances can burn out on 230V.
  • Forgetting PTA. A phone that is not registered will stop working on Pakistani networks after the grace period.
  • Trusting the invoice value for duty. Customs can assess on a higher value under a valuation ruling.
  • Expecting the brand warranty to work in Pakistan. It usually does not for items bought abroad.

Frequently asked questions

Can I import a single phone from China to Pakistan?

Yes, but it must be registered with PTA through DIRBS within the grace period or it will be blocked on Pakistani networks. The PTA tax depends on the model, so check the current amount before buying.

Do Chinese chargers work in Pakistan?

Most phone and laptop chargers are rated 100-240V and work fine. You may need a plug adapter because China mainly uses type I plugs while Pakistan uses C and D sockets.

Can I ship a power bank from China to Pakistan by air?

Usually not easily. IATA classifies power banks as batteries, and standalone lithium-ion batteries cannot go on passenger aircraft without approval, so many couriers refuse them.

Will the warranty work in Pakistan?

Usually not. Many brands limit warranty service to the country of purchase, so rely on the seller's promise, the platform's buyer protection and paid local repair.

How much duty is charged on electronics?

It depends on the PCT code. Customs duty, additional customs duty, regulatory duty where it applies and 18% sales tax commonly add up to 25% to 60% of the customs value. Confirm the current rate for your item.

How do I know if a gadget is the global version?

Check the listing title and specs for Global Version, ask the seller in writing, and after delivery check the software region and whether Google Play is installed.
