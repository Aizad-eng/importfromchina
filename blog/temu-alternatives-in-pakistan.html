Temu Alternatives in Pakistan: Where Else to Buy Chinese Products

By Muhammad Shamshad AslamUpdated October 23, 20269 min read
Quick answer

The main Temu alternatives for buyers in Pakistan are AliExpress for the widest Chinese catalogue, Shein for clothing and home items, Daraz for fast local delivery with cash on delivery and returns, Alibaba.com for single pieces from manufacturers, and Taobao or 1688 through a buying service for things none of these carry. The right one depends on how fast you need the item, how you want to pay, and whether you might need to return it.

Why look for a Temu alternative in Pakistan?

Temu works in Pakistan. Its Pakistan site lists free standard shipping on all orders, with delivery in 5 to 13 business days after 1 to 3 days of processing, and a 90-day return window. For cheap everyday goods it is hard to beat. People look elsewhere for a few specific reasons:

  • The item is not on Temu Pakistan, or will not ship to their address.
  • They want cash on delivery, which Temu's Pakistan site did not offer when we checked in October 2026.
  • They need it this week, not in two weeks.
  • They want a specific brand, model or spec that Temu's listings do not carry, or describe vaguely.
  • Quality has let them down on a past order, and they want a seller with a longer track record.

Before you leave Temu, check one thing. If your app is set to another country, perhaps because of a VPN or an account made abroad, you may be seeing the wrong catalogue. Switch the region to Pakistan, check that prices show in rupees, and search again. If the item is still missing, the alternatives below are worth your time.

Is AliExpress a good Temu alternative?

AliExpress has the biggest catalogue of any Chinese site that ships to Pakistan, and it is where you find niche items Temu does not carry: replacement parts, hobby electronics, specific connectors, tools and brand stores run by Chinese manufacturers. When we checked in October 2026, AliExpress opened from Pakistan showed prices in rupees.

The trade-offs are real. Payment needs a Visa or Mastercard with international transactions turned on, since JazzCash and Easypaisa are not accepted there and PayPal is not available to people in Pakistan. Delivery by post commonly takes 25 to 45 days, and a wrong item often has to go back to China at your cost. Shipping is set per listing by the seller, so each item can have a different cost and time. Our guide on buying from AliExpress in Pakistan covers the card problems.

Is Shein a better choice than Temu for clothes?

For clothing, often yes. Shein is built around fashion, and each listing carries its own size chart in centimetres. When we checked its pages with the location set to Pakistan in October 2026, Shein listed standard shipping of 7 to 12 business days, and its payment page listed JazzCash and Easypaisa alongside cards.

Returns are the weak point. The return method shown for Pakistan was self-return, where you pay your own courier and Shein does not reimburse it, and swimwear, lingerie, underwear, jewellery and beauty items cannot be returned. Temu's Pakistan policy, by contrast, offers a free first return per order within 90 days. Read how to buy from Shein in Pakistan before you choose.

When is Daraz the better option?

Daraz is a Pakistani marketplace, so much of its stock is already in the country. That brings three things Temu cannot match: delivery in days, cash on delivery, and local returns. Daraz's return policy says a return request must be raised within 14 days of delivery. Change-of-mind returns apply only to some categories, such as clothing and shoes, and not to non-DarazMall items shipped from overseas. Orders paid by cash on delivery are refunded to your Daraz Wallet.

Much of what Daraz sellers stock comes from the same Chinese suppliers, with a local margin added. For common, cheap items, that margin is often worth paying for the speed and the return route. Compare the two in Temu vs Daraz in Pakistan.

Can you buy just one item on Alibaba.com from Pakistan?

Sometimes. Alibaba.com is a business-to-business marketplace, but many suppliers list in-stock Ready to Ship products with low minimum orders, and some accept a single piece. It suits equipment, tools, parts and trade items that consumer sites do not sell. Trade Assurance gives some payment protection on qualifying orders.

The catch is shipping. A supplier sending one piece by international courier often quotes a shipping cost close to, or above, the item price. Ask the supplier for the shipping cost to your city before you pay, and compare it with the item price.

What about Taobao, 1688 and Pinduoduo?

These are Chinese domestic platforms. Taobao and Pinduoduo sell to Chinese consumers, and 1688 is the domestic wholesale site. They need Chinese payment methods and their sellers ship within China, so none of them deliver to Pakistan directly. Pinduoduo is run by the same parent company as Temu, but it is a separate app for China only.

Prices on 1688 are often well below what the same item costs on Temu or Daraz, and the range of grades, colours and sizes is wider. To buy there you need a buying service with a Chinese account and address, which then ships the item to Pakistan.

What about local pages selling "Temu products"?

Facebook and Instagram pages that sell Temu or China items in Pakistan are resellers. They buy in bulk or order on your behalf, add a margin and set their own delivery and return terms. Some offer cash on delivery, which is why people use them. Before paying, check how long the page has been active, whether it asks for advance payment to a personal account, and what happens if the item is wrong. Temu's own return policy does not cover items bought from a reseller.

How do you compare the real cost between these sites?

Compare the delivered total in rupees, not the listing price. A price that looks lower on one site can end up higher once everything is added. For each option, write down:

  1. The item price for the exact variant you want, not the cheapest variant shown in search.
  2. The shipping line at checkout for your Pakistani address.
  3. Any import tax shown at checkout, and the chance of extra duty on arrival.
  4. Card charges and your bank's exchange rate if you pay in a foreign currency.
  5. The cost of a return if the item is wrong, which is zero on some sites and an overseas courier fee on others.

Then compare the totals side by side. Post and courier parcels worth Rs 1,000 or less are duty-free under section 19C of the Customs Act, and above that duty can apply, so a cheap-looking order can grow. See customs duty from China to Pakistan for rates.
