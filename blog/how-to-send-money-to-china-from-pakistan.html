How to Send Money to China From Pakistan

By Muhammad Shamshad AslamUpdated November 12, 20269 min read
Quick answer

You send money to China from Pakistan in one of three legal ways: a Visa or Mastercard on the seller's own checkout, a bank transfer made as a registered importer through the Pakistan Single Window, or a Pakistan-based service you pay in rupees. PayPal, Raast and Wise could not move rupees to a Chinese seller when we checked in November 2026.

What are the ways to send money from Pakistan to China?

There are three routes that work and several that people try and fail with. Which one fits depends on whether you are paying a platform such as Alibaba or AliExpress, paying a factory directly as a business, or just want one item delivered. This guide is about moving the money. Can you pay a Chinese seller with a Pakistani debit or credit card?

Yes, as long as the card is on the Visa or Mastercard network and you pay on the seller's own checkout page. Most Pakistani bank cards come with international online use switched off, so turn it on in your banking app first. A PayPak-only card will not work abroad.

Card payments abroad sit inside rules set by the State Bank of Pakistan. When the SBP set them in November 2022, Dawn reported:

  • An annual limit of USD 30,000 on international card transactions, counted per person across all your cards, from 1 January 2023.
  • Personal use only. Banks must make sure card use matches the cardholder's profile and personal needs. Businesses with cross-border payments are meant to use a separate framework in the Foreign Exchange Manual.

For one item this limit will not matter. What does matter is the extra cost: your bank converts the dollar price at its own rate, and an advance tax applies to international card payments. Our guide to tax on international card payments in Pakistan explains the current rates for filers and non-filers.

How does a bank transfer to China work for an importer?

A business that imports in its own name pays a Chinese supplier by telegraphic transfer through its bank, and the payment is tied to the goods through the Pakistan Single Window (PSW). The old Electronic Import Form is gone. PSW's page on the change says banks now send a "financial instrument" to PSW electronically, so traders no longer collect an EIF from the bank before clearing goods.

PSW lists the payment modes a financial instrument can cover:

  • Advance payment. You pay before the goods ship.
  • Letter of credit. The banks handle payment against shipping documents.
  • Contract or collection. Payment against documents under a registered contract.
  • Open account. PSW says no financial instrument is needed for open account imports, although a bank still reports cash margin where one applies.

On advance payments, the SBP relaxed its rules in early 2024. Dawn reported on 1 February 2024 that banks may make import advance payments without prior SBP approval, against an irrevocable letter of credit or an invoice, for up to 100% of its value, with the bank doing due diligence. So a supplier asking for full payment upfront is not blocked by SBP rules, but it is your bank that checks and sends it.

What does an importer need before the bank will send the money?

An importer needs a tax registration, a PSW account and a bank account at a PSW-integrated bank, all linked together. In order, the steps look like this:

  1. Register for tax. A commercial importer needs an NTN, and usually STRN for sales tax.
  2. Subscribe to PSW. PSW says you must be an active subscriber with a valid account at a bank integrated with PSW.
  3. Link your bank profile. Under Registration in PSW, select the bank and enter your IBAN plus the email and mobile number registered with that bank. The bank verifies it.
  4. Get the supplier's proforma invoice. The bank needs it to set up the advance payment or contract.
  5. Ask the bank to remit and issue the financial instrument. The bank shares it with PSW.
  6. Associate the instrument with your goods declaration. PSW says it must be active, not expired, match the payment mode and be in the same currency as the declaration.

Our guide to the Pakistan Single Window covers PSW registration in more detail. This route is built for businesses. If you only want one item, it is far more paperwork than the item is worth.

Can an individual send a bank transfer to China for one item?

In practice it is rarely the right tool. The import payment framework is designed around importers with tax numbers and PSW accounts, and the SBP's card rules point businesses, not individuals, to that framework. A one-off transfer for a personal item still has to clear customs as an import, so a bank transfer does not save you any steps on the Pakistan side.

There is also no buyer protection on a direct wire. If you send money to a supplier's own account and the item never ships, getting it back is very hard. A card payment on a platform's checkout, or paying a Pakistan-based service that buys for you, keeps a way back if something goes wrong.

Why can't you use PayPal, Raast or Wise to pay China?

Each one fails for a different reason, and all three were confirmed when we checked in November 2026:

  • PayPal. PayPal has no direct operations in Pakistan. ProPakistani reported in May 2026 that PayPal launched in Sri Lanka while Pakistan was still left out. You cannot open an account as a Pakistan resident.
  • Raast. In September 2026 the SBP described Raast as a domestic instant payment system that does not currently facilitate cross-border transfers, as reported by Arab News. It is excellent for paying someone in Pakistan, and useless for paying a seller in China.
  • Wise. Wise's own help page on PKR transfers says you can send from PKR only if you already hold PKR in your Wise account, and that adding PKR from an external bank account is not currently possible. So a Pakistani bank account cannot fund a Wise payment to China.

Can you send money to someone's Alipay or WeChat from Pakistan?

Not in any normal way. Chinese sellers on Taobao and 1688 often expect Alipay or WeChat Pay, but there is no Pakistani bank or wallet route that tops up either one. Foreign cards linked to WeChat Pay cannot make person to person transfers at all. We cover the details in our separate guides on Alipay and WeChat Pay in Pakistan.

Be wary of anyone offering to "load" a Chinese wallet for you in exchange for a rupee transfer to a personal account. That is outside the banking system, and you have no recourse if the money disappears.

How do you avoid losing money when paying a Chinese seller?

Keep the payment on the platform and check every bank detail before money moves. Most losses follow the same few patterns:

  • Changed bank details. An email says the supplier's account has changed. Confirm by voice call before sending anything, because email accounts get taken over.
  • A personal account. The account name does not match the company you are dealing with.
  • A discount for paying off the platform. A small saving in exchange for losing buyer protection is a bad trade.
  • Deposits by direct transfer. A request to send a deposit by WeChat, a direct Alipay transfer or to a friend's account.
  • Payment links in messages. Pay only on the site or app itself, never through a link a seller sends.

What mistakes do people make when sending money to China?

  • Using a card for business stock. The SBP says cards are for personal payments. Commercial imports belong in the importer framework.
  • Forgetting the extra costs. The bank's exchange rate and the advance tax on card payments sit on top of the dollar price.
  • Paying before checking the seller. Look at years on the platform and verification before paying anything.
  • Counting only the item price. Shipping to Pakistan and customs come on top of what you pay the seller.
  • Trying PayPal through a relative abroad. An account opened with someone else's country details can be limited.

Frequently asked questions

Can I send money to China from Pakistan for personal shopping?

Yes. The usual way is a Visa or Mastercard with international online use switched on, used on the seller's own checkout. You can also pay a Pakistan-based service in rupees and let it pay the seller.

Is there a limit on paying China with a Pakistani card?

Yes. The SBP set an annual limit of USD 30,000 per person on international card transactions from January 2023, counted across all your cards. Cards are meant for personal use.

Can I pay a Chinese factory by bank transfer from Pakistan?

A registered importer can. The bank remits the payment and shares a financial instrument with PSW, which is then linked to the goods declaration. Individuals buying one item rarely use this route.

Can I use Raast to pay a seller in China?

No. Raast moves money between accounts in Pakistan. In September 2026 the SBP said it does not currently facilitate cross-border transfers.

Does Wise work for sending money from Pakistan to China?

Not from a Pakistani bank account. Wise says you can send from PKR only if you already hold PKR in your Wise account, and you cannot add PKR from an external bank account.

What is the safest way to pay a Chinese seller?

Pay through the platform's own checkout, such as Alibaba with Trade Assurance, or pay a Pakistan-based service with a written refund policy. Avoid direct transfers to personal accounts.
