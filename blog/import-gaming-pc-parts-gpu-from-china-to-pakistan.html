Importing PC Parts From China to Pakistan: GPUs, CPUs, Keyboards

By importfromchina.shop teamUpdated October 3, 20267 min read
Quick answer

Yes, you can import a single GPU, CPU, motherboard, RAM kit, SSD or mechanical keyboard from China to Pakistan. None of these contain restricted batteries (wireless keyboards have built-in ones, which are fine). Customs duty and taxes apply on the landed value, there is no warranty support in Pakistan for grey imports, and the biggest risk with graphics cards is used or mining stock sold as new. Buy from rated sellers and specify the exact SKU.

Can I import one PC part from China to Pakistan?

Yes. PC components are a popular single-item import because Chinese prices on parts, especially from Taobao, JD and 1688, are often lower than Pakistani retail, and because some parts never reach local shops at all. Chinese mechanical keyboards in particular have a large enthusiast market with brands and switches that are hard to find in Pakistan. There is no PTA involvement and nothing on the restricted list. The questions are duty, warranty and whether what arrives is what was listed.

Small, dense, high-value parts suit air freight: a graphics card, CPU, motherboard, RAM kit, SSD, keyboard, mouse or cooler. Cases, monitors and power supplies are bulky or heavy, so freight eats the saving, and monitors are fragile. For those, the local price often wins.

What duty applies to PC parts?

Each part type has its own HS code and duty slab. On top of customs duty you pay additional customs duty, sales tax at the standard rate and advance income tax, all calculated on the customs value, which is the invoice price plus freight and insurance. Customs applies valuation rulings on many electronics, so a graphics card may be assessed at a set minimum value even if you found a bargain. Combined taxes on imports commonly land between 25 and 60 percent of customs value, and the exact figure for your part depends on its code and the current budget. Confirm the current rate; it changes. The customs duty guide walks through the calculation.

The used-GPU problem

This is the single biggest risk in the category. Cheap listings for popular graphics cards on Chinese platforms are often used cards, including ex-mining cards, with cleaned fans, new thermal pads and sometimes a replacement shroud, sold as new or "99% new". Used electronics are also restricted for import in many categories, so a card that is obviously used can be assessed at a higher value or refused by customs. Protect yourself:

  • Buy from a high-volume, highly rated seller, ideally a brand's official store on Taobao or JD.
  • Ask the seller for the serial number before shipping and check it with the manufacturer's warranty tool.
  • Ask for a sealed retail box. A card arriving in a plain brown box or an antistatic bag alone is a warning sign.
  • Avoid listings described as second-hand, refurbished, mining, or at a price far below every other seller.

Is there any warranty in Pakistan?

Assume not. The manufacturer's warranty on a China-market card or board is honoured in China. Pakistani distributors of the same brand generally will not service a grey import, and local shops offer no cover on parts they did not sell. If a card fails, your options are a paid repair, shipping it back to China at your own cost, or writing it off. Weigh that against the local price, especially on an expensive card.

Packaging and static

Components are sensitive to static and to impact. Ask the seller to ship in the original retail box with its foam, inside an outer carton with padding. Bare boards should be in an antistatic bag. Heavy graphics cards benefit from extra padding around the ends to prevent the PCB flexing. Air freight parcels are handled many times between the seller and your door, and a card that rattles inside a loose box can arrive with a cracked PCB or bent fins. Ask the seller before dispatch; nothing can be changed in transit.

Mechanical keyboards and Chinese software

Chinese keyboards are excellent value and ship easily; wireless models have built-in batteries that travel by air normally. Two things to check. First, the layout. Most are ANSI US layout, which is what Pakistani buyers expect, but some are Chinese-market variants with different key legends. Second, the software. Many Chinese boards use a Chinese-language driver for remapping and lighting that may not have an English version. Boards that support VIA or QMK avoid the problem entirely. Ask the seller before buying.

What commonly goes wrong?

  • Used card sold as new. The most common complaint. Buy from official stores and check serials.
  • Wrong variant. Same model name, different VRAM, clock or cooler. Specify the exact SKU.
  • Fake SSD or RAM capacity. Cheap unbranded drives report a capacity they do not have. Stick to known brands from their own stores.
  • Transit damage. Bent fins or cracked boards from poor packing.
  • Exchange rate movement. A rupee quote on an expensive card can shift if you delay paying.

Frequently asked questions

Can I import a single graphics card from China to Pakistan?

Yes. A GPU has no battery and is not restricted. Duty and taxes apply on arrival, there is no local warranty, and you should buy from an official brand store to avoid used cards sold as new.

Is there warranty on PC parts imported from China?

Only in China. Pakistani distributors generally do not service grey imports. Treat any fault as your own cost when deciding whether the saving is worth it.

How do I avoid buying a used mining GPU from China?

Buy from the brand's official store or a high-volume rated seller, ask for the serial number and a sealed retail box, and avoid any listing far below the market price.

Can a wireless mechanical keyboard ship by air?

Yes. The battery is built into the keyboard, which air carriers accept. Check the layout and whether the configuration software has an English version or the board supports VIA or QMK.

Is it worth importing a PC case or monitor from China?

Usually not for a single unit. Both are bulky, monitors are fragile, and air freight is priced by weight and volume. The local price often wins once freight is added.
