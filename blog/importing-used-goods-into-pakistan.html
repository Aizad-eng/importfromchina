Can You Import Used Goods Into Pakistan?

By Muhammad Shamshad AslamUpdated November 12, 202610 min read
Quick answer

Yes, for many items, but not all. Pakistan's Import Policy Order 2022 lists goods that are banned in used or second-hand condition in Appendix C, allowing only the exceptions written into it, and used cars have their own schemes. Other used goods come in like new ones and pay duty on their value, so check the PCT code before you buy.

New to importing? Read our step-by-step guide on how to import from China to Pakistan.

Is it legal to import second-hand goods into Pakistan?

Yes, unless the item appears in the used goods list. The Import Policy Order 2022, issued as SRO 545(I)/2022, has an appendix for this, Appendix C. Goods named in Appendix C cannot be imported in used or second-hand condition except where the appendix itself makes an exception. Goods not named there follow the same rules as new goods, including any NOC or standard that applies to them.

So the question is not whether second-hand is allowed in general. It is whether your particular product, by its PCT code, is on the restricted list. For the full process of bringing in one item, see how to import from China to Pakistan.

What is on the used goods restriction list?

We could not open a current consolidated copy of Appendix C, so we can only describe what reputable summaries confirm. The International Energy Agency's summary of the order says Appendix C lists items prohibited for import even in used condition, and gives examples tied to the age of used buses and other vehicles and to steel scrap from auto parts. The same summary notes that the order works alongside the Pakistan Environmental Protection Act 1997, under which imports of hazardous waste are prohibited.

Appendix C works the opposite way to Appendix B. Appendix B lists goods that may come in once a condition is met, while Appendix C lists goods that are refused when they are used, whatever paperwork you hold. Because the list changes through SROs, the safe approach is to look up your exact code in the current amended text, or ask a clearing agent who works with that code. Do not rely on an old copy of the order.

Can you import a used car into Pakistan?

Used cars are the most changed category, and the rules differ for personal and commercial imports:

  • Personal routes. Dawn reported on 16 January 2026 that the personal baggage scheme for used cars had been abolished, leaving only the gift and transfer of residence schemes. The gap allowed between imports under those schemes was extended from 700 to 850 days, a car brought in this way cannot be transferred for one year, and Engineering Development Board standards apply.
  • Commercial imports. The same report said commercial imports of used cars carried a 40 percent additional duty until 30 June 2026, set to fall by ten percentage points a year from 2026-27 and reach zero by 2029-30. ProPakistani reported on 30 October 2026 that the five-year age limit for commercial used vehicle imports under PCT headings 8702, 8703, 8704 and 8711 was removed with effect from 1 July 2026, while other policy conditions and industry standards still apply.

Cars are outside what we do, but the history shows how fast used goods rules move.

How does customs value a used item?

The starting point is the same as for new goods. Section 25 of the Customs Act sets customs value as the transaction value, meaning the price actually paid, plus freight and insurance to Pakistan. If customs cannot accept that price, the Act moves through other methods, such as the value of identical or similar goods. A low price on a used item is not automatically accepted.

Valuation rulings under section 25A matter too. Where a ruling sets a value for a product, customs assesses at that value, and if your declared value is higher, the higher figure applies. Our guide on how customs valuation rulings work explains those.

Section 79 of the Customs Act has a proviso written for used goods. If an importer cannot make a full and correct declaration of used goods for want of information, an officer not below Additional Collector may let them examine the goods first and then file the declaration. This exists because used consignments rarely match a neat invoice.

What about used phones, laptops and electronics?

  • Phones. A used phone still needs PTA registration in DIRBS to work on Pakistani networks. PTA's 2021 regulations say duplicated, cloned, stolen and counterfeit devices are not valid for a certificate of compliance, so a second-hand phone with a bad IMEI cannot be fixed by paying tax. The same regulations allow a person up to five mobile devices a year for personal use, so check how many you have already registered. Our guide on importing a phone and PTA registration covers the tax side.
  • Laptops and cameras. These pay duty and taxes on their value like any other goods. Their lithium batteries fall under air shipping rules, which our guide to lithium battery rules explains.
  • Other electronics. Check the code against Appendix C first, then against any standard or approval that applies to the new version of the same product, such as PTA type approval for Wi-Fi and Bluetooth devices.

What extra risks come with second-hand items from China?

The legal rules are only half of it. A used item bought from a Chinese marketplace usually comes with no warranty that works in Pakistan, and the description is only as good as the seller. Before paying, ask the seller for pictures of the actual item, its serial number and any faults, and keep the conversation.

Value disputes are more common with used goods too. If you paid very little, keep proof of the price so the declared value matches what customs sees. Our guide to buying from Xianyu in Pakistan covers the second-hand market in China in more detail.

How do you check a used item before buying, step by step?

  1. Find the PCT code for the item.
  2. Check Appendix C in the current amended Import Policy Order for that code.
  3. Check the new-goods rules for the same code, such as PTA approval, PSQCA standards or a regulator's NOC.
  4. Check the battery and size, because shipping rules decide whether it can fly.
  5. Get proof of price and condition from the seller.
  6. Work out the landed cost with duty on the real value, not a guess.

What mistakes do people make with used imports?

  • Declaring used goods as new, or new as used, to change the value. Both are false statements under section 32 of the Customs Act, and customs can issue a notice for short-paid duty up to three years later for an honest error, or five years where there was collusion or deliberate misstatement.
  • Trusting an old copy of the rules, especially for vehicles and scrap.
  • Buying a used phone without checking the IMEI.
  • Assuming a low price means low duty. Customs can reject a price that does not fit the goods.
