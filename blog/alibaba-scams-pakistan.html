Alibaba Scams Pakistani Buyers Should Know About

By Muhammad Shamshad AslamUpdated November 12, 20269 min read
Quick answer

The Alibaba scams that hurt Pakistani buyers most are requests to pay outside Alibaba, changed bank details on an invoice, fake login links, social media agents who take money and vanish, branded items at impossible prices, and fake customs fee messages. Almost all of them work by moving your payment away from Alibaba's checkout, where Trade Assurance protects it.

Is Alibaba itself a scam?

No. Alibaba.com is a genuine platform run by Alibaba Group, and most suppliers on it are real businesses. The scams happen around it: a dishonest supplier, a fake website or a middleman uses Alibaba's name to get your money outside the system that protects you. Alibaba's Trade Assurance payment page puts the main rule in one line: never pay outside of the platform.

Understanding the common patterns is the best protection, because each one has a simple tell. This guide lists them, what they look like in practice and what to do. Why is paying outside Alibaba the biggest risk?

Because once money leaves Alibaba's checkout, Alibaba cannot hold it, refund it or judge a dispute about it. Trade Assurance works by holding your payment and releasing it to the supplier after delivery. Alibaba's How It Works page says you must pay for your order on Alibaba.com to qualify, and the Trade Assurance payment page says bank transfers should use the official bank details from Alibaba.com.

The request to pay directly usually comes with a reason that sounds reasonable: the platform fee is high, the payment system is down, the order is too small for Alibaba, or the boss wants payment to a different account. Any of these is a reason to stop, not a reason to pay. A genuine supplier that wants your order will accept payment through Alibaba.

How do changed bank detail scams work?

A message or invoice arrives with different bank details, sometimes from what looks like the supplier's real email address. The account may be in a person's name, or in a company name that does not match the supplier. If you pay it, the money goes to whoever controls that account, and getting it back from a foreign account is very hard.

The fix is simple. Never use bank details from chat, email or a PDF invoice. When you choose bank transfer at Alibaba checkout, Alibaba shows the beneficiary details for that order. Use those, and nothing else.

What do phishing attempts look like?

They look like Alibaba. An email says your order is on hold, your account is limited or a payment needs confirming, and gives a link. The page behind the link copies Alibaba's login screen and collects your password, or asks for card details. Alibaba's own safety article from 2022 warns about messages that send links presented as proof of payment.

  • Open alibaba.com or the app yourself instead of clicking links.
  • Check your order status inside your account, not in the email.
  • Never share one-time codes sent by your bank.
  • Turn on two-step login for your Alibaba account if it is offered.

Are "Alibaba agents" on Facebook and WhatsApp safe?

Some are genuine businesses, but you cannot tell from a page alone. The risk is that you send money to a personal JazzCash, Easypaisa or bank account, and the person disappears or delivers something else. Before you pay any agent or service, check:

  • A business name you can check, a physical address and a history you can verify.
  • A written price that says what is included and when you pay.
  • A written refund policy.
  • Payment to a business account, not a personal one.

Pakistan's National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency (NCCIA) handles online fraud complaints. A ProPakistani report in September 2026, based on official documents, said Pakistanis lost Rs 8.32 billion to cyber fraud over three years. Not all of that relates to shopping, but it shows how common online fraud is.

How do you spot a fake or misleading supplier on Alibaba?

Look for gaps between what the supplier claims and what you can check. A company that says it is a factory but lists hundreds of unrelated products, a profile created very recently, certificates that you cannot find on the issuer's website, or a business licence whose name does not match the supplier are all warning signs. Our guide on how to verify a Chinese seller shows how to check a company on China's official registry.

Watch for a few other patterns too. A supplier may ask for a sample fee by a money transfer service and then stop replying. Another may quote one price, take the order and then demand an extra payment for packing, shipping or a certificate before dispatch. A third may copy the name and pictures of a well-known supplier on a new profile. In each case the protection is the same: everything is agreed in the Alibaba order, every payment goes through Alibaba checkout, and any extra charge must be added to the order by the supplier, not paid on the side.

Badges help but do not prove everything. A Verified Supplier badge means an outside firm assessed the company at one point in time, and a Gold Supplier badge is a paid membership. Our guide to what Alibaba's Verified and Gold Supplier badges mean explains the difference.

What about branded products at very low prices?

Treat them as copies. Alibaba's own safety article says that when the price is too good to be true, it is best to avoid it, and that suppliers offering major brands cheaply are likely selling knock-offs. In Pakistan the risk is bigger than wasting money: customs can seize counterfeit goods, and you lose both the item and what you paid for it.

What should you do if you think you have been scammed?

  1. Stop paying. Do not send a second payment to "release" goods or fix a problem.
  2. If you paid through Alibaba, open a dispute from the order in your account and upload the chat, order and payment records. Alibaba says Trade Assurance protects an order for 30 days after you receive it.
  3. If you paid by card, call your bank to ask about blocking the card and disputing the charge.
  4. If you paid outside Alibaba, report the supplier to Alibaba anyway and keep every record.
  5. Report to the NCCIA if a person or page in Pakistan took your money.

What habits prevent almost every Alibaba scam?

  • Pay only at Alibaba checkout, never to an account given in chat.
  • Keep all messages inside Alibaba's Message Center.
  • Get the exact model and terms into the order before paying.
  • Do not click payment or login links in emails or SMS.
  • Skip famous brands at impossible prices.
  • Confirm delivery only after checking the item.

Frequently asked questions

Is Alibaba safe for buyers in Pakistan?

Yes, if you pay through Alibaba's checkout and keep communication on the platform. Most losses happen when payment is moved outside Alibaba.

Can I get my money back if I paid a supplier directly?

It is very hard. Alibaba cannot hold or refund money paid outside its checkout. Report the supplier and keep all records.

Why does a supplier want payment to a personal account?

Usually to avoid Alibaba's protection. A genuine supplier will accept payment through Alibaba checkout.

Are Gold Suppliers safe?

Gold Supplier is a paid membership. It shows a registered business, not good conduct, so still pay only through Trade Assurance.

I got an SMS asking for customs duty. Is it real?

Do not pay from the link. Contact the courier through its official channels and ask for the customs assessment.

Where can I report an online shopping fraud in Pakistan?

The National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency (NCCIA) handles online fraud complaints. Also report the seller on the platform.
