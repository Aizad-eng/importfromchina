Do You Need an Import License in Pakistan?

By Muhammad Shamshad AslamUpdated November 12, 20268 min read
Quick answer

No, Pakistan does not issue one general import licence. Imports are allowed unless the Import Policy Order bans or restricts them. A commercial importer instead needs an NTN, usually a sales tax registration and a Pakistan Single Window subscription, and some products need a permit from DRAP, PSQCA, PTA or another regulator. One personal item needs none of this.

Do you need an import license in Pakistan?

No, there is no single import licence that every importer must hold. Pakistan's rule works the other way round: goods may be imported from anywhere unless the Import Policy Order bans them or attaches a condition. What people call an "import licence" is usually a mix of three things: tax registration, registration on the customs system, and a product permit where the goods are regulated. For the wider process, see our guide on how to import from China to Pakistan.

What law decides what you can import?

The Imports and Exports (Control) Act 1950 gives the federal government the power to control imports, and the Ministry of Commerce uses it through the Import Policy Order. The current order is the Import Policy Order 2022, issued as SRO 545(I)/2022 on 22 April 2022. When it was notified, ProPakistani reported its general rule that the import of all goods may be allowed from worldwide sources, unless the order bans, prohibits or restricts them.

So the starting point is permission, not a licence. The work is in checking whether your product is one of the exceptions. That is also why the right order of questions is: is this item allowed, does it need a regulator's approval, and only then, who will file the customs declaration. People who start by looking for a licence often skip the first two questions, which are the ones that actually stop shipments. Our explainer on the Import Policy Order covers its appendices in detail.

What do commercial importers need instead of a licence?

A commercial importer needs to be identifiable to the tax system and registered on the customs system. These are the pieces, in the order people usually get them:

None of these is called an import licence, but together they do the job a licence does elsewhere: they let customs know who is importing and hold that person responsible.

How do you register to import in your own name?

You register with FBR first, then subscribe on PSW. When we checked PSW's subscription page in November 2026, it asked for:

  1. An NTN, FTN or STRN. FBR says an individual is registered once e-enrolled on IRIS, with the CNIC as the NTN.
  2. For a company, its SECP registration number or CUIN.
  3. A SIM registered in your own name.
  4. The email address exactly as it appears in IRIS.
  5. A one-time, non-refundable fee of Rs 500, paid against a system-generated PSID.
  6. Biometric verification at a NADRA e-Sahulat centre, which only the subscriber can do.

The user ID stays valid for two years, and PSW says you get at least fifteen days' notice before it is deactivated. After subscribing, you register your trader profile with the customs system from inside the portal. Our PSW guide walks through the screens.

Which goods need a permit before you import them?

Goods listed as restricted in Appendix B of the Import Policy Order, or covered by another regulator's law, need that regulator's permission. PSW calls these licences, permits, certificates and other documents LPCOs, and says filing through its Single Declaration is mandatory where such a requirement applies. Appendix B has separate parts for health and safety requirements, procedural requirements, live animals and animal products, plants and plant products, narcotic drugs and psychotropic substances, and medical devices. Common examples for goods from China:

  • Medicines and medical devices need DRAP permission.
  • Goods under compulsory standards need a PSQCA release order, applied for through PSW.
  • Phones and telecom equipment for sale need PTA type approval and a certificate of compliance.
  • Plant and animal products need the relevant quarantine clearance.

These are permissions for a product category, not a general licence to import.

Do you need a licence to import one item for personal use?

No. For one item you buy for yourself, the courier or clearing agent files the customs declaration under its own registration and identifies you by your CNIC. Since 15 August 2026, under SRO 918(I)/2026 as reported by ProPakistani, the import manifest carries the importer's NTN or FTN, with a CNIC or passport number accepted for small parcel importers and one-time importers without an NTN. Our guide on whether you need an NTN for one item goes further.

Which personal imports still need permission?

A few categories need permission even when the item is for you. Being personal does not lift the regulator's rule:

  • Medicines. DRAP takes applications for a personal-use NOC from the patient or applicant on its online portal, reviewed by its Import and Export Section.
  • Phones. Under PTA's 2021 regulations, as reported by ProPakistani, a person may import five mobile devices for personal use in a calendar year, and each must be registered with PTA to work on Pakistani networks.
  • Drones and items on the banned list, which a personal reason does not change.

What happens if quantities look commercial?

Then the import is treated as commercial, and commercial registration and paperwork follow. FBR defines a commercial quantity as goods imported prima facie for trading or pecuniary gain and not for personal use or gift. Its travel guide says goods brought in commercial quantities are released only on payment of a fine equal to 30% of their value, on top of duty and taxes. There is no fixed unit count, so several identical units or a range of sizes of the same product is where personal clearance stops working.

What happens if goods arrive without the permission they need?

They wait, and paying the duty does not release them. Where an LPCO applies, PSW says filing through its Single Declaration is mandatory, and customs releases the goods only once the regulator's document is in place. The regulator can also ask for more. On PSW's PSQCA module, for example, PSQCA can request further documents, call for a laboratory check, and issue a temporary release order with a conformity assessment report before the goods are finally cleared. Each step adds days, and port or airport storage charges can build up while the goods wait.

Some goods cannot be fixed by applying late. Items on Appendix A of the Import Policy Order are banned. PTA does not issue a certificate of compliance for duplicated, cloned, stolen or counterfeit devices. And goods that need a regulator's prior approval may not qualify if the shipment arrived first. The practical rule is simple: check the product's tariff code against the Import Policy Order and the regulator's rules before you pay the supplier, not after the goods land.

Is an NTN the same as an import licence?

No. An NTN is a tax registration. It is required to subscribe to PSW and to import commercially, but it does not on its own let you file declarations, and it does not permit restricted goods. You still need the PSW subscription, customs registration and any product permit. Equally, holding an NTN does not make a personal parcel duty-free or change how it is assessed. Being on FBR's Active Taxpayers List does matter in one way: section 148 income tax on imports doubles for people who are not on it, so filing your return lowers the tax on every import, personal or commercial.

Frequently asked questions

Is an import licence required in Pakistan?

There is no single general import licence. Commercial importers need an NTN, usually an STRN and a PSW subscription, and restricted goods need a permit from the relevant regulator.

How do I get registered as an importer in Pakistan?

E-enrol with FBR on IRIS for an NTN, then subscribe on PSW with your NTN, CNIC, own SIM and IRIS email, pay the Rs 500 fee and complete NADRA biometrics.

Do I need a licence to import one item from China for myself?

No. The courier or service clears it under its own registration using your CNIC. Only regulated items such as medicines or phones need extra permission.

What is an LPCO in Pakistan?

PSW's term for licences, permits, certificates and other documents from regulators such as DRAP, PSQCA or PTA that some goods need before customs releases them.

Does an NTN let me import anything?

No. An NTN is a tax registration. You also need a PSW subscription and customs registration, and restricted goods still need their own permit.

Which law controls imports in Pakistan?

The Imports and Exports (Control) Act 1950, applied through the Import Policy Order 2022 issued by the Ministry of Commerce as SRO 545(I)/2022.
