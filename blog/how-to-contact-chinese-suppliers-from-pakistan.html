How to Contact Chinese Suppliers From Pakistan (WeChat, Email, Alibaba Chat)

By Muhammad Shamshad AslamUpdated November 12, 20269 min read
Quick answer

Start on the platform: use Alibaba's chat or inquiry button, or the built-in chat on 1688 and Taobao, and keep the order and payment there. Use WeChat or email only for quick questions with a seller you have already checked. A short first message with the listing link, the model, a quantity of one and your destination gets the most useful reply.

What is the best way to contact a Chinese supplier from Pakistan?

The best first contact is the chat on the platform where you found the product, because the conversation stays on record if you need help later. On Alibaba that means the chat or inquiry button on the listing. Alibaba's Trade Assurance page says protection applies to orders made with a supplier on Alibaba.com and paid through Alibaba.com, so a conversation that starts there and ends with an order there keeps you covered.

How do you message a supplier on Alibaba?

Open the listing and use its chat or contact supplier button. You need a free buyer account. The chat works like any messenger, and the supplier sees which product you were viewing. A few points make it work better:

  • Message from the exact listing, so the product link is attached to the conversation.
  • Use simple English. Many sales staff use machine translation, and short sentences survive it better than long ones.
  • Use Request for Quotation when you cannot find the item. Alibaba's RFQ page describes it as a way to get quotes for a custom request. Describe the item, its specification and a quantity of one, and suppliers reply with offers.
  • Check the supplier before you go further. Years on the platform and verification badges matter. Our guide on how to verify a Chinese seller covers what to look for.

How do you contact sellers on 1688 and Taobao?

1688 and Taobao have their own built-in chat, called Aliwangwang (旺旺), on every listing. The sellers serve Chinese buyers, so expect replies in Chinese and expect them to ask for a Chinese delivery address.

Messaging is the easy part on these sites. Paying and delivery are harder, because they expect Chinese payment methods and ship inside China. Write to these sellers in short, simple Chinese, and ask one thing at a time: stock, variant, price, then whether delivery inside China is free (包邮吗？). If you only need one item, a buying service is usually simpler than setting up an account yourself.

How do you set up WeChat in Pakistan to talk to suppliers?

Download WeChat from your app store and sign up with your Pakistani mobile number. Some new accounts are asked for an extra verification step. WeChat's help centre describes an assisted registration in which an existing WeChat user scans a QR code from their own account to confirm you, so it helps to know someone who already uses WeChat.

Once you are in:

  1. Add the supplier by scanning their WeChat QR code or searching their WeChat ID.
  2. Send a short hello that names the platform and the listing, so they know who you are.
  3. Translate replies by long-pressing a Chinese message. WeChat's help centre describes translating chat messages into your app language this way.
  4. Copy anything agreed back into the platform chat before you place the order.

Do not use WeChat to pay. Foreign cards linked to WeChat Pay cannot send transfers to people, and a payment outside the platform has no protection.

Should you email Chinese suppliers?

Email is fine for documents, and weak for everything else. Suppliers reply faster on chat, and email carries a specific risk: if a supplier's mailbox is taken over, a fake message can say the bank account has changed. Never act on new bank details from an email. Confirm on the platform chat or by voice call first. For a single item paid on a platform, you rarely need email at all.

What should your first message say?

Keep it short and specific. A supplier answers a clear question faster than a vague one. This works on Alibaba chat, WeChat or email:

English: Hello. I want to buy 1 piece of this product for my own use: [link]. Model: [model]. Colour: [colour]. I need 220V with a European round-pin plug. Is it in stock? What is the price for 1 piece and the dispatch date? Delivery is to Pakistan. Thank you.

Simple Chinese: 您好，我想买一件这个产品，自己用：[链接]。型号：[型号]。颜色：[颜色]。需要220V，欧规插头。请问有现货吗？一件多少钱？什么时候可以发货？送到巴基斯坦。谢谢。

Saying it is one piece for your own use is honest, and it saves both sides time. For more templates, including how to ask for a better price, see our guide on negotiating with Chinese suppliers for one piece.

How do you write messages that survive machine translation?

Write as if the reader will paste your message into a translator, because many will. A few habits make a big difference:

  • One question per line. A numbered list of three questions gets three answers. A paragraph gets one.
  • Numbers in digits, with units. Write 1 piece, 220V, 30 cm, 2 kg. Avoid words like "a couple" or "a few".
  • No slang or Roman Urdu. Words like "plz", "kindly revert" or "asap" translate badly.
  • Use the seller's own terms. Copy the model name and option labels from the listing exactly as written.
  • Repeat the key detail in the order. If you agreed a plug type in chat, it should also appear on the order.
  • Send a picture of the listing option you want, if the seller seems unsure which one you mean.

When the seller replies in Chinese, translate it before answering, and if anything is unclear, ask again in one short line rather than guessing. On WeChat, long-press the message to translate it.

When is the best time to message suppliers from Pakistan?

China is three hours ahead of Pakistan, so a message sent in your morning lands in the Chinese working afternoon. Sending between about 6 am and 2 pm Pakistan time catches most offices during working hours, which is roughly 9 am to 5 pm in China. Replies slow down at weekends, during Chinese public holidays, and for weeks around Chinese New Year. Our guide to Chinese New Year and 11.11 delays shows when to expect closures.

What should you never agree to off the platform?

  • Paying a deposit by WeChat, direct Alipay transfer or a personal bank account.
  • A discount for skipping the platform. It removes your protection.
  • New bank details sent by email or chat. Confirm first.
  • A different product than the listing, agreed only in a WeChat voice note.

What mistakes do buyers make when contacting suppliers?

  • Writing one long paragraph. Machine translation mangles it.
  • Not naming the variant. Plug, voltage and colour need to be stated, or you get the default.
  • Pretending to be a large buyer. Suppliers can tell, and it wastes everyone's time.
  • Contacting ten suppliers with no listing link. One precise message to three checked sellers is better.
  • Keeping the agreement only on WeChat. Copy it into the platform order.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use WeChat in Pakistan to contact Chinese suppliers?

Yes. You can sign up with a Pakistani number, though some new accounts need an existing WeChat user to scan a QR code to confirm you. Use it for talking, not for paying.

Is it better to contact suppliers on Alibaba or WeChat?

Start on Alibaba. The conversation stays on record and the order can be paid through Alibaba with Trade Assurance. Move to WeChat only for quick questions with a seller you have checked.

Do Chinese suppliers speak English?

Most Alibaba sales staff write in English, often with machine translation. 1688 and Taobao sellers usually reply in Chinese. Short, simple sentences work best.

What time should I message Chinese suppliers from Pakistan?

China is three hours ahead, so roughly 6 am to 2 pm Pakistan time matches the Chinese working day. Expect slow replies at weekends and around Chinese New Year.

Should I give a supplier my WhatsApp number?

It is fine for chatting with a checked seller, but keep the final order and payment on the platform so you stay protected.

What should I do if a supplier emails new bank details?

Do not pay. Confirm the change on the platform chat or by voice call first, because fake bank detail emails are a common scam.
