Importing Mobile Accessories From China to Pakistan

By Muhammad Shamshad AslamUpdated November 12, 20269 min read
Quick answer

Yes, you can import mobile accessories from China to Pakistan, but most are cheap enough that shipping of Rs 5,000 to 20,000 makes a single order pointless. Importing suits higher-value items such as multi-port GaN chargers, Qi2 stands or camera rigs. Match USB PD and PPS to your phone, avoid power banks by air, and check model names.

New to importing? Read our step-by-step guide on how to import from China to Pakistan.

Can you import mobile accessories from China to Pakistan?

Yes. Chargers, cables, cases, wireless chargers, phone mounts, gimbals and similar accessories are ordinary goods and can be imported for your own use. Chinese sites list models and versions that never reach Pakistani shops. Our pillar guide on how to import from China to Pakistan explains the process. Phones themselves are a separate topic with PTA registration, covered in our phone and PTA guide.

Is it worth importing a cheap accessory?

Usually not. Shipping one parcel from China commonly costs Rs 5,000 to 20,000 depending on size and type, before duty and our fee. A case, cable or basic charger costs less than that to begin with, and similar items are on sale in every Pakistani city. Importing makes sense for accessories worth around Rs 10,000 or more, or a few different higher-value items in one order. If you want several things, such as a charger, a stand and a gimbal, putting them in one order spreads the shipping cost across all of them, which can tip the sum in your favour. Good candidates include multi-port GaN desktop chargers, certified Qi2 stands, phone gimbals, camera cages and lens kits for mobile filmmaking, and accessories for a specific new phone model that local shops have not stocked yet.

We only do single items, so accessories in quantity for a stall or shop are not something we handle. That is a commercial import, through a cargo company or the commercial route.

Which charger standard does your phone need?

Most current phones charge fastest over USB Power Delivery (USB PD), and some Samsung phones also need PPS. The USB-IF, which manages the standard, says USB PD was once limited to 100W at 20V using cables rated at 5A, and that PD Revision 3.1 extends this to up to 240W, with new fixed voltages of 28, 36 and 48V for 140, 180 and 240W. It also adds an adjustable voltage mode. A 240W charger is for laptops. A phone only takes what it can use.

Samsung is the common special case. Its Pakistan page for the 45W adapter lists Super Fast Charging 2.0 at up to 45W with PD 3.0 PDO and PPS, a 100 to 240V input, and a USB-C to USB-C 5A cable in the box. A PD charger without PPS will still charge a Samsung phone, just more slowly. When you compare listings, look for:

Brand-specific fast charging on some Chinese phones only reaches full speed with that brand's own charger and cable, so a generic PD charger may charge them at a lower rate.

What should you check on a cable?

Check its rated current and power, and buy from a brand that states them clearly. Under USB-IF's description, 100W charging uses Type-C cables rated at 5A at 20V, and 240W needs cables that meet the 240W requirements of the updated USB Type-C specification. A cable sold as "fast" with no rating may only carry 3A. Data speed is separate from charging power, so a cable that charges a laptop may still transfer files slowly. USB-IF says companies using certified USB logos must have a trademark licence and pass compliance testing, so a logo on a cheap unbranded cable is not proof of anything.

Are Qi2 wireless chargers worth importing?

They can be, for a certified model. Qi2 adds magnets for alignment, similar to MagSafe. MacRumors reported on 24 July 2025 that the Wireless Power Consortium had announced Qi2 25W, the new name for Qi2.2, raising the maximum from the original Qi2's 15W with third-party chargers. The WPC did not name specific phones at launch. Many cheap "magnetic" chargers are not Qi2 certified at all, so look for the brand's own certification claim, and check that your phone supports Qi2 or MagSafe before paying more for it.

Can you import a power bank from China?

Not easily by air. A power bank is a lithium battery shipped on its own, which is the hardest category under air cargo rules, and many sellers and couriers will not send one to Pakistan. Accessories with a small built-in battery, such as earbuds or a gimbal, ship far more easily. Our lithium battery shipping guide explains why. If you need a power bank, buying one in Pakistan is usually the practical choice.

Do Bluetooth accessories need PTA approval?

Commercial imports of Bluetooth devices do. Bureau Veritas reported in 2019 that under regulations in force since 1 December 2018, PTA removed RFID, Bluetooth, NFC and Wi-Fi access point devices from its exempt list, so imports of items such as Bluetooth speakers or peripherals would require type approval. PTA requires type approval for terminal equipment imported for commercial purposes. Anything with a SIM, such as a SIM smartwatch or a 4G hotspot, also falls under PTA's DIRBS system, which covers any SIM-based device. Our wearables guide covers earbuds and watches in more detail.

How are accessories taxed at customs?

Accessories pay duty and taxes on the customs value under their PCT code, unless the parcel is worth Rs 1,000 or less. Section 19C exempts post and courier parcels at or below that value. Above it, the whole value is dutiable. The customs value is CIF, converted at NBP rates published by FBR. When we checked in November 2026, customs duty slabs ran from 0 to 20 percent with some goods higher, additional customs duty was 0 percent on the 0 to 15 percent slabs and 2 percent on the 20 percent slab, regulatory duty applied only to listed goods and was capped at 20 percent from 1 July 2026, and sales tax was 18 percent on the value plus duties, plus advance income tax under section 148.

What are the common mistakes?

  • Ordering a case or screen protector for the Chinese model name when your phone has a different global name and size.
  • Buying a PD charger without PPS for a Samsung phone and expecting full speed.
  • Trusting unrated "fast" cables.
  • Ordering a power bank and finding it cannot fly.
  • Paying more in shipping than the accessory is worth.

What should you check when the accessory arrives?

Check the labels and the charging behaviour first. A charger should print its input range, ideally 100 to 240V, and its output wattage on the body. Many chargers come in several plug versions. Pakistan uses type C and D sockets, so a type C two-pin plug fits many sockets directly, while a type A, G or I plug needs an adapter. Plug in your phone and see whether it shows its fast charging message, such as Samsung's super fast charging notice. A cable should carry its current or wattage rating on the box or the connector. For a Qi2 stand, check that the phone snaps into place and charges without sliding. If something does not match the listing, contact us quickly, because a wrong or non-working item is covered by our refund terms.

Frequently asked questions

Is it cheaper to import phone accessories from China?

Rarely for a single cheap item, because shipping often costs Rs 5,000 to 20,000. It can make sense for accessories worth around Rs 10,000 or more.

Which charger do I need for a Samsung phone?

For 45W Super Fast Charging 2.0, Samsung lists USB PD 3.0 with PPS and a 5A USB-C cable. Other PD chargers work but charge more slowly.

Can I import a power bank from China to Pakistan?

It is difficult by air, because a power bank is a battery shipped on its own. Many sellers and couriers refuse it, so buying locally is usually easier.

What is Qi2 25W?

It is the Wireless Power Consortium's name for Qi2.2, announced in July 2025, raising magnetic wireless charging from 15W to 25W on supported phones.

Do Bluetooth speakers need PTA approval?

For commercial imports, yes. PTA removed Bluetooth devices from its exempt list from December 2018, so imports for sale need type approval.

Is there duty on a small accessories parcel?

Post or courier parcels worth Rs 1,000 or less pay no duties or taxes under section 19C. Above that, the whole value is taxed.
