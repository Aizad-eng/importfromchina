Can You Import Furniture From China to Pakistan?

By Muhammad Shamshad AslamUpdated November 12, 20269 min read
Quick answer

Yes, but only some furniture makes sense as a single import. An office chair, gaming chair, lamp, small side table or flat-pack piece can be imported from China to Pakistan by air. Sofas, beds, wardrobes and dining sets are charged on volume, not weight, so air shipping usually costs more than the furniture. For those, a sea cargo company is the realistic route.

New to importing? Read our step-by-step guide on how to import from China to Pakistan.

Can you import furniture from China to Pakistan as one piece?

Yes, you can, and people do, but the item's size decides whether it is worth it. China makes a huge range of furniture, from ergonomic office chairs to designer lamps, and much of it is listed one piece at a time on Taobao, 1688, JD.com and Alibaba. The catch is shipping. Furniture is big and mostly air inside the box, and air couriers charge for that space.

As a rule, a single piece that ships flat-packed in a box of moderate size and is worth a fair amount is a candidate. A large assembled piece is not. For the general process of buying from China as an individual, see our guide on how to import from China to Pakistan.

Why is furniture charged by size rather than weight?

Air couriers charge whichever is higher, the actual weight or the volumetric weight. DHL's formula is length times width times height in centimetres, divided by 5,000. Air cargo commonly uses 6,000 instead. A box that is light but large is charged as if it were heavy. Our explainer on volumetric weight walks through the calculation.

The examples below use illustrative carton sizes to show the effect. The real figure depends on the seller's carton, which is why you should always ask for the carton size and gross weight.

Shipping a normal parcel from China usually costs around Rs 5,000 to 20,000 depending on size and type. A big office chair sits at the top of that range. A sofa goes far past it.

Which furniture pieces are worth importing by air?

The pieces that make sense are compact, valuable or hard to find locally:

  • Ergonomic office chairs and gaming chairs, especially specific models that are not sold in Pakistan. These ship flat-packed and are assembled at home.
  • Lamps and light fittings, including designer floor and pendant lamps that pack small.
  • Small tables, stools and shelves that ship flat.
  • Chair parts: gas lifts, armrests, castors and mechanisms, which are small and fix an existing chair. Our guide on gym and fitness equipment shows the same weight logic for another bulky category.
  • Standing desk frames, sometimes, but they are heavy steel, so check the gross weight first.

An item worth around Rs 10,000 or more is where single imports start to make sense. Below that, shipping usually eats the saving.

When should you use sea freight instead?

Use sea freight for anything large, heavy or numerous, such as a sofa, bed, wardrobe, dining set or several pieces for one house. Sea shipping is usually charged by volume at a much lower rate per cubic metre, though it takes weeks longer and needs a cargo company to handle the container space, port clearance and delivery from Karachi.

Sea freight also suits pieces that are heavy for their size, such as marble tops, solid wood tables and steel cabinets, where even the actual weight makes air shipping costly. Expect to arrange delivery from the port or the cargo company's warehouse, and allow for the longer wait when you plan a move or a new house.

We only ship single items by air, so for a sofa or a houseful of furniture we are not the right service. A cargo company that groups goods into shared containers is the realistic route.

What should you check on a furniture listing?

Read the full specification and ask the seller for anything missing. The details that matter are:

  • Carton size and gross weight, so you can estimate shipping before you decide.
  • Flat-pack or assembled. Assembled pieces cost far more to ship.
  • Dimensions in centimetres of the finished piece, checked against your room.
  • Materials: solid wood, board, metal, fabric or leather, and the finish.
  • Maximum load for chairs and shelves.
  • Hardware and tools included, and whether the instructions are in English or pictures.
  • Colour variant, selected before you copy the link.
  • Packing: ask whether the seller uses foam corners and strong cartons for export.

What duty and tax applies to furniture?

Normal import duty and taxes apply above Rs 1,000. Under section 19C of the Customs Act, post and courier parcels worth Rs 1,000 or less pay no duties or taxes, which rules out almost all furniture. Above that, customs assesses the whole value by tariff code.

For FY 2026-27 customs duty slabs are 0, 5, 10, 15 and 20 percent, with some goods higher. Additional customs duty is 2 percent on the 20 percent slab and 4 percent on 25 percent and above. Regulatory duty applies to listed goods only and was capped at 20 percent from 1 July 2026. Sales tax is 18 percent on the value plus duties, and withholding income tax is added. We could not confirm the current rates for the main furniture headings from an official FBR page in November 2026, so check the tariff code for your item, and expect finished consumer goods like furniture to carry a significant tax share.

How should you assemble and check furniture when it arrives?

Open the carton carefully and check everything before you start building. Lay out the parts and count them against the instruction sheet, including every bolt, washer and tool.

  • Look over each board, leg and frame for cracks, dents or bent metal before assembly, while the damage is easy to see.
  • Follow the assembly order in the instructions. Chairs in particular need the base, gas lift and mechanism fitted in the right sequence.
  • Tighten bolts in stages rather than fully at once, then go round again once the piece stands level.
  • Do not exceed the stated maximum load on chairs and shelves.
  • Keep the carton and packing until you are sure nothing is missing or damaged.

If something arrives broken or missing, report it straight away with clear details, as it is much easier to resolve early.

What can go wrong with furniture from China?

  • Transit damage. Corners and legs take knocks. Good packing matters more than with most goods.
  • Missing hardware. Count the bolts and fittings as soon as you open the box.
  • Size surprises. A chair or desk can feel bigger or smaller than expected. Measure your space.
  • Colour and finish. Screens and listing images vary. Ask the seller for the material name or colour code.
  • Smell. New foam and board can smell for a few days. Air the piece before use.
  • Weight underestimated. Steel frames and chair bases are heavier than they look.

Frequently asked questions

Can I import one office chair from China to Pakistan?

Yes. A flat-packed office or gaming chair is one of the more sensible furniture imports by air, especially for a good model not sold locally.

Is it worth importing a sofa from China by air?

No. A sofa's volumetric weight is very high, so air shipping usually costs more than the sofa. Use a sea cargo company for large pieces.

How is furniture shipping calculated?

Couriers charge the higher of actual weight and volumetric weight. DHL divides length times width times height in centimetres by 5,000.

What duty is charged on furniture in Pakistan?

Customs duty by tariff code, plus 18 percent sales tax and income tax, and possibly regulatory duty. Check the current rate for your item's tariff code.

What should I ask the seller before ordering furniture?

Ask for the carton size, gross weight, materials, maximum load, included hardware and how it is packed for export.

Can you ship furniture for a whole house?

No. We only handle single items by air. For a houseful of furniture, a sea cargo company is the realistic route.
