Is It Cheaper to Buy From China or in Pakistan? An Honest Answer

By importfromchina.shop teamUpdated October 17, 20269 min read
Quick answer

It depends on the item's price and size. For cheap or bulky things, buying in Pakistan is usually cheaper, because shipping one item from China usually costs Rs 5,000 to 20,000 before customs. For items worth around Rs 10,000 or more that carry a big local markup, are hard to find, or come in better versions in China, importing is often cheaper. Compare the full landed price, not the listing price, and count warranty and returns too.

Is it cheaper to buy from China or in Pakistan?

The honest answer is that it depends on three things: what the item costs, how big it is, and what you would give up by importing it. The China price is almost always lower. The question is whether it is still lower once you add the cost of getting one item here.

For cheap items, small accessories and anything bulky, buying in Pakistan usually wins. For specialist or higher-value items, roughly Rs 10,000 and up, importing often wins, especially when local sellers are few or charge heavily for the same thing. Everything in between needs a quick calculation, which this guide shows you how to do.

If you are comparing with Daraz specifically, our guide on Daraz vs importing from China goes into that marketplace. This page covers the wider question, including shops, markets and social media sellers.

Why does the same item cost less in China?

Because you are buying closer to where it is made. Many suppliers on 1688 are factories and wholesalers, and their prices are tiered, so the price falls as the quantity rises. A Pakistani shop selling the same item has paid for:

  • Buying in China, plus its own shipping and customs on the whole batch.
  • Shop costs such as rent, staff and electricity, or platform commission if it sells online.
  • Unsold stock and returns, which have to be covered by the items that do sell.
  • A margin, which is fair, since the shop takes the risk and gives you the item today.

None of that means local prices are unfair. It means the gap is biggest for items with few local sellers, where competition does not push the margin down.

What do you have to add to the China price?

For one item, these are the costs that sit between the listing price and your door.

  • Shipping. Usually Rs 5,000 to 20,000 for one item, depending on its size and type. Bulky boxes are charged on volume, not weight. Our guide to the cost to ship one item from China to Pakistan has worked examples.
  • Customs. Post and courier parcels worth Rs 1,000 or less are duty-free under section 19C of the Customs Act, as set by the Finance Act 2025. Above that, duty and taxes apply depending on the product. See our guide to customs duty from China to Pakistan for rates.
  • Payment costs. If you pay with a card, your bank converts at its own rate and may add charges.
  • A service fee, if someone buys and ships the item for you.

The total of the item and all of these is the landed price. It is the only number worth comparing with a local price.

How do you compare China and Pakistan prices fairly?

Use one simple test: China price + shipping + customs + any fee, compared with the best local price for the same item and version.

Two examples show how quickly the answer flips. The prices are made up for illustration.

  • A Rs 4,000 gadget that sells for Rs 9,000 in Pakistan. The Rs 5,000 gap looks big. But shipping alone starts at around Rs 5,000, so the saving is gone before customs. Buy it locally.
  • A Rs 30,000 specialist tool that sells for Rs 65,000 in Pakistan, if you can find it. Even at the top of the shipping range, and with customs added, there is room for a real saving. Get an exact landed price before you decide.

The lesson is that the percentage gap does not matter much. What matters is the rupee gap compared with a shipping cost that does not shrink for cheap items.

Does the rupee exchange rate change the answer?

It can, at the margin. Chinese sellers price in yuan, or in dollars on Alibaba and AliExpress, so the rupee price of the same item moves when the rupee moves. A local shop's price moves too, but more slowly, because it bought its stock at an older rate and reprices when the next batch arrives.

That has two practical effects. First, a China price you saw last month may not be the rupee price today, so recheck it before you compare. Second, if you pay with your own card, your bank converts the amount at its own rate on the day the payment settles, and you only see the final rupee figure on your statement. Our guide on how RMB to PKR prices work explains this in more detail.

What about warranty, returns and after-sales?

This is where buying in Pakistan earns its higher price, and where many comparisons go wrong.

  • Warranty. A local seller or official distributor can repair or replace an item here. A Chinese seller's warranty usually means sending it back to China, which is rarely practical. Check what the seller actually offers before you buy.
  • Returns. Returning a wrong size or a change of mind to China costs more than most items are worth.
  • Time. A local shop gives it to you today. Importing one item by air takes 7 to 15 days after the seller dispatches, and much longer by post.

So if the saving is small, the local option is often the better deal even when the China total is a little lower.

When is buying in Pakistan the clear winner?

  • The item costs less than a few thousand rupees.
  • It is big or light for its size, so shipping is charged on volume.
  • Many local sellers stock the same model, so the margin is already low.
  • You want an official warranty or easy returns.
  • It is a phone and the local price already includes PTA tax.
  • You need it this week.

When does buying from China win?

  • The item is worth around Rs 10,000 or more and local sellers charge much more for it.
  • It is not sold in Pakistan at all. Our guide on how to get an item that is not sold in Pakistan covers this case.
  • The local version is a lower grade, an older model or the wrong variant.
  • It is compact and dense, so shipping is near the bottom of the range.
  • You can wait a week or two.

How do you check the real price before you decide?

  1. Find the best local price. Check Daraz, the relevant market and social media sellers, for the exact model.
  2. Find the same item in China. Match the model number, version and accessories.
  3. Estimate shipping. Use the package size and the volumetric formula, length x width x height in cm divided by 6,000.
  4. Add customs if the item is worth more than Rs 1,000.
  5. Add any fee, then compare the landed price with the local price.
  6. Value the warranty. If the gap is small, local usually wins.

What mistakes make importing look cheaper than it is?

  • Comparing the listing price. The China price without shipping and customs is not a fair comparison.
  • Using the bulk price. The price for 100 pieces is not the price for one.
  • Forgetting volumetric weight. A light item in a big box costs a lot to fly.
  • Assuming small parcels are duty-free. The duty-free limit is only Rs 1,000.
  • Ignoring PTA. A cheap imported phone can cost much more once it is registered.
  • Not valuing the warranty. A local warranty can be worth more than a small saving.

Frequently asked questions

Is it cheaper to buy things from China or from Pakistan?

For cheap or bulky items, Pakistan is usually cheaper once you add shipping, which is usually Rs 5,000 to 20,000 for one item. For specialist or higher-value items, roughly Rs 10,000 and up, buying from China is often cheaper, especially when few local sellers stock them.

Why are things so much cheaper on Chinese websites than in Pakistani shops?

You are buying closer to the factory. A Pakistani shop has paid for shipping and customs on its stock, plus rent, staff, unsold stock and a margin. The gap is biggest where few local sellers compete.

Is it worth importing a cheap item from China to Pakistan?

Usually not. Shipping one item from China usually costs at least around Rs 5,000, so for an item that costs a few thousand rupees the shipping can wipe out the saving. Temu's Pakistan site can be an exception for low-cost items.

How do I calculate whether importing from China will save me money?

Add the China price, shipping, customs if the item is worth more than Rs 1,000, and any service fee. Compare that landed price with the best local price for the same model, and give some weight to a local warranty.

Is it cheaper to buy a phone from China or in Pakistan?

Often Pakistan, if the local price already includes PTA tax and an official warranty. An imported phone needs PTA registration, and the tax depends on the model, so a cheap China price can end up higher once registered.

Do I lose the warranty if I buy from China instead of Pakistan?

Usually you lose easy local warranty service. Claims typically mean dealing with the Chinese seller, and sending an item back to China is rarely practical. That is why a small saving often is not worth it.
