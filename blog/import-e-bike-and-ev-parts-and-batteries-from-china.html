Importing E-Bike and EV Parts and Batteries From China to Pakistan

By Muhammad Shamshad AslamUpdated October 23, 20269 min read
Quick answer

Most e-bike and EV parts are easy to import from China because they contain no battery. Controllers, displays, throttles, sensors and chargers ship by air like other electronics, and motors are fine but heavy. Loose lithium battery packs are the problem: shipped alone as UN 3480, they cannot go on passenger aircraft without approval, and most e-bike packs are well above 100Wh.

Which e-bike and EV parts can you import from China easily?

Anything without a lithium battery inside is straightforward. That covers most of what people need to fix or upgrade an e-bike or e-scooter, and a lot of small EV accessories:

  • Controllers. The box that turns battery power into motor power. Light, compact and easy to ship.
  • Displays and control pads. Usually matched to a specific controller protocol, so buy them together.
  • Throttles, brake levers with cut-off switches and pedal-assist sensors. Small parts, often the first to fail.
  • Chargers. A charger is not a battery. It ships like any other power adapter.
  • Wiring harnesses, connectors and cables. Check connector type against your bike.
  • Motors. Allowed, but heavy, which changes the shipping maths.

The part that causes trouble is the battery pack, and to a lesser extent anything with a pack built in, such as a complete e-bike or scooter.

Why do loose lithium battery packs rarely fly?

Because air rules treat a battery shipped on its own as the highest-risk category. IATA's Battery Guidance Document, revised for the 2026 regulations, says lithium-ion batteries packed by themselves, under Packing Instruction 965, cannot be shipped on a passenger aircraft unless the relevant authorities approve it, and must be shipped at no more than 30% state of charge. The same document says packages of loose lithium-ion batteries under the fully regulated sections must carry a Cargo Aircraft Only label.

IATA also makes clear that battery packs count as batteries. Units with several cells, commonly called battery packs, modules or assemblies, whose main job is to power another piece of equipment, are treated as batteries. An e-bike pack is exactly that.

Couriers add their own limits. DHL Express says not all lithium batteries are accepted in its network, only pre-approved account holders can ship them, and defective, damaged or recalled batteries are not permitted. So a seller may not be able to book a loose pack on an air service at all, even if the listing says it ships to Pakistan. Our guide to lithium battery shipping rules explains the categories in full.

What do UN 3480, UN 3481 and UN 3556 mean for your order?

They are the codes that decide how your parcel is treated. IATA uses them like this:

The 2026 change matters for e-bike buyers in one place. From 1 January 2026, IATA says lithium-ion batteries packed with equipment, and vehicles powered by lithium-ion batteries, must be offered for air transport at reduced charge unless the relevant states approve otherwise.

How big is an e-bike battery in watt-hours?

Usually several hundred watt-hours, far above the 100Wh line in IATA's rules. Watt-hours are the nominal voltage multiplied by the capacity in amp-hours. A common 48V 14Ah pack is 48 × 14 = 672Wh. A 36V 10Ah pack is 360Wh.

IATA's flowcharts use 20Wh per cell and 100Wh per battery as the key thresholds. Batteries above 100Wh fall into the fully regulated category, with specification packaging, hazard labels and a shipper's declaration. A phone battery sits well under that line. Almost every e-bike pack sits well over it, which is why a spare pack is about the hardest consumer item to send by air.

If you only need new cells to rebuild a pack, the same rule applies. Loose cells shipped by themselves are UN 3480.

Are motors worth shipping by air?

They can be, but weight sets the cost. Hub motors and mid-drive units are dense lumps of copper, steel and magnets. Retailer spec sheets list the popular Bafang BBS02 mid-drive at roughly 4 to 4.6 kg for the motor alone, and a rear hub motor laced into a wheel is bulky as well as heavy.

Air shipping from China is charged on actual weight or volumetric weight, whichever is higher, so a wheel-built hub motor is charged for its size too. A motor without a wheel ships more cheaply. Our guide to the cost of shipping one item from China explains how weight and size turn into rupees.

How do you match voltage between battery, controller, display and charger?

Everything on the bike runs at the battery's system voltage, so match that first. E-bike systems are commonly 36V, 48V or 52V. The controller, display and motor must be rated for the battery you have, and the charger must be built for that battery's voltage and chemistry. A 48V charger on a 36V pack, or the other way round, can damage the pack or start a fire.

The wall side of the charger is a separate check. Read the input rating on the charger label. Pakistan's mains is 220 to 240V at 50Hz, so a charger rated 100 to 240V or 220V input works here. Check the plug as well. A Chinese or European plug needs a proper adapter for Pakistani sockets. For other mains-powered gear, see our voltage guide for appliances from China.

Also match the connectors. Controllers and displays from one brand often use their own communication protocol, so a display that fits physically may not talk to your controller. Buy the controller and display as a matched set where you can.

What about EV car parts and chargers?

Small EV accessories are generally simple, but check the charging standard first. China uses its own GB/T standard, which has separate AC and DC charging ports on China-market cars, while many export-market cars use a single combined inlet such as CCS2. GB/T AC connectors look similar to the European Type 2 but are not interchangeable.

So before ordering a charging cable, portable charger or adapter for an EV in Pakistan, look at the inlet on your own car and buy for that. Portable chargers and wallboxes have no traction battery inside, so they ship like other electrical equipment, though they can be heavy.

Traction batteries and battery modules for EV cars are a different matter. They are large lithium-ion batteries shipped by themselves, which puts them under the same UN 3480 rules as e-bike packs at a much bigger scale. They are not a realistic single-item air shipment.

Can a whole e-bike or scooter be shipped?

It can, but it is a regulated, bulky shipment. A complete e-bike or e-scooter with a lithium-ion battery is normally shipped under IATA's rules for battery-powered vehicles, as UN 3556, Vehicle, lithium-ion battery powered. If the battery is above 100Wh, which is normal for adult e-bikes, it must travel at no more than 30% charge or with the gauge showing no more than 25%, and higher charge needs state approval.

Add the box size of a full bike and air shipping one is rarely sensible. Sea freight is often used for complete e-bikes, with a much longer timeline.

A step-by-step check before you order parts

  1. Write down your system voltage, controller model and connector types from the parts already on the bike.
  2. Choose parts rated for that voltage, ideally from one brand as a matched set.
  3. Check whether anything in your cart contains a lithium cell. Remove spare packs and loose cells from an air order.
  4. For a charger, confirm the output matches your battery and the input covers 220 to 240V.
  5. For motors, ask the seller for the packed weight and box size, not just the motor weight.
  6. Ask the seller in writing how they will ship to Pakistan, and by which carrier.

Common mistakes with e-bike and EV parts

  • Adding a spare battery to an air order. It can hold up the whole parcel.
  • Buying a display that does not match the controller. Protocols differ between brands.
  • Using a charger from another bike. Voltage and chemistry must match the pack.
  • Ignoring the box size of a hub motor wheel. Volumetric weight can double the cost.
  • Accepting a battery declared as something else. Undeclared dangerous goods can be held, returned or destroyed.

Frequently asked questions

Can I ship an e-bike battery from China to Pakistan by air?

Rarely. A battery shipped by itself is UN 3480, which IATA says cannot go on passenger aircraft without approval and must be at 30% charge or less. Most couriers and sellers will not take one by air.

Which e-bike parts are easy to import from China?

Parts with no lithium battery: controllers, displays, throttles, brake levers, pedal-assist sensors, wiring and chargers. Motors are allowed but heavy, so shipping costs more.

Can I ship an e-bike charger from China?

Yes. A charger is not a battery and ships like other power adapters. Check that its output matches your battery and its input covers Pakistan's 220 to 240V mains.

What is the difference between UN 3480 and UN 3481?

UN 3480 is a lithium-ion battery shipped by itself. UN 3481 is a lithium-ion battery contained in or packed with equipment, which is much easier to ship by air.

How do I work out my e-bike battery's watt-hours?

Multiply the nominal voltage by the amp-hours. A 48V 14Ah pack is 672Wh, far above the 100Wh line where the fully regulated air rules start.

Will a Chinese EV charging cable work on my car in Pakistan?

Only if it matches your car's inlet. China-market cars use GB/T with separate AC and DC ports, and GB/T AC is not interchangeable with Type 2, so check your car before ordering.
