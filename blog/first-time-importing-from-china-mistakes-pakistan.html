12 Mistakes First-Time Importers From China Make in Pakistan

By Muhammad Shamshad AslamUpdated November 12, 20268 min read
Quick answer

The most common first-time mistakes when importing from China to Pakistan are comparing the listing price with a local price, ordering items too cheap to ship, ignoring box size, missing the Rs 1,000 duty line, under-declaring value, buying restricted items, mismatched names, commercial quantities on a personal route, and paying outside platform protection. Each is easy to avoid with a check before paying.

What mistakes do first-time importers from China make most often?

Most first-time mistakes come from one of four places: the price, the rules, the paperwork or the timing. None of them is hard to avoid once you know about it. Which price mistakes catch people out?

The price mistakes all come from looking at one number instead of the full cost.

1. Comparing the listing price with a local price. The Chinese price leaves out shipping, customs duty, sales tax, income tax and handling. At 2026-27 rates, duties and taxes alone commonly add about a fifth to two thirds of the customs value, depending on the tariff code. Compare the landed price with the Pakistani price, not the listing price. Our cost guide shows worked examples.

2. Ordering items too cheap to ship. Shipping one item from China usually costs Rs 5,000 to 20,000. A Rs 2,000 item can end up costing four times its price. Importing one item usually makes sense from around Rs 10,000 upwards, or when the item is not sold in Pakistan at all.

3. Ignoring box size. Air shipping charges the higher of actual weight and volumetric weight, which is based on the box's dimensions. A light lampshade in a large box can cost more to fly than a heavy tool in a small one.

What do people get wrong about duty?

People get the small-parcel rule and the honest-value rule wrong more than anything else.

4. Misreading the Rs 1,000 line. Section 19C of the Customs Act, as amended by the Finance Act 2025, says no duties and taxes are demanded on post or courier goods worth Rs 1,000 or less. Many people still quote the old Rs 5,000 limit. And above Rs 1,000 there is no allowance: the whole value is dutiable, not just the part above the line.

5. Under-declaring the value. Asking a seller to write a lower price or mark a purchase as a gift is a customs offence, and it rarely works. Customs can use a valuation ruling instead of your invoice, and a value that looks wrong leads to closer review and delay. Declare what you paid.

Which product mistakes cause the most trouble?

The worst product mistakes are buying something that cannot be cleared, and buying something that will not work.

6. Buying a restricted item. The Import Policy Order bans some goods and allows others only with a regulator's permission. Medicines need DRAP permission, which for personal use DRAP handles through an NOC on its online portal. Phones need PTA registration, and PTA's 2021 regulations, as reported by ProPakistani, allow a person five mobile devices for personal use in a calendar year. Drones need prior permission, counterfeit branded goods are seized, and loose batteries and power banks are restricted on flights. See what you cannot import from China.

7. Buying a version that does not work here. Check the voltage on the label, the plug type, and whether the product is a China-only version whose app is in Chinese or locked to a Chinese account. A US-market appliance rated for 110V only needs a converter here.

8. Expecting Taobao or 1688 to ship to Pakistan. Their sellers ship within China and take Chinese payment methods. Items from those sites need someone to buy and ship them for you.

What paperwork mistakes do first-timers make?

The paperwork for one parcel is light, so the errors are small but costly.

9. Names and numbers that do not match. The courier identifies you by your CNIC. Since 15 August 2026, under SRO 918(I)/2026 as reported by ProPakistani, the import manifest carries the importer's NTN or FTN, with a CNIC or passport number accepted for small parcel importers without an NTN. A misspelt name or a wrong digit can hold a parcel until it is corrected.

10. Commercial quantities on a personal route. FBR defines a commercial quantity as goods imported prima facie for trading or gain, not for personal use or gift. Its travel guide says such goods are released only on payment of a fine of 30% of their value on top of duty and taxes. Ten identical units do not look personal. If you need stock, use the commercial route with an NTN and a customs agent; our import checklist covers both routes.

How do people lose money on payment?

11. Paying outside platform protection. Paying a seller directly by bank transfer or through a chat link removes the platform's dispute process. Pay inside the official checkout, or pay a known service in Pakistan that buys for you. Also be wary of SMS messages asking for a "customs fee" through a link. A genuine courier will show you the customs assessment and tell you how to pay through its own channels.

Card payments carry their own surprises: the bank's exchange rate and charges are often worse than the rate you see online, and some cards are declined for international use until you switch it on in your banking app.

When is the worst time to order from China?

12. Ordering at the wrong time. Chinese New Year, which falls between late January and mid February, closes many factories and logistics companies for weeks. The 11.11 sale in November fills flights out of China and slows deliveries for some time after. Eid holidays in Pakistan can also hold a parcel that lands just before them. If you need something by a date, order well before these periods.

What should you do if you have already made one of these mistakes?

Act quickly and through official channels, because most problems get harder to fix the longer a parcel sits. What to do depends on the mistake:

  • A parcel held for documents. Reply to the courier's own message with your order receipt or invoice and a clear CNIC copy. Do not send documents to a number or link you cannot confirm belongs to the courier.
  • A wrong name or CNIC digit. Contact the courier with the tracking number and your correct details. A simple typing error is usually fixable before customs assesses the parcel.
  • A restricted item already shipped. Ask the regulator what it needs. For medicines for personal use, DRAP takes NOC applications on its online portal. Without the permission, the item will not be released.
  • A duty bill you did not expect. Ask for the customs assessment that shows the tariff code and value used, and check it against your invoice before paying.
  • A seller who stops replying. If you paid inside the platform, open a dispute there within its time limit. If you paid outside it, your options are much weaker.

Frequently asked questions

What is the biggest mistake first-time importers make?

Comparing the Chinese listing price with a Pakistani shop price. The fair comparison is the landed price after shipping, duty and taxes.

Is the duty-free limit for parcels Rs 5,000?

No longer. Since the Finance Act 2025, section 19C waives duties and taxes only on post or courier goods worth Rs 1,000 or less.

Can I ask the seller to declare a lower value?

You should not. Under-declaring is a customs offence, and customs can use a valuation ruling instead of the invoice anyway.

How many phones can I import for personal use?

PTA's 2021 regulations, as reported by ProPakistani, allow five mobile devices for personal use in a calendar year, and each needs PTA registration.

Is it worth importing a cheap item from China?

Rarely. Shipping one item usually costs Rs 5,000 to 20,000, so it makes most sense for items worth around Rs 10,000 or more.

When should I avoid ordering from China?

Around Chinese New Year, late January to mid February, and in the weeks after the 11.11 sale, when factories close or flights are full.
