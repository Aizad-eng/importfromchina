Is AliExpress Legit and Safe to Use in Pakistan?

By Muhammad Shamshad AslamUpdated October 23, 202610 min read
Quick answer

Verdict: yes, AliExpress is a legitimate Alibaba Group marketplace and is generally safe to use from Pakistan if you pay only inside the app or website. The real risks are weak sellers, counterfeit branded goods that Customs can seize, slow or lost parcels and duty on arrival. Buyer protection covers non-delivery within the guaranteed window and items not as described, reported within 15 days.

Is AliExpress legit and safe to use in Pakistan?

Yes, AliExpress is a real marketplace, and buying through the official app or website is generally safe. It belongs to Alibaba Group, which lists it alongside Taobao, Tmall and 1688 at the foot of its pages, and its iPhone app is published by Alibaba.com Singapore E-Commerce Private Limited. Payment goes through AliExpress, not to the seller directly, and there is a dispute system if things go wrong.

Safe does not mean risk-free. AliExpress is made up of thousands of independent stores, and the problems Pakistani buyers run into are about which seller you pick, what you buy and what happens to the parcel after it lands. The sections below take each risk in turn.

Are there fake sellers on AliExpress?

There are weak and misleading sellers, though outright fake stores that never ship are less common, because AliExpress holds the payment and refunds non-delivery. The bigger issue is listings that look better than the product.

Two things we noticed on listings in October 2026 are worth knowing:

  • Some product pages say the page aggregates consumer reviews and sales volume of similar items from multiple merchants. In other words, the review count you see may not belong only to the store you are buying from.
  • Store details on the listing show the seller's store name and whether it is registered as a trader. Open the store page and look at how long it has been selling and its overall feedback, not just the item rating.

Buyer reviews that include real pictures of the item, recent dates and specific comments are more useful than a big star rating. Our guide on spotting fake listings goes deeper, including how to read a store's history and feedback score.

Will I get a counterfeit, and will customs take it?

You can, if you buy a big brand at a price that is too good to be true, and yes, Customs can seize it. Section 15 of the Customs Act prohibits bringing into Pakistan any counterfeit product and goods carrying a counterfeit trade mark. A seized replica is simply lost, and AliExpress buyer protection is not designed to compensate you for goods your own country confiscates.

The safer path is to buy unbranded goods for what they are, or buy branded goods from the brand's official store on AliExpress. If a famous brand's product costs a fraction of its usual price, assume it is not genuine.

Is it safe to use my Pakistani card on AliExpress?

Generally yes, as long as you enter it only on the official site or app. AliExpress product pages state that it does not share your personal details with third parties without your consent. WorldFirst's July 2026 guide for Pakistan calls a Visa or Mastercard the most reliable checkout option.

Simple precautions help more than anything else:

  • Use a debit card with a modest balance, or a card with a low online limit, rather than your main account.
  • Turn on international online use only when you need it, if your banking app allows that.
  • Never type your card details into a link sent by message, even if it uses the AliExpress name.
  • Expect your bank to add a foreign transaction fee, and check its schedule of charges.

What if my AliExpress order never arrives?

You can claim a refund. AliExpress's Buyer Protection page says an item that does not arrive, or is lost in transit, within the guaranteed delivery window shown on your order detail page qualifies for a refund. Note that date when you order.

Parcels to Pakistan do go missing or get stuck, most often because of an incomplete address, a customs hold or a refused delivery. Give a full address with area, street, landmark and a working mobile number. If the window passes, open a dispute from the order page rather than waiting. Our guide to AliExpress delivery times explains what is normal.

Will I be charged customs duty on arrival?

Only if the parcel is worth more than Rs 1,000. Section 19C of the Customs Act, unchanged by the Finance Act 2026, exempts post and courier parcels up to that value. Above it, the whole value is taxed: customs duty by product code, then 18% sales tax, then advance income tax. Pakistan Post or the courier collects it before handing over the parcel.

This is not a scam, but it surprises people. See our AliExpress customs duty guide for worked examples by item value.

Product pages for Pakistan said items were eligible for returns and refunds within the designated order protection period, so check that period on your own order.

Can I return an item to AliExpress from Pakistan?

Technically yes, practically rarely. Unless the Free Return service is offered on your order, you pay the postage back to China, which for most items costs more than the refund is worth. For a cheap item, ask in the dispute whether a refund without a return is possible. Keep clear pictures of the item and its packaging as evidence before you open the dispute, and describe exactly how the item differs from the listing, quoting the listing's own specification where you can. Vague complaints are harder to win.

Are "AliExpress Pakistan" pages and parcel payment messages a scam?

Often, yes. Buyer protection covers orders placed on the AliExpress app or website. A Facebook or Instagram page using the AliExpress name is a third party, and nothing you buy from it is covered.

Fake delivery messages are the other trap. Pakistan Post says it collects duty and taxes only through its own manual system, has no online system or call centre to collect money, and never asks for online payment through its website. A text asking you to pay a fee through a link to release your AliExpress parcel is not from Pakistan Post.

Common mistakes that make AliExpress unsafe

  • Paying outside the platform. A seller who asks for a bank transfer to "save fees" removes your protection.
  • Confirming delivery before checking the item. Open the parcel and check it works first.
  • Missing the 15-day window. Report problems soon after the parcel arrives.
  • Buying branded replicas. Counterfeit goods can be seized under section 15 of the Customs Act, whatever the listing says.

Frequently asked questions

Is AliExpress a real company or a scam?

It is a real marketplace that belongs to Alibaba Group. The risk is in individual sellers and listings, not the platform itself.

How many days do I have to open a dispute on AliExpress?

AliExpress's Buyer Protection page says items not as described or damaged must be reported within 15 days of receiving them. Non-arrival is covered within the guaranteed delivery window on your order page.

Is it safe to put my debit card on AliExpress from Pakistan?

Generally yes on the official app or site. Use a Visa or Mastercard with a modest limit and never enter card details through a link sent by message.

Will Pakistan Customs seize fake branded items from AliExpress?

They can. Section 15 of the Customs Act prohibits importing counterfeit products and goods with a counterfeit trade mark.

Does AliExpress refund if my parcel is lost in Pakistan?

Yes, if it does not arrive within the guaranteed delivery window shown on your order detail page. Open a dispute from the order page.

Is a message asking me to pay customs online for my AliExpress parcel real?

Treat it as a scam. Pakistan Post says it collects duty only through its own manual system and never asks for online payment.
