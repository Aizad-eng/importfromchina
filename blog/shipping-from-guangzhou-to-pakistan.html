Shipping From Guangzhou to Pakistan

By Muhammad Shamshad AslamUpdated November 12, 20268 min read
Quick answer

Shipping from Guangzhou to Pakistan goes by air from Guangzhou Baiyun International Airport, a major cargo hub, or by sea from the Port of Guangzhou, including Nansha. For one item, the seller ships it by express courier or to a forwarder's warehouse, and it flies to Pakistan. Large loads go by sea to Karachi. Customs applies in Pakistan either way.

Why do so many China shipments start in Guangzhou?

Many shipments start in Guangzhou because it is the capital of Guangdong, one of China's main manufacturing provinces, and it has a large cargo airport, a major seaport and a long trading history. The Canton Fair, officially the China Import and Export Fair, has been held in Guangzhou twice a year since 1957, and many suppliers that sell abroad are based in the city or nearby.

For a buyer in Pakistan, that means a seller on Alibaba, 1688 or Taobao is quite often in Guangzhou or a neighbouring city, and the item can reach an airport or forwarder quickly. Neighbouring Shenzhen is covered in our separate guide on shipping from Yiwu and Shenzhen to Pakistan.

How does one item get from a Guangzhou seller to Pakistan?

One item from a Guangzhou seller reaches Pakistan in one of two ways: the seller books an international express courier directly, or the seller sends it by Chinese domestic courier to a forwarder's warehouse, which flies it to Pakistan. Which one depends on the platform.

  • Alibaba.com sellers often offer DHL, FedEx or UPS directly to Pakistan for single pieces and samples.
  • 1688 and Taobao sellers ship only within China, so the item must go to a forwarder or buying service with a warehouse in China first.
  • AliExpress sellers use AliExpress's own shipping options to Pakistan where available.

If the forwarder's warehouse is in Guangzhou or nearby, a local seller's parcel can reach it quickly by domestic courier. If the warehouse is in another province, allow extra days. Our guide on how to buy from 1688 in Pakistan covers the domestic step in detail.

Which airport do Guangzhou parcels fly from?

Air shipments from Guangzhou usually leave from Guangzhou Baiyun International Airport (code CAN), about 28 km north of the city centre. It handled about 2.44 million tonnes of cargo in 2025, up 2.4% on the year before, according to its published figures.

Baiyun is a hub for China Southern Airlines and also for FedEx Express, which matters if your seller books FedEx. For a single parcel, the slow part is rarely the flight. It is the seller's handling time and customs in Pakistan.

Are there direct flights from Guangzhou to Pakistan?

Yes. China Southern Airlines launched a direct Guangzhou to Islamabad route on 25 August 2024, flight CZ8069, operating three times a week on a Boeing 787 when it started. Flight schedules change, so treat these as background, not a promise for your parcel.

For a buyer, a direct passenger route mainly means there is regular belly space on aircraft between the two countries. Your parcel's route is chosen by the courier or forwarder, and it may go through a hub such as an express courier's regional centre. What you can control is the paperwork and the box size.

How does sea freight leave Guangzhou?

Sea freight from Guangzhou leaves from the Port of Guangzhou, which includes the Nansha terminal. Containers from Guangdong can also leave from Shenzhen's ports nearby. For Pakistan, sea freight arrives at Karachi Port or Port Qasim.

Sea freight only suits larger commercial loads. Shared container (LCL) shipments are priced per cubic metre or tonne with minimums, and full containers per container. Our guide on cargo from China to Pakistan explains how air, sea and courier compare.

What should you send a Guangzhou seller before they ship?

Send the seller your exact delivery details and ask for a clear invoice, because the paperwork decides how fast the item clears in Pakistan. A short message covering these points is enough:

  1. Your name exactly as on your CNIC, since couriers match the two during clearance.
  2. Your full address and a phone number that will be answered on delivery day.
  3. A plain description and the real price on the commercial invoice, such as "LED desk lamp, 1 piece".
  4. Whether the item has a battery, and a request that it is declared.
  5. The packed box size and weight, so you can check what the courier will charge.

If the seller ships to a forwarder's warehouse inside China instead, give them the warehouse address and your customer code exactly as the forwarder asks, or the parcel can sit unclaimed.

Do you pay customs on items shipped from Guangzhou?

Yes, if the item is worth more than Rs 1,000. Section 19C of the Customs Act, as amended by the Finance Act 2025, waives duties and taxes on post and courier parcels worth Rs 1,000 or less. Above that, the whole value is assessed on Pakistan's normal tariff for that product, with customs duty, 18% sales tax and income tax as the usual layers. The Chinese city makes no difference.

Products that need permits in Pakistan, such as phones needing PTA registration or medicines needing DRAP approval, need them whichever city they come from. A courier may also add its own handling fees for clearing the parcel.

What is the Canton Fair, and does it matter for one item?

The Canton Fair is China's oldest and largest trade fair, held in Guangzhou every spring and autumn, where over 24,000 companies exhibit. It is a trade event for buyers looking at production quantities, so it does not help someone who wants a single item.

It does matter indirectly. Around the fair, suppliers, hotels and logistics in Guangzhou are busy, and some sellers are slower to answer messages or ship. If you are ordering one item from a Guangzhou supplier during the fair, allow a few extra days for a reply.

What goes wrong when shipping from Guangzhou?

Most problems are the same as anywhere in China, with a few that come up often for Guangzhou sellers:

  • Assuming "free shipping" covers Pakistan. Free shipping on 1688 or Taobao means free within China only.
  • Vague invoices. Descriptions such as "gift" or "parts" slow customs in Pakistan. Ask for a plain item name and the real value.
  • Undeclared batteries. Electronics with lithium batteries need to be declared, or the parcel can be refused or returned.
  • Big retail boxes. Couriers charge by volume, so a large display box can double the shipping cost.
  • Name mismatch. The receiver's name should match the CNIC exactly for courier clearance.
