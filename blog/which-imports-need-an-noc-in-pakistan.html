Which Imports Need an NOC in Pakistan?

By Muhammad Shamshad AslamUpdated November 12, 202610 min read
Quick answer

Goods listed with conditions in Appendix B of Pakistan's Import Policy Order 2022, and goods tied to a regulator, need an NOC, permit or release order before customs releases them. The common ones are medicines and medical devices (DRAP), plants and animals (NAFSA), radio and telecom gear (PTA), Appendix N products (PSQCA) and larger drones (Ministry of Defence).

What does an NOC mean for an import in Pakistan?

An NOC, or no objection certificate, is written clearance from a government body other than customs saying it has no objection to a particular import. Customs will not release goods that need one until it is on record. In practice the paper goes by several names: an NOC, an import permit, a release order or a certificate of compliance. They all do the same job for the importer, which is to prove that the right authority has cleared the goods.

This guide lists the item types where we found an NOC or similar clearance named in an official or reputable source, and the authority behind each one. It does not repeat the banned list. For goods you cannot bring in at all, see our guide on what you cannot import from China to Pakistan. For the full picture of how one import moves, start with how to import from China to Pakistan.

Where do the NOC rules come from?

Most NOC requirements come from the Import Policy Order 2022, issued by the Ministry of Commerce as SRO 545(I)/2022. The order has three appendices that matter here:

  • Appendix A lists goods that are banned.
  • Appendix B lists goods that may be imported only on the conditions written against them, such as an NOC from a named ministry or regulator.
  • Appendix C lists goods restricted in used or second-hand condition.

A separate appendix, Appendix N, lists products that must meet Pakistani quality standards set by PSQCA. On top of that, the Customs Act 1969 gives the federal government power under section 16 to prohibit or restrict imports by notification, and section 17 lets customs detain or seize goods imported in breach of those rules.

The order is amended often. When we checked in November 2026, amendments during 2026 had added chloroform to Appendix B, changed the used vehicle schemes and added a ban on goods made with forced labour. Always check the current version, not an old copy.

How does the NOC reach customs now?

For most regulators the NOC no longer travels on paper. Pakistan Single Window (PSW) links customs with other government agencies, and the importer or clearing agent files one declaration that goes to customs and the relevant regulator together. PSQCA and DRAP are two regulators that now issue their clearances this way.

  • PSQCA. A PSW circular dated 22 December 2023 told importers of goods under Appendix N to file a Single Declaration through PSW so that PSQCA issues an electronic release order. That applied from 3 January 2024 at Karachi's seaports, Jinnah International Airport and the dry ports and airports in Lahore and Peshawar.
  • DRAP. A DRAP letter dated 16 October 2026 told medical device importers to submit import applications through PSW, keeping DRAP's older online system only as a temporary fallback. Importers were also told to keep their establishment licence and device registration records up to date.

Our guide to Pakistan Single Window explains who can file on PSW and what registration it needs.

Does a personal parcel need an NOC too?

Often yes, because the restriction attaches to the goods, not to the size of the order. Section 17 of the Customs Act lets customs detain goods imported in breach of a prohibition or restriction, and it does not carve out personal parcels. Some rules do name personal use separately. PTA's 2021 regulations, for example, allow a person to import up to five mobile devices for personal use in a year.

Where a rule is written only for commercial importers, such as PSQCA's release orders, the official notices we read did not say how a single personal item is treated. In that grey area the courier or agent filing the declaration and the customs officer assessing it decide. If an item is medicine, a drone, a radio or anything that eats, grows or breathes, assume a regulator is involved until you confirm otherwise.

What happens if an item arrives without the NOC?

Customs holds it. Section 17 allows detention for up to fifteen days, which an Additional Collector or Additional Director can extend by another fifteen days, followed by seizure and confiscation through adjudication if the breach stands. Before any confiscation or penalty, section 180 requires a written show cause notice, a chance to reply and a hearing.

If restricted goods are not cleared within sixty days of arrival, section 82 lets the Collector direct the importer, or the shipping line if the importer cannot be traced, to send them back out of Pakistan. For an individual buyer the usual result is a parcel that sits at the airport while storage builds up, then is returned or lost. How do you check whether your item needs one, step by step?

  1. Find the PCT code. Regulators list goods by code, so start with the eight-digit code. Our guide on finding the HS code for your product shows how.
  2. Check the Import Policy Order appendices. Look for the code or description in Appendix B and, for second-hand goods, Appendix C.
  3. Check Appendix N if the item is food, a cosmetic, a building material, a tyre or a household electrical product.
  4. Ask what the item does. Medicine or a medical device points to DRAP. Anything that transmits a radio signal points to PTA. Seeds, plants, pets or animal products point to NAFSA.
  5. Look for recent SROs. The Ministry of Commerce amends the order through SROs, and news outlets such as Business Recorder and Profit report most of them.
  6. Get the NOC before the goods ship. An NOC applied for after arrival means storage charges while you wait.

Which recent changes should you know about?

  • January 2026: chloroform moved to Appendix B with a DRAP NOC condition.
  • January 2026: used cars can no longer come in under the personal baggage scheme; only the gift and transfer of residence schemes remain.
  • April 2026: imports of goods made wholly or partly with forced labour were prohibited, with evidence required for goods from countries the government notifies.
  • August 2026: PTA's Telecommunication Equipment Standards Regulations were notified through SRO 1110(I)/2026, applying international standards to telecom equipment brought in by importers.
  • October 2026: DRAP moved medical device import clearance onto PSW.

Frequently asked questions

What is an NOC for imports in Pakistan?

It is written clearance from a ministry or regulator, other than customs, confirming it has no objection to a specific import. Customs will not release goods that need one until the NOC or release order is on record.

Do I need an NOC to import medicine from China?

Medicines and medical devices fall under DRAP, and imports are cleared against DRAP licensing and registration. Personal parcels of medicine are often held, so confirm the rule for your product before ordering.

Who handles import clearance for seeds and plants now?

The National Agri-Trade and Food Safety Authority, NAFSA, which was formed in July 2025 by merging the Department of Plant Protection and the Animal Quarantine Department.

Does a walkie-talkie need PTA clearance?

Radio equipment falls under PTA type approval, and PTA's 2021 regulations set licensing conditions for VHF, HF and UHF devices. Check the frequency and approval status before you buy.

How long can customs hold goods without an NOC?

Section 17 of the Customs Act allows detention for fifteen days, extendable by another fifteen days, before seizure and confiscation proceedings. Storage charges usually build up during that time.

Where can I see the official list of restricted goods?

Appendix B of the Import Policy Order 2022, issued as SRO 545(I)/2022, as amended by later SROs from the Ministry of Commerce. Use the current amended text, not an old copy.
