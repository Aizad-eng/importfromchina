What is the best way to get a mechanical keyboard in Pakistan?

If the exact board or switch you want is not sold here, the best way is to import that model from the brand's official store or a well-rated seller in China, where most of these keyboards are designed and built. Keychron, Akko and Epomaker are Chinese brands, and Gateron and Kailh are Chinese switch makers. Buying in China means you choose from the full range of layouts, switches and colours, rather than whatever a local shop happened to stock.

Keyboards are also friendly to import. They are light compared with most electronics, they have no plug or voltage issues because they run off USB, and the battery in a wireless board is built in. The things that go wrong are almost all choices made at checkout: the wrong layout, switches that do not fit the board, or a magnetic board bought without understanding it. This guide covers those choices.

Which layout should you pick: 75%, TKL or full size, ANSI or ISO?

Pick by what you use. A full-size board has a number pad. A TKL (tenkeyless) drops the number pad. A 75% board, the most common size in the table above, squeezes the arrow keys and function row in tighter and saves desk space. A 60% or 65% board drops the function row as well, which suits people who rarely use F keys.

The second choice is ANSI or ISO. ANSI has a wide, single-row Enter key and a long left Shift. ISO has a tall, L-shaped Enter key and a short left Shift with an extra key beside it, and is used for UK, German, Nordic and other European layouts. Most Chinese listings default to ANSI with US legends. Keychron, for example, sells the V1 Max in ANSI as standard and runs a separate ISO collection with German, UK, Nordic and Swiss options. If you are buying replacement keycaps later, ANSI sets are far easier to find, so it is the simpler choice unless you are already used to ISO.

What does hot-swap mean, and which switches fit?

Hot-swap means the switches sit in sockets on the circuit board, so you can pull them out and push new ones in without soldering. It is the feature that makes importing switches worthwhile, because you can try a new feel without buying a new keyboard.

The catch is pin count. A mechanical MX-style switch has either 3 pins (just the two metal pins and the centre post) or 5 pins (two extra plastic legs for stability). A board that accepts 5-pin switches, such as the Keychron V1 Max or the Galaxy75 where both are listed, also takes 3-pin ones. A board with 3-pin sockets only takes a 5-pin switch if you clip off the two plastic legs with flush cutters, which is permanent. Akko lists the 5075B with 5-pin sockets. Always read the board's listing for "3-pin" or "5-pin" before you order switches for it.

Low-profile switches are a separate family. Kailh's Choc V2 switches, for example, take Cherry MX low-profile keycaps with a short skirt, and are only partly compatible with the older Choc V1 footprint. They are made for low-profile keyboards, so do not buy them for a standard-height board.

Are magnetic (Hall effect) keyboards different?

Yes, very. A magnetic or HE keyboard senses how far each key is pressed using a magnet in the switch, instead of two metal contacts touching. That allows features like adjustable actuation and rapid trigger, where a key resets the moment you start to lift it. Akko says the MOD007 V5 HE adjusts both in 0.005 mm steps and polls at 8,000 Hz, which is why these boards are popular for fast games.

The important rule for importing is that magnetic switches and mechanical switches are not interchangeable. Gateron's page for its Magnetic Jade Pro switches says plainly that they are designed for magnetic (Hall effect) keyboards, not for mechanical keyboards. It also says they need a keyboard whose magnets face north pole down, so even between magnetic boards you must check compatibility. If you own an HE board, buy switches the board maker lists as compatible. If you own a normal mechanical board, ignore every switch with "magnetic" or "HE" in the name.

What should you know when buying Gateron or Kailh switches on their own?

Read four numbers on the listing: type, force, travel and pack size. Gateron's KS-3X1 Milky Pro series is a good example of how it is written. The pink switch is linear and silent at 45 gf, the purple is tactile and silent at 55 gf, and the yellow is heavy tactile at 60 gf. All three are 5-pin, and Gateron sells them in packs of 35 or 110. Its Magnetic Jade Pro, by contrast, is listed with a 36 gf initial force, 50 gf at the bottom, 3.5 mm total travel and packs of 10, 70 or 90.

Pack size matters because a 75% board has a lot of keys. The Galaxy75, for example, has 81. A 35-pack will not fill it, while a 90 or 110 pack covers it with spares. Switches are very light, so a full set adds little to shipping, but a single small pack is usually too cheap to be worth importing alone. Add it to a keyboard order instead. Counterfeit switches and lookalike listings do exist, so buy from the brand's own store or a seller with a strong rating. Our guide to spotting fake listings lists the warning signs.

Can a wireless keyboard with a built-in battery be shipped by air?

Yes. Under IATA's air cargo rules, lithium batteries contained in equipment are a separate category from loose batteries, and a keyboard with its battery installed falls into it. Couriers handle that category routinely, the same way they ship phones, laptops and earbuds.

The batteries in these boards are not small, Keychron lists 4000 mAh for the V1 Max and Epomaker lists 8000 mAh in two cells for the Galaxy75, but they are fixed inside the case. What causes trouble is a spare battery or a power bank packed separately. Do not add one to the same order. Our guide to lithium battery rules for shipping from China to Pakistan explains the categories in detail.

Do you need Urdu legends on the keycaps?

No. Windows includes an Urdu keyboard layout as standard, listed by Microsoft as "Urdu" with the identifier 00000420. You add it in Windows language settings and switch between English and Urdu with a shortcut, whatever is printed on the keys. Most people who type Urdu regularly learn the layout or use transparent keycap stickers.

That means the standard English keycaps that come with Chinese keyboards work for everyone. Do check the keycap material if it matters to you. Epomaker, for example, lists PBT dye-sub keycaps on the Galaxy75, which hold their legends longer than cheaper ABS. Also check the software: the Keychron V1 Max and Akko 5075B are remapped with VIA, the MOD007 V5 HE with Akko's own driver, and the Galaxy75 with Epomaker Hub or VIA. Mac users should look for a listing that says Mac support, since the modifier keys differ.

Common mistakes when importing a keyboard or switches

  • Buying magnetic switches for a mechanical board, or the reverse. They do not fit each other.
  • Ignoring pin count. 5-pin switches need clipping for a 3-pin-only board.
  • Ordering too few switches. Count your keys first. A 35-pack does not fill a 75% board.
  • Picking ISO by accident. Check the Enter key shape in the listing pictures.
  • Using Bluetooth for gaming. On many boards Bluetooth polls far slower than the dongle or cable.
  • Adding a power bank to the parcel. Loose batteries cause shipping problems that the keyboard itself does not.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best way to get a mechanical keyboard in Pakistan?

If the model you want is not sold here, import it from the brand's official store or a rated seller in China. Keyboards are light and their built-in batteries ship normally by air.

Can wireless keyboards with batteries be shipped from China to Pakistan?

Yes. A battery built into the keyboard counts as a battery contained in equipment, which couriers ship routinely. Avoid adding loose spare batteries or power banks to the same parcel.

Do I need a keyboard with Urdu letters printed on it?

No. Windows has a built-in Urdu keyboard layout you can switch to with a shortcut, so standard English keycaps work. Stickers help while you learn the layout.

Will Gateron magnetic switches fit my mechanical keyboard?

No. Gateron says its magnetic Jade switches are designed for Hall effect keyboards, not mechanical ones. Buy switches that match your board's switch type and pin count.

What is the difference between 3-pin and 5-pin switches?

A 5-pin switch has two extra plastic legs for stability. Boards with 5-pin sockets take both types, while 3-pin-only boards need those two legs clipped off.

Should I buy an ANSI or ISO keyboard?

ANSI, with the wide single-row Enter key, is the default on most Chinese listings and the easiest to find keycaps for. Choose ISO only if you already type on a UK or European layout.
