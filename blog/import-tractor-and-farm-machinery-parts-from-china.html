Importing Tractor and Farm Machinery Parts From China to Pakistan

By Muhammad Shamshad AslamUpdated November 12, 20269 min read
Quick answer

Yes, single tractor and farm machinery parts can be imported from China to Pakistan, but check local sources first. Millat runs an official online parts store for its Massey Ferguson models, so China makes most sense for parts for imported, older or Chinese-made machines, and for small, valuable items like pumps, seals, sensors and controllers. Heavy castings are rarely worth flying.

New to importing? Read our step-by-step guide on how to import from China to Pakistan.

When does it make sense to order a tractor part from China?

It makes sense when the part is not available locally, or when it is small and valuable enough that air shipping is a small share of its price. Most tractors on Pakistani farms are Massey Ferguson models made by Millat Tractors and New Holland models made by Al-Ghazi Tractors, and both have local dealer and parts networks. When we checked in November 2026, Millat's website linked to MTL Parts, an online official spare parts store that lists parts by model with prices in rupees, excluding GST.

So for a common part on a locally made tractor, look locally first. China becomes useful when:

  • The machine itself came from China, such as many small tillers, sprayers, seeders, threshers, harvesters and rotavators.
  • The part is out of stock locally, or the machine is old and the local dealer no longer carries the part.
  • The part is an electronic or hydraulic component, such as a sensor, controller, display, solenoid valve or small pump, where China has many makers.
  • You need an attachment part that local markets do not stock in your size.

How do you identify the exact part before ordering?

Start with the part number, because a picture alone is not enough for farm parts. Many parts look alike and differ by a thread, a spline count or a few millimetres. Official parts stores help here. MTL Parts shows part numbers in its listings, for example a yoke for the MF-240 listed with its number in brackets. Copy the number exactly.

  1. Find the number. Look on the old part, in the operator's or parts book, or in the maker's parts store.
  2. Note the machine. Write the make, model, year and engine model. For Chinese machines, copy the nameplate exactly, including Chinese text if that is all there is.
  3. Measure what matters. For seals, the inner and outer diameter and the width. For shafts, the diameter and spline count. For hoses and fittings, the thread type and size.
  4. Check the connector. For electrical parts, count the pins and note the plug shape and the voltage, usually 12 V on tractors.
  5. Ask the seller to confirm fit in writing. Send them the number and the machine model, and keep their reply.

What is the difference between genuine, OEM-pattern and generic parts?

Genuine parts come from the machine maker's own parts system, OEM-pattern parts are made to the same design by another factory, and generic parts are made to a common size. In Chinese listings these are often mixed, and the words used are not always reliable.

  • Genuine. Supplied through the brand's channel, with its packaging and number. For locally made tractors, the local official store is the natural source.
  • OEM-pattern. Listed as fitting a model or replacing a number. Many work well, but quality varies, so read reviews and ask about material.
  • Generic. Bearings, seals, filters and fittings that follow international sizes. These are often the safest single imports because the number fully defines the part.

A listing that uses a tractor brand's logo on a cheap part may be a counterfeit, and counterfeit goods can be seized at customs. "Fits model X" is fine. A copied logo is not.

How do you check fit for tractors made in Pakistan?

Compare part numbers, not model names. Millat's parts store listed parts for the MF 240, MF 260, MF 350 Plus, MF 360, MF 375, MF 375 4WD, MF 385 and MF 385 4WD, plus Deluxe variants, when we checked in November 2026. Al-Ghazi added the New Holland NH 750 and NH 85-56 to its range, as ProPakistani reported in September 2026.

Tractors with the same model name are built in several countries, and specifications can differ between markets and years. A Chinese listing that says "for MF 240" may have been made for a different version from the one on your farm. Ask the seller which original part number their part replaces, and check that number against your own part or the local parts store. If you cannot match a number, do not order.

Why are heavy farm parts a shipping problem?

Air shipping is charged by weight, so iron and steel parts get expensive quickly. A parcel from China usually costs somewhere around Rs 5,000 to 20,000 to ship depending on size and type, and heavy parts sit at the top of that range or beyond it. Couriers charge whichever is higher, the actual weight or the volumetric weight. For dense parts such as gears and castings, actual weight wins, and every extra kilogram adds cost.

As a rough rule, a single part worth around Rs 10,000 or more that weighs a few kilograms can make sense. A heavy part worth less than its shipping does not. For a heavy item or for many parts at once, a cargo company by sea is the realistic route.

Does the agricultural machinery duty exemption cover parts?

Not automatically. The Budget 2026-27 salient features list an exemption of customs duty, additional customs duty and regulatory duty on imports of agricultural machinery. Whether a particular spare part gets that treatment depends on its tariff code and any conditions attached, and parts are often classified separately from complete machines. Sales tax and income tax are separate from that exemption.

The general rules still apply to a courier parcel. Under section 19C of the Customs Act, parcels worth Rs 1,000 or less pay no duties or taxes. Above that, customs assesses the parcel by tariff code. For FY 2026-27 customs duty slabs are 0, 5, 10, 15 and 20 percent, with some goods higher, and sales tax is 18 percent on the value plus duties. What are the common mistakes with farm machinery parts?

  • Ordering from China when the local official store has the part in stock.
  • Ordering by model name alone, without the original part number.
  • Guessing seal sizes instead of measuring all three dimensions.
  • Forgetting the voltage or plug shape on electrical parts.
  • Flying heavy iron parts whose shipping is more than the saving.
  • Assuming the agricultural machinery exemption covers every spare part.
  • Buying a part with a copied brand logo.
