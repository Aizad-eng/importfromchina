Personal Use vs Commercial Import: Where Pakistan Customs Draws the Line

By Muhammad Shamshad AslamUpdated October 23, 20269 min read
Quick answer

Pakistan Customs treats goods as commercial when they look imported for trading or profit rather than for personal use or as a gift. That is how the FBR defines commercial quantity, and the definition sets no fixed number. One item, or a few different items for yourself, is personal. Many identical units or goods invoiced to a business look commercial and need commercial clearance under an NTN.

Where does Pakistan Customs draw the line between personal and commercial?

At the purpose the goods appear to serve. If the goods look like they are meant for trade or profit, they are treated as commercial. If they look like they are for your own use or a gift, they are personal. There is no single number of units in the definition, so the decision is made on the goods themselves.

Definition. The FBR's travel guide defines commercial quantity as a quantity of goods imported prima facie for trading or pecuniary gain and not for personal use or gift. Prima facie means on first view, so customs judges it from what is in front of the officer: how many, how alike, how packed and how invoiced.

This guide explains the rules behind that line, what the Import Policy Order says about gifts, baggage and samples, and how ordinary single-item orders compare with ones that look like stock.

What does Pakistani law actually say?

There is no single law that lists every case, but several rules fit together. These are the ones that matter for a parcel from China, as we read them in October 2026:

  • Commercial quantity. FBR's definition above. For passengers, its travel guide adds that goods brought in commercial quantities are released only on payment of a fine equal to 30% of their value, in addition to duties and taxes.
  • Samples. The Import Policy Order, 2022 defines samples as goods in limited quantity clearly identified as such and of no commercial value.
  • Banned goods in commercial quantity. The same Order says items on its banned list, Appendix A, imported in commercial quantity shall not be released in any circumstances and are to be re-exported at the importer's or shipping line's cost.
  • Counterfeits. The Order bans the import of counterfeit products, whatever the quantity.
  • Low-value parcels. Section 19C of the Customs Act, as amended by the Finance Act 2025, says no duties and taxes are demanded on goods imported through post or courier worth Rs 1,000 or less, subject to conditions set by the Board.

Rates and lists change through budgets and notifications, so treat this as a map, and confirm the current text for anything you rely on.

What does the Import Policy Order say about personal baggage and gifts?

Less than most people expect, because its personal baggage and gift provisions are about vehicles. Paragraph 16 of the Import Policy Order, 2022 allows the import of vehicles under the personal baggage, transfer of residence and gift schemes, following the procedure in Appendix E. Appendix E sets eligibility rules for those schemes. For example, the 2022 text says cars older than three years are not allowed under them, and that the minimum stay abroad for import as personal baggage is 180 days within the last seven months before the application.

So the word gift in the Import Policy Order does not create a gift allowance for a parcel you order from a Chinese seller. That parcel is an import of goods and is assessed like one. What counts as a sample?

Under the Import Policy Order, a sample is goods in limited quantity, clearly identified as samples and of no commercial value. All three parts matter.

  • Limited quantity. One or a few pieces to look at, not a quantity you could sell.
  • Clearly identified. Marked or documented as samples by the supplier, for example on the invoice.
  • No commercial value. Typically given free or for evaluation, not a retail item you paid the full price for.

A single retail product you bought for yourself is not a sample just because it is one piece. It is a personal import. Calling it a sample does not change how customs values it.

The definition turns on purpose, so these examples show how ordinary orders tend to read against it. They are illustrations of the definition, not legal thresholds, and an officer can take a different view of a particular parcel.

What happens if customs decides your goods are commercial?

They then need commercial clearance, with the paperwork that goes with it. DHL's guide lists what commercial importers provide: a commercial invoice on letterhead with HS codes, a packing list, the airway bill or bill of lading, an NTN, an STRN, the most recent sales tax return, a certificate of origin for trade agreement benefits and a financial instrument from an authorised bank. A parcel held for this kind of query sits at the courier or in a customs shed until the documents are produced or the goods are returned.

For passengers carrying goods in their baggage, the FBR travel guide is specific about the cost: release only on payment of a fine of 30% of the value of the goods, on top of duties and taxes. For banned Appendix A items in commercial quantity, the Import Policy Order says they are not released at all. Does a personal import mean no duty?

No. Personal status decides which clearance route applies, not whether duty is charged. Under section 19C, only post or courier goods worth Rs 1,000 or less escape duties and taxes. Above that, a personal parcel is assessed for customs duty, sales tax and the other charges that apply to its tariff code, using the customs value. Writing gift or personal on the label does not change the assessment. Our guide to customs duty from China to Pakistan explains how the amount is worked out.

What if you buy one tool or part for your work?

One unit for your own use is not a quantity for trading, so it does not fit the definition of commercial quantity. Doctors, engineers, technicians and studios often need exactly one specialist item that is not sold in Pakistan. Our only real example is like this: we sourced one TDH39/DD45-type audiometric headset as a single piece from a verified Alibaba supplier and delivered it in Pakistan.

How the item is invoiced can still matter. If a company wants the import in its own name, with its own tax records, it clears under its NTN like any business import. If you are unsure which route your item needs, ask a licensed clearing agent before you order.

Common mistakes about personal and commercial imports

  • Thinking a gift label removes duty. It does not. Only the Rs 1,000 post and courier limit removes duty and taxes.
  • Calling a paid retail item a sample. The Import Policy Order requires samples to have no commercial value.
  • Ordering several identical units of a phone, which needs PTA clearance beyond a personal quantity.
  • Under-declaring the value. DHL warns that customs compares declared prices with historical data and holds parcels that look undervalued until you show proof of payment.
  • Using a personal route for stock you plan to sell. If the goods are for trade, use the commercial route from the start.

Frequently asked questions

What is a commercial quantity in Pakistan customs?

The FBR defines it as a quantity of goods imported prima facie for trading or pecuniary gain and not for personal use or gift. It sets no fixed number of units.

How many items can I import for personal use?

There is no fixed number in the definition. One item, or a few different items for yourself or family, reads as personal. Many identical units or a range of sizes looks commercial.

Is a gift from China duty-free in Pakistan?

Not as such. The Import Policy Order's gift scheme covers vehicles. A parcel bought from a seller is assessed as an import, and only post or courier goods worth Rs 1,000 or less escape duty.

What counts as a sample under the Import Policy Order?

Goods in limited quantity, clearly identified as samples and of no commercial value. A retail item you paid for is not a sample.

What happens if customs treats my parcel as commercial?

It needs commercial clearance, usually with an NTN, STRN, commercial invoice and packing list. For passenger baggage, FBR says a fine of 30% of value applies on top of duty.

Can I import one item for my work as personal use?

One unit for your own use is not a trading quantity. If a company wants the import in its own name, it clears under its NTN like any business import.
