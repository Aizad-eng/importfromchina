Made-in-China.com for Pakistani Buyers: How It Works

By Muhammad Shamshad AslamUpdated November 12, 20269 min read
Quick answer

Made-in-China.com is a Chinese business-to-business directory where buyers in Pakistan contact manufacturers and trading companies, ask for quotes and place orders. It is run by Focus Technology of Nanjing. Some suppliers are third-party audited, and orders paid through its Secured Trading Service get refund rules, but shipping is arranged with the supplier and most listings expect more than one piece.

New to importing? Read our step-by-step guide on how to import from China to Pakistan.

What is Made-in-China.com?

Made-in-China.com is a business-to-business platform that connects buyers worldwide with suppliers in China. Its help centre says it is operated by Focus Technology Co., Ltd., founded in 1998 and based in Nanjing, and that the platform itself does not make or sell products. Listings belong to supplier members. When we checked its English home page in November 2026, it claimed more than 2.5 million suppliers, more than 110 million products and buyers in more than 220 countries and regions.

In practice it works like Alibaba.com: you search, open a supplier's page, send an inquiry, negotiate and then pay. Its strength is industrial goods, machinery, parts and materials. It is much less useful for everyday consumer shopping. For the overall import process, see how to import from China to Pakistan.

Can a buyer in Pakistan use Made-in-China.com?

Yes. Registering as a buyer is free, and you can browse, message suppliers and post a sourcing request from Pakistan. What you cannot assume is that every supplier will sell a single piece, quote shipping to Pakistan or accept your payment method. Each supplier sets its own minimum order, and shipping is agreed between you and the supplier.

Made-in-China.com's help centre lists credit and debit cards and bank transfer (T/T) among payment methods for its Secured Trading Service, along with local methods for some countries. PayPal is not available in Pakistan, and JazzCash, Easypaisa and Raast are not Chinese payment options, so for most Pakistani buyers this means an international card or a bank transfer.

How does buying on Made-in-China.com work, step by step?

  1. Search or post a request. Search by product, or use Post Sourcing Request, which the home page describes as a way to tell suppliers what you need and receive quotes.
  2. Shortlist suppliers. Look at member level, audit status, years on the platform, main markets and how specific the product pages are.
  3. Send an inquiry. State the model, quantity, delivery city and the shipping method you want quoted.
  4. Agree terms. Price for your quantity, packing, dispatch date and who pays which charges.
  5. Pay through Secured Trading if offered. This is the route with platform refund rules.
  6. Track shipping and handle customs. The supplier or a forwarder ships it, and you pay duty in Pakistan.
  7. Confirm receipt or claim a refund within the time limits below.

Your first message decides the quality of the replies you get. A useful inquiry states the model or a clear description, the quantity, the city in Pakistan, whether you need the item with a Pakistani plug or 220V supply, and that you want the price and shipping quoted separately. Suppliers who reply with a specific answer to each point are usually easier to deal with than those who send a generic catalogue. If a supplier asks you to move the conversation to another app, keep at least the agreed terms and the final price in the platform's messages, so there is a record if you need a refund.

What do the supplier labels on Made-in-China.com mean?

They tell you how much the platform or an outside firm has checked the company, not how good one unit will be. The help centre describes two levels of checking.

How does the Secured Trading Service protect your payment?

It holds your payment and releases it to the supplier only after the order is fulfilled, with refund rules if it is not. These are the main points from Made-in-China.com's help centre and its buyer terms when we read them in November 2026:

  • Who you contract with. For buyers outside Mainland China, the service is provided by Focus Global Sourcing Services Co., Limited in Hong Kong.
  • Fee. The service is free to use, but buyers pay a transaction fee when they complete an online payment. The terms say this fee is not returned if the seller later refunds you.
  • Refund window. Refund requests for items that are defective, not as described or fake must be made within 30 natural days after you confirm receipt. Customised products are not refundable.
  • Late delivery. Goods arriving more than 15 natural days after the agreed time count as late, and goods not delivered within that extra 15 days count as not received.
  • Supplier response. The supplier must respond to a refund request within 72 hours.
  • Refund timing. Approved refunds usually arrive in 5 to 10 working days, depending on the bank, back to the original card or a designated account.
  • Where to file. The help centre says refund requests can only be filed on the PC site, not in the app.

Payments sent directly to a supplier's own bank account are outside these rules. If a supplier pushes you to pay off-platform, choose another supplier.

How is shipping arranged from Made-in-China.com to Pakistan?

You agree it with the supplier. The help centre says the platform does not provide shipping directly and suggests buyers agree shipping details with the supplier or search for a shipping agent. It gives delivery as generally taking 3 to 60 days, which reflects how different the options are, from express courier to sea freight.

For a single parcel, express courier is the normal choice. Ask for the courier name, the chargeable weight and the delivery terms in writing. Under section 19C of the Customs Act, parcels worth Rs 1,000 or less pay no duty or taxes, but above that customs assesses the whole value, and the courier usually collects the amount before delivery.

Will suppliers on Made-in-China.com sell one piece?

Some will, many will not. Machinery and equipment suppliers often sell one unit, because one unit is a normal order for a machine. Suppliers of small parts and consumer goods usually set minimums of dozens or hundreds. Ask directly in your first message: "Can you sell one piece, and what is the price and shipping to Lahore?" or whatever your city is. What mistakes do buyers make on Made-in-China.com?

  • Treating a Gold or Diamond membership as proof of quality.
  • Asking for a quote without stating quantity and city, then receiving a price for hundreds of units.
  • Paying by direct transfer to save the transaction fee, and losing the refund rules.
  • Confirming receipt before checking the goods, which starts the 30-day clock.
  • Forgetting that customised items cannot be refunded.

Frequently asked questions

Is Made-in-China.com legit?

Yes. It is a long-running Chinese B2B platform operated by Focus Technology of Nanjing. Individual suppliers vary, so check their labels and pay through Secured Trading.

Is Made-in-China.com the same as Alibaba?

No. It is a separate company with its own suppliers, payment protection and supplier checks, though some suppliers list on both.

Does Made-in-China.com ship to Pakistan?

The platform does not ship goods itself. Suppliers or shipping agents send orders to Pakistan by courier, air or sea, as agreed with you.

How long do I have to ask for a refund?

Under the Secured Trading Service, within 30 natural days after confirming receipt, for items that are defective, not as described or fake.

Is the Secured Trading fee refunded?

No. The buyer terms say the transaction fee is not returned to you if the seller refunds the order.

Can I buy a single sample from Made-in-China.com?

Sometimes. Ask the supplier directly whether it sells one piece and what shipping to your city costs.
