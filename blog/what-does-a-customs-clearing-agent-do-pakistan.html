What Does a Customs Clearing Agent Do in Pakistan?

By Muhammad Shamshad AslamUpdated November 12, 202610 min read
Quick answer

A customs clearing agent is a person licensed by Pakistan Customs to deal with customs on an importer's behalf. The agent files the goods declaration on WeBOC or PSW, works out the duty, arranges examination and regulator release orders, answers customs queries and gets the goods released. Under section 207 of the Customs Act, nobody may do this for someone else without a licence.

What is a customs clearing agent?

A customs clearing agent, called a customs agent in the law, is someone licensed to transact customs business for another person. Section 207 of the Customs Act 1969 says no person may act on behalf of any principal for customs clearance work, or the import or export of goods or baggage, unless they hold a licence granted under the rules as a customs agent or shipping agent. Acting as an agent without a licence can bring a penalty of up to Rs 10,000 under clause 88 of section 156.

The licensing rules sit in the Customs Rules 2001 and have been tightened several times. For where an agent fits in the full process of one import, see how to import from China to Pakistan.

What exactly does a clearing agent do for an importer?

A clearing agent handles the customs side from the moment cargo arrives to the moment it leaves the port, dry port or airport:

  • Files the goods declaration. Under section 79, the owner must declare imported goods within ten days of arrival. The agent files this on WeBOC or Pakistan Single Window with the commercial documents and uploads everything the system marks as mandatory. Failing to upload those documents can bring a penalty of up to Rs 50,000.
  • Classifies the goods. The agent picks the PCT code, which sets the duty and tells everyone which regulator is involved.
  • Self-assesses and pays duty. The declaration carries the duty and tax calculation, which is paid through the system.
  • Attends examination. If the risk system selects the goods, the agent arranges and attends the examination.
  • Gets regulator clearances. PSQCA release orders, DRAP clearance, plant and animal quarantine and PTA certificates are filed through the same channels.
  • Answers customs. If customs reassesses the goods under section 80, a notice comes through the system with a chance of hearing, and the agent responds. Where customs needs more inquiry, the agent can ask for provisional assessment under section 81 so the goods are released against security.
  • Fixes errors. Section 155R lets an officer correct clerical or typing mistakes in a declaration on request.
  • Arranges release and delivery once duty is paid and the clearance order is issued.
  • Follows up afterwards, such as refund claims under section 33 or representing the importer at a hearing, since section 180 allows a hearing through a counsel or duly authorised agent.

Who is responsible if the agent makes a mistake?

Mostly the importer. Section 209 says that when a principal authorises an agent, anything the agent does is treated as done with the principal's knowledge and consent, so the principal is liable in customs proceedings as if they had done it themselves. The agent can also be treated as the owner of those goods, but duty that was short-levied for reasons other than the agent's wilful act, negligence or default is not recovered from the agent.

Agents carry their own risk too. A December 2024 amendment to the Customs Rules, reported by ProPakistani, introduced a points system: misdeclaration and false statements cost a licensee points out of 50, and reaching zero blocks the licence and starts revocation proceedings. The same amendment allows revocation where a licensee files no goods declaration in a year and does not appear when called.

Do you need a clearing agent to import?

Not by law. Section 208 lets a principal transact business directly, or through an employee with written authority. A business with its own NTN, STRN and WeBOC or PSW access can file its own declarations, and ProPakistani's report on the 2022 rules said a principal can seek approval for self-clearance from an Assistant Collector.

In practice most commercial importers use an agent, because the work needs system access, knowledge of codes and someone physically at the port. For courier parcels the courier company acts as the agent and files under its own registration. For a personal parcel you do not hire anyone.

How does someone become a licensed customs agent?

The licence comes from customs under the Customs Rules 2001, and the requirements have changed through several SROs:

  • 2022: draft SRO 1764(I)/2022 proposed a sound financial record verified by a bank, a qualifying exam at the Pakistan Customs Academy on customs law, procedures and computer skills including WeBOC and PSW, an exam fee of Rs 10,000, a sales tax registration certificate, a non-transferable licence for two years at first, and a smart card in place of the licence booklet, as ProPakistani reported.
  • 2024: SRO 2071 of December 2024 set an annual call for applications each November through newspaper adverts, an exam set by an accredited institution with at least 50 percent needed to pass, and the points system for misdeclaration.

Licence terms and fees have been revised more than once, so check the current FBR notice rather than an older guide.

What should you ask before hiring one?

  1. Their licence. Ask for the licence details and check they are current.
  2. Their fee, separately from duty. Duty goes to the government; the agency fee is what you pay them. Ask for both in writing.
  3. Experience with your code. An agent who clears your kind of goods knows the NOCs and valuation issues.
  4. Storage and delays. Ask who pays port storage and container detention if clearance runs late.
  5. Copies. Ask for the goods declaration and payment receipts. You are liable for what is filed in your name.
  6. Written authority. Section 208 lets customs ask the agent for your written authority, so provide one and keep a copy.
  7. Their approach to reassessment. Ask whether they will request a hearing under section 80, or provisional release under section 81, before agreeing to a higher figure on your behalf.

The points system also works in your favour here. Because the agent's own licence is at stake on every declaration, a good agent has a reason to push back on a value or code that does not fit, and you should treat that as care rather than obstruction.

What are the warning signs of a bad agent?

  • Offering to declare a lower value. Concocted documents and untrue declarations are fiscal fraud under section 32A, and the liability lands on you.
  • Refusing to share the declaration or payment proof.
  • Promising release without paperwork for goods that need an NOC.
  • No licence. Acting as an agent without one is an offence under the Act.

Frequently asked questions

Is a customs clearing agent required by law in Pakistan?

No. Section 208 of the Customs Act lets an importer transact directly or through an authorised employee, but anyone acting for someone else needs a licence under section 207.

Who pays if the clearing agent makes an error?

Usually the importer. Section 209 treats the agent's acts as done with the principal's consent, though duty short-levied without the agent's fault is not recovered from the agent.

How many days does an importer have to file the goods declaration?

Section 79 of the Customs Act says within ten days of the goods arriving, and further delays bring penalties under section 82.

Can a clearing agent lose their licence?

Yes. Since December 2024 a points system deducts points for misdeclaration and false statements, and reaching zero blocks the licence.

Do I need a clearing agent for a courier parcel?

No. The courier acts as the licensed agent and files the declaration under its own registration.

What should a clearing agent give me after clearance?

Ask for the goods declaration, duty payment receipts and the release order, since you are liable for what is filed in your name.
