AliExpress Customs Duty in Pakistan: What You Actually Pay

By Muhammad Shamshad AslamUpdated October 23, 202610 min read
Quick answer

Parcels worth Rs 1,000 or less pay nothing, because section 19C of the Customs Act exempts post and courier imports up to that value, and the Finance Act 2026 left it unchanged. Above Rs 1,000 the whole value is taxed: customs duty set by the product code, then 18% sales tax, then advance income tax. Pakistan Post or the courier collects it before handing the parcel over.

Do I pay customs on AliExpress orders in Pakistan?

Only if the parcel is worth more than Rs 1,000. Below that, no duty or tax is demanded. Above it, the parcel is assessed like any other import, and the bill depends on what the item is.

Most AliExpress orders that people actually care about, such as earbuds, a smartwatch, a tool or a camera accessory, are worth well over Rs 1,000. So for anything beyond a cheap phone case or a pack of cable ties, plan for some tax. In our worked examples below, the total comes to roughly a quarter of the item's value for a low-duty item and about half for an item in the 20% duty slab.

Is the Rs 1,000 duty-free limit still in force after the Finance Act 2026?

Yes. FBR's copy of the Customs Act, 1969 as amended up to 30 June 2026 still says, in section 19C, that where the value of goods imported through post or courier does not exceed one thousand rupees, no duties and taxes shall be demanded. The footnote shows the figure came from the Finance Act 2025. The Finance Act 2026 did not change it.

Two older numbers still float around online, so it helps to know where they came from:

  • Rs 5,000 was the limit before 1 July 2025. Many forum posts and older guides still quote it.
  • Rs 500 was what the 2025 budget bill proposed. The final Act set Rs 1,000 instead, but some pages repeated the proposal.

The limit is a value test, not an allowance. It does not reset per month, and it does not give you Rs 1,000 off a bigger parcel.

What happens if my parcel is worth more than Rs 1,000?

The whole value becomes dutiable, not just the amount over Rs 1,000. A Rs 1,200 parcel is taxed on Rs 1,200.

The value Customs works with is the customs value. Under section 25 of the Customs Act, that value adds the cost of transport and insurance to the price of the goods. So an item listed at Rs 900 with Rs 627 shipping is not a Rs 900 import. Keep that in mind when an item sits just under the limit.

Once a parcel is over the line, Customs assesses it against the Pakistan Customs Tariff using the item's PCT code, the eight-digit Pakistani version of the HS code. The parcel is then held until the tax is paid.

Which taxes apply to an AliExpress parcel, and in what order?

Up to five charges apply, and each one is worked out on a base that includes the ones before it. That stacking is why the total is bigger than the duty rate suggests.

Notice the jump between Rs 1,000 and Rs 1,200. Crossing the limit by Rs 200 can cost several hundred rupees, because the whole parcel becomes taxable.

How do I find the duty rate for my item?

Find the item's PCT code, then look up that line in the Pakistan Customs Tariff for the current year. The code decides the customs duty, ACD and any regulatory duty. Similar-looking items can sit under different codes with different rates, so "electronics" is not a rate.

Our guide on how to find the HS code for your product walks through the search. The full method behind the numbers above is in our customs duty guide.

Who collects the duty: Pakistan Post or the courier?

It depends on how the parcel arrives. Ordinary AliExpress parcels usually come by post. Express ones come by courier.

  • By post: Customs assesses the parcel and Pakistan Post collects the duty and taxes on its behalf. Pakistan Post says it does this through its own manual system, with no online system or call centre for collecting money, and that it never asks for online payment through its website. You usually pay at delivery or at the post office named in the notice.
  • By courier: The courier clears the parcel and pays Customs, then recovers the money from you. DHL's Pakistan help page, for example, says the receiver pays duties and taxes before the shipment can be delivered, in cash to the courier at delivery.

Our comparison of Pakistan Post vs courier explains both routes in more detail.

Does AliExpress charge Pakistani customs at checkout?

Not in a way you can rely on. When we checked listings with the region set to Pakistan in October 2026, product pages carried the line "Tax excluded, add at checkout if applicable". That tells you a tax line may appear at checkout for some orders. It does not promise that Pakistan Customs will accept it as the final figure, and nothing on the listing says the parcel will arrive with nothing to pay.

Treat any amount shown at checkout as an estimate, and keep some money aside for a possible charge on arrival.

Can I avoid customs by splitting orders or declaring a lower value?

No, and trying can make things worse. Asking a seller to write a lower value is under-invoicing, which can lead to a hold, a reassessment on Customs' own valuation, or a penalty.

Splitting also adds shipping charges on every parcel, which raises the customs value of each one. For anything above a few thousand rupees, there is no honest way to keep the parcel under Rs 1,000.

Common mistakes when estimating AliExpress customs

  • Using the old Rs 5,000 limit. It ended on 1 July 2025.
  • Forgetting shipping. Customs value includes the cost of getting the item here, not just the item price.
  • Multiplying by the duty rate alone. Sales tax and income tax are charged on top of the duty, so a 20% duty line costs about 52% in total in our example.
  • Assuming one rate for a whole category. The PCT code sets the rate, line by line.
  • Paying a "customs" link sent by text message. Pakistan Post says it does not collect duty online. Pay only at the post office or to the courier named on your tracking.
  • Ignoring restricted goods. Some items are not just taxed but held. Check what you cannot import from China first.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a duty-free limit for AliExpress parcels in Pakistan?

Yes. Under section 19C of the Customs Act, parcels by post or courier worth Rs 1,000 or less pay no duties or taxes. The Finance Act 2026 did not change this limit.

Is the duty-free limit still Rs 5,000?

No. The Rs 5,000 limit ended on 1 July 2025, when the Finance Act 2025 set it at Rs 1,000.

If my parcel is worth Rs 3,000, do I pay tax on Rs 2,000 or Rs 3,000?

On Rs 3,000. Once a parcel is over the Rs 1,000 limit, the whole customs value is dutiable, not just the part above the limit.

What percentage of the item price will I pay in customs?

It depends on the item's PCT code. In our examples, a 0% duty item came to about 24.5% of the customs value and a 20% duty item to about 52%, for an active taxpayer.

How do I pay customs on an AliExpress parcel in Pakistan?

For postal parcels, Pakistan Post collects the duty at delivery or at the post office, through its own manual system. For courier parcels, the courier asks you to pay before or at delivery.

Can the seller mark my parcel as a gift to avoid duty?

It does not help. Gifts are still assessed on their value, and DHL's Pakistan help page says duties apply whether the item is a purchase, gift or sample.
