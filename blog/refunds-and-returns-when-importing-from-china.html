Refunds and Returns When Importing From China to Pakistan

By importfromchina.shop teamUpdated October 3, 20267 min read
Quick answer

There is no cheap way to return an item from Pakistan to China, so returns in the Daraz sense do not exist. Realistic options are a platform dispute for non-delivery or a clear mismatch, a seller-funded replacement or partial refund, or a transit damage claim. A single-item service contacts the seller and raises the dispute for you, but cannot promise a refund the seller or platform will not give. Getting the right item first time is the best protection.

Can I return an item to China from Pakistan?

Technically yes, practically no. Sending a parcel from Pakistan to China costs a significant amount by courier, the seller often will not accept it, Chinese customs may charge the seller import duty on it, and the refund you receive is the item price without your original shipping or Pakistani duty. For almost any single item, the return postage exceeds what you would recover. Treat every import as final sale and plan around that.

How do platform disputes work?

You open a dispute inside the order, choose a reason, and upload evidence: photos of what arrived, the listing, chat messages. The seller responds with a counter-offer, a refund or a refusal. If you cannot agree, the platform steps in and decides. Non-delivery claims are usually straightforward if tracking shows the parcel never arrived or was returned. Not-as-described claims depend on evidence and often end in a partial refund rather than a full one. Every platform has a time limit, commonly measured from the delivery date or the end of the promised delivery window. Miss it and you have no claim. Details for the most common platform are in how to buy from AliExpress in Pakistan.

When does a seller send a replacement?

Rated sellers care about their store score, and a replacement is often cheaper for them than a dispute. Sellers commonly agree to reship when they sent the wrong variant, when an item arrived dead on arrival with clear evidence, or when a part is missing. They rarely agree when the complaint is about quality expectations, colour under different lighting, or size, which is why reading the cm measurements before ordering matters so much. A replacement travels the same route as the original and takes the same 7 to 15 days by air, and someone has to pay that freight. Sellers usually cover domestic shipping inside China but not international freight.

What about items damaged in transit?

Report damage immediately, on the day of delivery, with photos of the outer box, the inner packing and the item. Who is responsible depends on where the damage happened. If the seller packed badly, the seller or platform may refund. If the carrier damaged it, the carrier's liability is usually limited unless insurance was bought. For fragile items, ask the seller before paying to pack with extra protection; most will if asked.

What can I do before ordering to avoid all this?

  • Buy from rated sellers with a long history and high scores. See how to find reliable Chinese suppliers.
  • Read the listing's cm measurements and the variant table. Chinese sizes run smaller than UK or US sizes.
  • Ask the seller for real photos of the actual item or batch before it ships. Many will send them on request.
  • Confirm voltage and plug type for appliances. Pakistan uses 220 to 240 volts.
  • Avoid branded goods at unbranded prices. A seized replica cannot be refunded by anyone.
  • Keep every chat message and screenshot until the item has been in your hands for a few weeks.

What does a single-item service do on your behalf?

A service buys the item on its own account, so the dispute rights sit with the service, and it exercises them for you. If the item arrives wrong, missing a part or dead on arrival, you tell the service, send photos, and the service contacts the seller, negotiates a replacement or refund, and opens a platform dispute if the seller refuses. It relays the outcome to you in rupees. What a service cannot do is force a seller or platform to refund, or make a return to China economical. We are clear about that. The item travels from the seller as the seller packed it, which is why we point customers to rated sellers and ask them to confirm the exact variant before paying.

Realistic expectations

Non-delivery: usually refunded. Wrong variant or missing part: often fixed by the seller. Dead on arrival with evidence: often refunded or replaced. Quality disappointment, size, colour, or change of mind: usually no remedy. If a return option matters more than price or selection, Daraz is the better fit for that purchase. Frequently asked questions

Can I return an AliExpress item from Pakistan?

You can, at your own cost by courier, but the postage usually exceeds the refund. Most buyers settle for a partial refund through a dispute instead.

What happens if my item from China never arrives?

Open a dispute on the platform before the protection window closes. Non-delivery claims with tracking evidence are usually refunded.

Will a Chinese seller send a replacement?

Often, for a wrong variant, missing part or dead-on-arrival item with photo evidence. Rarely for quality expectations, size or colour.

Does a sourcing service guarantee refunds?

No. It contacts the seller and raises disputes on your behalf, but it cannot force a refund the seller or platform will not give.

Who pays for a replacement to be shipped again?

Sellers usually cover shipping inside China. International freight for the replacement is normally the buyer's cost unless the seller agrees otherwise.
