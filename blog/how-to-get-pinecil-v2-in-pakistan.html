What is the Pinecil V2 and who is it for?

The Pinecil is a pocket soldering iron made by Pine64. Version 2 is the current model. It is about the size of a thick marker, weighs 28g with the tip fitted, and is powered either from a USB-C charger or a DC barrel jack. There is no base station and no mains cable on the iron itself. You plug it into a charger, set the temperature with two buttons, and it heats up.

It suits people who solder now and then and want something better than a basic 220V plug-in iron: phone and laptop repair hobbyists, people building keyboards or Arduino projects, drone and RC builders, and students who need something that fits in a bag. It is less suited to heavy soldering on thick wires or large ground planes, where a bigger station with more thermal mass still wins.

The firmware is Ralim's IronOS, an open-source project. That matters because the iron gets updates, the menus can be changed, and there is a large community writing guides for it.

Which charger works with the Pinecil V2?

Pine64's own documentation recommends a USB-C PD charger rated 65W that can supply 20V at 3A or more. In practice that means a decent laptop-style USB-C charger, not a phone charger. Look at the small print on the charger. You want to see a line like "20V⎓3.25A" or "20V⎓3A" among the outputs.

  • 65W or higher USB-C PD laptop charger: the right choice. At 20V the standard 8 ohm tips draw about 50W and the included 6.2 ohm short tip about 64W, according to the Pine64 wiki's power chart.
  • 12V Quick Charge phone chargers: Pine64 warns that most supply only around 1.5A at 12V, which can cause undervoltage or thermal runaway warnings. QC2 is not supported in IronOS at all.
  • 5V USB ports and old chargers: the iron shows "DC Low". That is enough for menus and firmware updates, not for heating.
  • Power banks: work if the power bank offers USB-C PD at 20V. Many cheaper ones only go to 12V or less, so check the output label.

Pakistan's 220 to 240V mains is not a problem, because the iron never touches mains power. Your charger does, and nearly all laptop-style USB-C chargers accept 100 to 240V. Check the label on the charger itself.

What about the barrel jack and 24V supplies?

The barrel jack gives the most power, up to the 88W rating at 24V. It takes a DC5525 plug (5.5mm outside, 2.5mm inside), centre positive, 12V to 24V, at least 3A. Pine64's wiki gives three warnings worth repeating:

  • A DC5521 plug (2.1mm inner pin) looks almost the same but can break the jack. Do not force it.
  • Cheap "universal" adapters have damaged the iron's power components. Use a fixed 24V supply from a known brand.
  • Never connect USB-C and the barrel jack at the same time.

The wiki also mentions tentative support for 28V USB PD 3.1 EPR chargers with 240W-rated cables. Pine64 has not made this an official rating, so treat it as an experiment, not a buying reason.

The cable matters too. Use a proper USB-C to USB-C cable that is rated for the current you want. A thin charge-only cable that came with a phone can limit the power. The Pine64 wiki specifically says magnetic-tip USB-C cables are not recommended because they are not USB compliant. If you already own a 65W laptop charger with a fixed cable, that cable will usually do the job.

How do you set it up the first time?

  1. Fit the tip and tighten the small screw on top. Do not overtighten it.
  2. Connect the iron to a 65W USB-C PD charger with a proper USB-C cable.
  3. If it heats slowly or shows an undervoltage or "DC Low" message, the charger or cable is the problem. Change it before blaming the iron.
  4. Use the two buttons to set your working temperature, anywhere from 100°C to 400°C. Follow the temperature printed on your solder's packaging.
  5. Check the IronOS version in the menu. Pine64's wiki notes that the Bluetooth apps need IronOS 2.21 or newer. Updating uses Pine64's Blisp tool on Windows, Mac or Linux.
  6. Look through the sleep and idle settings. IronOS has idle detection, a sleep mode and automatic shut-down, which save the tip and reduce risk if you forget it.

Common mistakes with the Pinecil V2

  • Using a phone charger and deciding the iron is weak.
  • Forcing a DC5521 barrel plug into the DC5525 jack.
  • Plugging in USB-C and the barrel jack together.
  • Ordering without a stand and resting a 350°C iron on the desk.
  • Ordering from a listing that does not say whether it is V1 or V2.

Frequently asked questions

Does the Pinecil V2 come with a charger?

No. Pine64 ships only the iron and one ST-B2 short tip. You need a USB-C PD charger or a 12V to 24V DC5525 supply.

Will my phone charger power the Pinecil V2?

It may power on but it will heat slowly or show warnings. Pine64 recommends a 65W USB-C PD charger that gives 20V at 3A.

Can I use a USB-C laptop charger from my laptop?

Usually yes, if it is USB-C PD and lists a 20V output of 3A or more. Check the label on the charger.

Does the Pinecil V2 work on Pakistan's 220V supply?

The iron itself runs on low-voltage DC. Your charger connects to the mains, and most USB-C laptop chargers accept 100 to 240V.

Are TS100 tips compatible with the Pinecil?

Pine64's wiki says the Pinecil is compatible with other TS100 tips. Short tips suit the V2 best at 20V.
