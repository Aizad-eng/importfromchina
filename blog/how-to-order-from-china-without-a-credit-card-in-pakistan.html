How to Order From China Without a Credit Card in Pakistan

By Muhammad Shamshad AslamUpdated October 17, 20269 min read
Quick answer

You do not need a credit card. Temu's Pakistan site accepts JazzCash and Easypaisa directly. For AliExpress and Alibaba, a Visa or Mastercard debit card with international online payments switched on works for many buyers, and so can a virtual debit card from an app such as NayaPay or SadaPay. PayPak cards and PayPal will not work. For Taobao, 1688 or anything else, you can pay a Pakistan-based service in rupees by bank transfer, Raast, JazzCash or Easypaisa.

Can you order from China without a credit card?

Yes. Most people in Pakistan who order from China do not use a credit card at all. You have four realistic options:

  • A mobile wallet, on sites that accept it. Temu's Pakistan site is the main one.
  • A debit card on the Visa or Mastercard network, with international online use switched on.
  • A virtual debit card from a fintech app, made for online shopping.
  • Paying someone in Pakistan in rupees, who then buys the item from the Chinese seller.

Which one works depends on the site. This guide covers each option and where it works. For the wider topic of paying suppliers, including bank wires to factories, see our guide on how to pay Chinese sellers from Pakistan.

How do you use a debit card on Chinese sites?

A normal bank debit card works on many Chinese sites, as long as it is on the Visa or Mastercard network and set up for international online payments. Follow these steps before your first order:

  1. Check the logo on the card. It must say Visa or Mastercard. A card with only the PayPak logo is domestic. NayaPay's help page, for example, says its PayPak card can be used only for in-store purchases and ATM withdrawals in Pakistan.
  2. Switch on international and online use. Most banks have a setting in the app, under card settings or card controls, sometimes with a daily limit. Some banks need a call to the helpline.
  3. Keep your phone nearby. Many payments ask for a one-time code from your bank, usually called 3D Secure.
  4. Put enough in the account. Your bank converts the price to rupees at its own rate and may add charges, so leave some margin.
  5. Pay from the site's own checkout. Never type card details into a link sent by a seller.

If a payment is declined, check the international setting first. Many cards are issued with it off, and repeated declined attempts can trigger a temporary block.

Do virtual cards from NayaPay or SadaPay work on Chinese sites?

They are designed for online shopping, and both apps say their cards work on international sites:

  • NayaPay issues a Visa virtual card when you open an account, and its website says you can use it on local and international sites.
  • SadaPay issues a Mastercard debit card with a free virtual card for online shopping, and advertises global acceptance.

In practice, check two things before you use one. First, that international online payments are switched on in the app. Second, the charges for foreign transactions, which neither home page listed when we checked. Both apps publish fee schedules. A virtual card is useful because you can load only what you need for one order, which limits the damage if the card details leak.

Can you pay Chinese sites with JazzCash or Easypaisa?

Only on sites that accept them directly. Temu's Pakistan site does. AliExpress does not support these wallets at checkout, according to a 2026 buying guide for Pakistan. Taobao, 1688 and Alibaba do not either.

Both JazzCash and Easypaisa have card products linked to the wallet. We could not confirm that either works for international online payments, so check with the provider before you count on it.

Raast is also domestic. It is the State Bank of Pakistan's instant payment system for moving money between accounts in Pakistan. It is very useful for paying someone here, but it cannot pay a Chinese seller.

Can you pay cash on delivery for an order from China?

Usually not. Cash on delivery is common inside Pakistan, but cross-border sites rarely offer it, because the seller in China would be sending the goods before any money moves. When we checked Temu's Pakistan site in October 2026, cash on delivery was not among the payment options shown. A 2026 buying guide for Pakistan says cash on delivery is rarely offered on AliExpress cross-border orders.

So if cash on delivery matters to you, your realistic choices are a local seller, or a service that you can trust with payment upfront because it states the full price and has a clear refund policy. Be wary of anyone offering cash on delivery for a China order and then asking for a deposit through a personal account. That is a common pattern in scams, not a normal way of working.

A related point: with a parcel you order yourself, any duty that customs assesses may still be collected before or at delivery. That is not cash on delivery for the item. It is tax, and it can come on top of what you paid at checkout.

Is there a limit on spending abroad with a Pakistani card?

Yes. The State Bank of Pakistan sets an annual limit of USD 30,000 per person for international transactions on cards. NayaPay's help page explains that it applies per person, not per card, so it covers all your cards from all banks together, and that only international transactions are restricted if you reach it. Cards are also meant for personal use, not business payments.

For one item this limit will not matter. It matters if you shop abroad a lot, or if someone asks to use your card for their orders.

How do you pay for Taobao and 1688 without a card?

Taobao and 1688 are built for buyers in China. They mostly take Chinese payment methods such as Alipay and ship within China. Even if you manage to pay, the item still needs a warehouse in China and a way to get to Pakistan.

So the realistic route is to pay a service in Pakistan in rupees. The service pays the Chinese seller, receives the item in China and ships it to you. Our guides to a Taobao buying service and buying from 1688 in Pakistan explain this.

Is it safe to pay a service in Pakistan before the item ships?

It is normal practice, because the service has to pay the Chinese seller first. What makes it safe is knowing what happens if it goes wrong. Before you pay anyone, check:

  • A written refund policy. Ours gives a full refund if the item is not delivered within 10 days after the agreed date, or if it arrives wrong, damaged or not working. The details are in our refund policy.
  • One total price. A price that leaves out shipping or customs is not the real price.
  • Payment to a named account. Avoid paying a personal account that does not match the business you are dealing with.

What mistakes do people make when paying Chinese sites from Pakistan?

  • Using a PayPak-only card. It will not work on foreign sites.
  • Leaving international payments off. The most common reason for a declined payment.
  • Trying PayPal. PayPal is not available to people living in Pakistan, and accounts opened with foreign details can be locked.
  • Using a relative's foreign card. A card from another country with a Pakistani address can trigger fraud checks and cancel the order.
  • Paying a seller outside the platform. A discount for a direct transfer removes your buyer protection.
  • Paying through links in messages. Fake customs and delivery fee messages are common. Pay only on the site or app itself.
