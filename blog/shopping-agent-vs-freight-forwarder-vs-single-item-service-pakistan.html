Shopping Agent vs Freight Forwarder vs Single-Item Service: Which Do You Need in Pakistan?

By Muhammad Shamshad AslamUpdated October 17, 20269 min read
Quick answer

It depends on who buys the item. A freight forwarder never buys anything. It ships goods you already paid for from a China address and gets them cleared in Pakistan. A shopping agent buys on Taobao, 1688 or Alibaba for you, then usually bills international shipping separately once the parcel is weighed. A single-item service buys, ships and clears customs for one rupee price agreed before you pay. For one item worth around Rs 10,000 or more, the single-item route is usually simplest. For bulk, use a forwarder.

What is the one question that separates these three services?

Ask: who pays the Chinese seller? Everything else follows from the answer.

  • Freight forwarder: you pay the seller yourself. The forwarder only moves the goods.
  • Shopping agent: the agent pays the seller with its Chinese account, and you pay the agent. Shipping to Pakistan is usually a second bill.
  • Single-item service: the service pays the seller, ships the item and clears customs, and you pay the service one price, once.

People in Pakistan often use the words agent, cargo, forwarder and sourcing company for the same thing, so do not go by the name a business gives itself. Go by what it does with your money and who is responsible for each step.

What does a freight forwarder actually do?

A freight forwarder coordinates and organises the movement of shipments on behalf of a shipper. It books space with carriers such as airlines and shipping lines, but it does not normally buy the goods. Typical work includes consolidating several small consignments into one shipment and preparing customs paperwork such as commercial invoices and bills of lading.

For a Pakistani buyer, a forwarder that handles China usually gives you an address at its warehouse in China. You order from the seller, enter that address, and pay the seller yourself. When the parcel reaches the warehouse, the forwarder weighs it, groups it with other customers' goods, flies or ships it to Pakistan, and arranges clearance.

This works well when you can already pay the seller, for example on Alibaba.com with a card that has international use enabled, or when you have a regular supplier and a large shipment. It does not solve the payment problem on Taobao or 1688, which take Chinese payment methods. Nor does it give you any help if the seller ships the wrong thing. That dispute is between you and the seller.

What does a shopping agent do?

A shopping agent, sometimes called a buying agent, purchases on your behalf on sites you cannot use directly. The usual flow looks like this:

  1. You send links with the colour, size and quantity you want.
  2. The agent quotes the item price, the seller's shipping inside China and its commission.
  3. You pay that first amount. The agent buys, and the seller ships to the agent's warehouse in China.
  4. The agent weighs the parcel, or several parcels together, and quotes international shipping.
  5. You pay the second amount, and the goods are sent to Pakistan.

The main thing to understand is that the full cost is not known when you first pay. Weight, box size and the shipping line chosen decide the second bill. Customs in Pakistan may be included in that second bill, or may be collected separately, depending on the agent. Ask before you start.

Shopping agents suit buyers who want several items from different sellers in one parcel, or who buy often enough to accept a bit of uncertainty on each order. For what a fuller sourcing agent does on bigger orders, see our guide to China sourcing agents in Pakistan.

What is a single-item service?

A single-item service handles one product from start to finish for a price agreed before anything is bought. You send a link. The service checks the listing, works out shipping for that item and the customs that will be charged on it in Pakistan, adds its fee, and gives you one total in rupees. You pay once. It buys, ships and clears the item and delivers it to your address.

The trade-off is scope. A single-item service is built for one item per order. It is not for consolidating twenty purchases from five sellers, and it is not for resale stock. In return, you know the final number before you pay and you deal with one business for everything.

The cheap-item row matters. Shipping one item from China usually costs Rs 5,000 to 20,000 on its own, so a single-item service makes sense for items worth around Rs 10,000 or more. Below that, shipping can cost more than the product.

How do the costs add up differently?

All three routes carry the same underlying costs: the item, shipping inside China, international freight, Pakistan customs and someone's fee. What differs is how they are shown to you and when.

  • Forwarder: you see the item price at checkout, then a freight bill per kilogram later. Air freight is charged on the greater of actual and volumetric weight, so a light but bulky box costs more than its weight suggests.
  • Shopping agent: you see item plus commission first, freight second. The first number looks small and the second can surprise you.
  • Single-item service: you see one number. It cannot be compared line by line, but it can be compared directly with the final total of the other two.

On customs, parcels arriving through post or courier worth Rs 1,000 or less are free of duties and taxes under section 19C of the Customs Act, as set by the Finance Act 2025. Almost anything worth importing on its own is above that, so expect customs to be charged. Rates depend on the product, and our customs duty guide explains how they are worked out. Who carries the risk when something goes wrong?

This is where the three really differ, and most people only ask about it after a problem.

  • Seller never ships. With a forwarder, you chase the seller or the platform. With an agent, many cancel and refund the first payment. With a single-item service, it should be covered by a written refund rule.
  • Wrong or broken item. A forwarder ships what arrived. An agent's help depends on its terms and how quickly the problem is spotted. A single-item service should say in writing what happens.
  • Customs charges more than expected. With a forwarder or agent, you usually pay the difference. With a one-price service, ask who pays. Ours does.
  • Restricted goods. Forwarders and agents commonly refuse or return items such as loose batteries and liquids. Check our list of what you cannot import from China before choosing any route.

How do you decide in five steps?

  1. Count the items. One item points to a single-item service. Many items from many sellers point to a shopping agent. Large quantities point to a forwarder or sourcing agent.
  2. Check whether you can pay the seller yourself. If not, a forwarder alone will not work.
  3. Check the item's value. Under about Rs 10,000, shipping one item rarely makes sense.
  4. Ask for the full delivered cost. Compare final totals, not first payments.
  5. Read the refund terms. Non-delivery, wrong items and customs surprises should each have a stated answer. Our checklist for choosing a China buying service goes through this in detail.

What are the common mistakes?

  • Comparing a forwarder's per kilogram rate with a single-item service's total. One excludes the item, the China leg and possibly customs. The other includes them.
  • Using a forwarder for a Taobao item. The forwarder can ship it, but someone still has to pay a Chinese seller.
  • Assuming the agent's first quote is the final cost. It usually is not.
  • Sending one cheap item alone. Shipping can be several times the price.
  • Not asking who handles customs. A parcel held at the airport with nobody responsible for it is the worst outcome.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a shopping agent and a freight forwarder?

A shopping agent buys the item from the Chinese seller for you and then ships it. A freight forwarder never buys anything. It only ships goods you have already paid for from its China warehouse to Pakistan.

Do I need a freight forwarder to buy one item from China?

Usually not. A forwarder helps when you can pay the seller yourself and have a lot to ship. For one item, a single-item service that buys, ships and clears it for one price is normally simpler.

Why do shopping agents charge me twice?

The first payment covers the item, shipping inside China and the agent's commission. The second covers international shipping, which can only be priced once the parcel is weighed at the agent's warehouse.

Can a freight forwarder buy from Taobao or 1688 for me?

A pure forwarder does not buy. Taobao and 1688 take Chinese payment methods, so you need a shopping agent or a single-item service that can pay the seller in China.

Which is cheapest for one item from China to Pakistan?

Compare the final delivered total, not the first quote. Shipping one item usually costs Rs 5,000 to 20,000, so for items under about Rs 10,000 none of these routes is usually worth it.

Who pays customs duty when I use a forwarder or agent in Pakistan?

It depends on the business. Some include customs and some collect it separately after clearance. A single-item service that quotes one price includes Pakistan customs in that price.
