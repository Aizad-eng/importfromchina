Volumetric weight calculator

By Muhammad Shamshad AslamRates checked October 23, 2026
Quick answer

Volumetric weight is length × width × height in cm divided by 5,000 for express couriers such as DHL, FedEx and UPS, or 6,000 for air cargo. You are charged for whichever is higher, the volumetric weight or the actual weight. Enter your box size and weight below.

Chargeable weight is whichever is higher: the actual weight or the volumetric weight. Measure the outer box, not the product.

What is the volumetric weight formula?

Volumetric weight in kg = length × width × height in centimetres ÷ a divisor. DHL Express, FedEx and UPS use 5,000 for international shipments. Air cargo and freight forwarders commonly use 6,000, the IATA standard. The carrier charges whichever is higher, the actual weight or the volumetric weight.

The last row is why a light but bulky item, like a chair or a large lamp, can cost more to ship than it is worth.

How can you keep the chargeable weight down?

  • Ask the seller to ship in the smallest box that protects the item, without the retail box if the product allows it.
  • Choose items that pack small. Parts, tools, gadgets and accessories usually ship on actual weight.
  • For one item, a courier price is per parcel, so combining several small items in one box can be cheaper than sending them separately.

Frequently asked questions

What is the divisor for DHL volumetric weight?

DHL Express divides length × width × height in centimetres by 5,000. FedEx and UPS use 5,000 for international shipments too.

Why is air cargo divided by 6,000?

6,000 cubic centimetres per kilogram is the IATA standard used by airlines and freight forwarders. It gives a lower volumetric weight than the 5,000 couriers use.

Do I pay for actual weight or volumetric weight?

You pay for the chargeable weight, which is the higher of the two.

Should I measure the product or the box?

The box. Carriers measure the outer packaging, including any padding.
